
Kathputli, often simply known as Kathi puppets or by their other Sanskrit name 'Savari,' represents one of India's most intriguing traditional puppetry forms, originating primarily from the vibrant state of Rajasthan in the western region.

This unique art involves thin bamboo rods inserted through holes strategically positioned near the puppet's joints. Performers dexterously move these rods hidden behind translucent fabrics like Veena, Chanderi, or Tant silk scrolls to create fluid motion for intricate characters carved usually from wood, sometimes incorporating clay or paper.

The name itself reflects their essence: *Kath* signifies the string or rod used to operate them (*Kathi*), while 'Putli' is a general term for puppets. Each Kathputli performer carries significant responsibility; they act as shadow puppet narrators during daylight using colourful scrolls, manipulating their puppets silently synchronized with rhythmic narrative chanting. This requires exceptional skill and dexterity.

Bearing direct lineage to ancient Sanskrit forms involving sophisticated figures like *Raga Putli*, the traditional Kathputli often incorporates folk tales popular in Rajasthan – legends of heroes defending forts against villains or animal stories inspired by Rudyard Kipling's works. The performer provides voice, narration direction (often with a musical box), and controls all characters simultaneously from behind heavy fabrics.

More than mere puppets, they are storytellers breathing life into narratives through their subtle physical language created by the performers' rod manipulation skills. A vital aspect of Rajasthani culture during festivals or on demand to private gatherings, Kathputli remains an active living tradition in contemporary India.
