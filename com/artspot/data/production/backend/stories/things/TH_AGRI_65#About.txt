
Introduced within Indian culinary culture, Mizo Chilli stands out as a potent local variant. Native to the state of Mizoram in northeast India, it earned its moniker from its distinct characteristics prevalent there.

Mizo Chilli is celebrated for its exceptional heat level; not all peppers are fiery enough to be considered truly *kochu* or 'hot'. Its flavour profile often includes earthiness and sharpness, contributing significantly to dishes' intensity. Historically, much of the dried Mizo Chilli production occurred in Kochi (Cochin), but its core identity lies with Mizoram's unique agricultural conditions.

Preserved primarily as a powder through solar drying – yielding red chilli flakes or smaller powder particles that retain their heat remarkably well when wetted – it remains integral to local cuisine. Its strong character means only small quantities are typically used per recipe, making dishes spicier using Mizo Chilli than others of similar weight.

This pepper offers flavour intensity alongside nutritional value but should be consumed with awareness due to its high capsaicin content and significant heat. It serves as a quintessential ingredient representing the unique tastes developed within Mizoram's distinct geographical and cultural context.
