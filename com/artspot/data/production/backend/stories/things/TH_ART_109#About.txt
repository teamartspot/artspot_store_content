
Located in Huzurpet (Old name: Villianur), this distinguished structure is believed to be one of the oldest surviving and intact terracotta tile works establishments in India, possibly dating back before Independence.

Visually distinct, it features a long corridor leading towards an open shed area. On either side of this main processing wing stand the living quarters – comfortable rooms designed for workers' habitation with sufficient privacy in separate small compartments accessed via doorways and staircases.

Beyond production, its defining feature is thought to be the specific curved structure over the central workshed dedicated to drying terracotta bricks/roof tiles. This innovative architectural element ensures proper ventilation during sun-drying without being overtaken by the main functional space itself, showcasing foresight in design.

The factory operated as an independent industrial unit but maintained ties with the prominent Palladium architects of India. The buildings exemplify early 20th-century commercial-industrial architecture prevalent across Southern India, blending utilitarian needs with comfortable accommodation above a ground floor (possibly workshop level) structure below open corridors leading towards drying yards and kiln sites.

Its preservation forms an important chapter in documenting pre-independence industrial heritage and the architectural solutions developed for functional building types. The complex provides tangible evidence of combined living and working spaces from that era, offering insights into daily life and manufacturing history before national independence profoundly altered these patterns across South India.
