
Nachiarkoil Kuthuvilakku stands as a monumental temple complex dedicated to Lord Annamalaiyar, revered in South Indian Hindu tradition. Its history traces back over two millennia, although its precise founding time remains legendary rather than strictly historical.

The origin story involves the mythical Agnishwami legend linked to Mount Arunachala (Thiruvannamalai). It is believed that Goddess Kali Subramaniya Devi appeared in a sacred grove near Dharasuram on Udagamandalam hill, marking the site for building temples. Her manifestations include the inner sanctum of Annamalaiyar and a nearby small Shiva temple (Kalaikandu), but Nachiarkoil Kuthuvilakku itself might be closely associated with this divine intervention.

Historical records place significant development upon Palasurai Nadu kings, particularly in their later stages. Construction likely continued significantly during the Chola dynasty's reign over Varadhar-konda (a peak on Arunachala). Archaeological evidence supports its antiquity, suggesting foundational elements date to pre-Chola periods or early medieval times.

The complex serves as an architectural marvel of early Dravidian style in South India. While exact origins are clouded in ancient folklore, its enduring presence reflects profound devotion and the deep integration of these deities' worship into Tamil cultural and spiritual history since at least the 7th century CE onwards.
