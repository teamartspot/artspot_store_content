
Cashew nuts, originating from West Africa, gained prominence in India through Portuguese traders who introduced them centuries ago. Like its fellow nut, Brazil Nut, it found favor in the coastal regions of Goa and subsequently spread to nearby districts.

In Maharashtra, specifically within Ratnagiri district where Vengurla is located (near Nashik), cashew cultivation took root significantly later than in Kerala or Goa itself. While Kerala remains the birthplace of modern commercial nut processing in India, it was the industrial powerhouse of Nashik and its surrounding areas, including Ratnagiri's coastline, that played a crucial role.

Vengurla Cashew emerged during this period due to the town's strategic position as one of Maharashtra's key cashew-growing centers alongside Kopargaon. Farmers in these regions capitalized on the opportunity to cultivate cashews under suitable conditions – often near coastal areas where specific processing infrastructure existed or was developing directly within Vengurla.

Unlike Brazil, which required complex initial extraction techniques even further south (initially in Kerala), nut cultivation simplified manufacturing. Nevertheless, from its origin as a simple agricultural crop, 'Vengurla Cashew' developed a reputation due to the synergistic relationship between farmers and processors, particularly those near Nashik handling distribution and export through ports like Vengurla's own.

Today, Vengurla Cashew signifies quality produced by Maharashtra’s agro-based economy, leveraging its favorable geography for both farming and early processing capabilities.
