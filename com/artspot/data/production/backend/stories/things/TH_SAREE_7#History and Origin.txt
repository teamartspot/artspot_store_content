
Bandhani sarees represent a fascinating example of intricate handiwork originating from Gujarat, India. The name itself often translates as "tie-dye," reflecting its core technique. While traceable forms exist in history, the craft associated specifically with saris reached prominence likely through regional specialization and perhaps external influences encountered by Gujarati traders over centuries.

Legend often credits local artisans near Dwarka or Dandi for developing the intricate patterns, though historical evidence points to Gujarat being one of India's earliest regions mastering this form of resist-dyeing. It became synonymous with royal courts in the area around Surat-Dewas during the Mughal and Maratha eras.

The tradition revolves around folding silk fabric tightly (the characteristic pleating) and tying numerous knots evenly, then dyeing it submerged in colored water or vat dyes. Upon burning off the bindings, vibrant concentric circles radiate from each central knot where the cotton core remained white ('ghoomar' patterns).

More than just a pattern, Bandhani is deeply cultural. It symbolizes prosperity (deep red/yellow often wished upon brides), protection (blue against the evil eye), and festivity. Worn on special occasions like Navratri or Diwali, intricate designs are common; simpler 'Kerchief' styles also exist for daily wear.

From its roots in elaborate wedding attire near Surat, Bandhani sarees spread across Gujarat's textile hubs via trade routes, evolving into diverse regional variations while retaining the signature pattern tied to the land and legacy.
