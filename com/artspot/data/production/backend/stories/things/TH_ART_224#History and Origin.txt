
Assam Bihu Dhol is a two-headed musical instrument unique to Assamese culture, especially integral to the traditional Bihu dance performed during Holi, the state festival celebrated across Upper Assam.

Its history traces back to ancient times. Evidence suggests dhol-like percussion existed even before Buddhism arrived in the region via Bodhidharma around 400-500 CE. These early forms likely featured bamboo flutes similar to today's traditional barrel components, played by communities integral to forests and hills, long pre-dating the medieval Ahom kingdom. The Ahoms, who ruled from the late 11th century onwards (c.AD 1234), further contributed to its evolution.

During British colonial rule, starting in 1830, a more refined cylindrical barrel design emerged with brass barrels joined by bamboo pegs and wooden side plates. However, as Assam gained independence after India's partition in 1947-48, the traditional use of bamboo-woven cylinders became prevalent again.

Today, artisans craft these bihu dhols using metal frames covered either inside with fine cylindrical barrels made from hollowed neem or sal wood filled with deer skin stretched over a small inner cylinder. Its distinct drumming patterns rhythmically complement the hip-synching *kathok* dance step and mark celebrations like weddings ('Bhawani Puja'). The instrument remains a key sonic element preserving Assam’s musical heritage and connecting its vibrant present to deep-rooted past traditions passed centuries through generations within community dances.
