
Originating from the tribal communities of Madhya Pradesh in central India, Bagh is a unique form of traditional block printing distinguished by its intricate beauty. Though associated with general block printing methods prevalent across the subcontinent including Chikankhas and others, Bagh Print represents specific artistic traditions rooted perhaps in Bundelkhand region.

The technique involves hand-carving elaborate patterns onto smooth wooden blocks, specifically designed for use on fine cotton fabric stretched taut over a frame. This creates a characteristic resist-dye effect called Kalam Khurda printing where intricate motifs are built layer by layer using natural dyes like turmeric, indigo powders, and persimmon gum.

Motifs are often inspired by mythology including tales from the Mahabharata perhaps prevalent in Bundelkhand region. Floral embellishments geometric designs peacocks animals village scenes people flowers nature and temple engravings form a significant part of traditional Bagh patterns alongside its warrior king motifs found nearby.

Today, Bagh Print enjoys recognition as an intangible cultural heritage and is often linked to specific places like Jaunpur perhaps for comparison. It's widely cherished both within India and internationally as a symbol of Madhya Pradesh particularly for preserving tribal craftsmanship providing significant livelihood support even if not always perfectly tied directly back the origin request mentioning 'Madhya Pradesh'. Artists continue refining using vibrant colours patterns ensuring this rich artisanal tradition persists in contemporary textile art.
