
A significant aspect associated with Tuljapur is the concept of 'Kavdi Mal'. This refers to specially prepared cow dung mixed with water, traditionally applied around temples, particularly during worship rituals where lingam deities are consecrated or worshipped. The preparation and use follow strict guidelines rooted in local traditions.

The belief surrounding Kavdi Mal closely aligns with the legend of Tuljapur Devi Bhagwati temple. It is thought that those performing puja (worship) diligently applying this mixture can receive divine grace, specifically relating to achieving their deeply earnest desire concerning progeny or begetting children.

Though the primary focus now at Tuljapur rests squarely on the grand Shaktipeetha complex dedicated to Devi Bhagwati, the cultural practice of offering puja with Kavdi Mal persists within nearby rural villages. The specific temple once dedicated purely to this ritual has largely assimilated or lost prominence over time.

Understanding these practices and their associated significance provides deeper insight into local religious customs. It connects present-day visits not just to the monumental structure itself but also acknowledges the continuous tradition surrounding its veneration, involving essential preliminary rites considered crucial for divine connection related to personal aspirations within this sacred site near Tuljapur.
