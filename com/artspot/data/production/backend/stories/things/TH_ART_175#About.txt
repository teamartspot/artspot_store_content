
Banda Shazar Patthar, a celebrated craft from Tiruchirappalli in Tamil Nadu, India, translates roughly as 'Hand Block Stone Print'. This unique art form distinguishes itself through its primary material: intricately carved blocks traditionally made of granite or soapstone, sometimes lime stone. Craftsmen create these patterns themselves or commission carvers based on designs provided – often from textiles like silk sarees.

The process involves applying fine grounds and vibrant colours meticulously to the textured surface of the handmade block, then pressing it firmly onto a wet, smooth slab of carved stone. Drying is crucial between applications to prevent smudging. This layer-by-layer approach allows for building depth with multiple colour coats, filling in the pattern's recesses.

The stones leave distinct impressions – subtle grooves replacing sharp lines – resulting in an image that feels textured when touched or viewed. It captures a different aesthetic than typical stencil work but conveys similar motifs using print techniques on surfaces like paper or cloth (both original and block versions). Historically, 'Patthu' itself refers to the cotton handloom silk used as its base material from day one.

Despite evolving trends, Banda Shazar Patthar remains a significant craft form. The intricate designs produced are visually stunning. It's also incredibly economical considering the materials; even simple uncarved soap stones can be bought affordably locally while providing ample surface area for carving beautiful patterns.

Block versions offer an accessible way to create multiple original pieces, showcasing design versatility and vibrant print outcomes across paper or cloth like silk/water colour fabric. Like much Bandicadam art, it serves as a vital medium of visual language and expression for artisans today.
