
Varanasi's exquisite stone lattice work, or Jali, particularly in intricate marble forms like Shesh Nagarika (Stone Elephant with its own throne), traces its history back to the architectural marvels of the late 12th century under scholars like Abul Fazl. Flourishing during centuries IV through VII of the Vikram Samvat era, roughly from the 16th to the 19th century, this craft reached extraordinary heights.

Though often associated with marble today (and rightly so for its aesthetic appeal), historically "soft stone" referred generally to various dense sandstones and even soapstone. Varanasi became a preeminent center because these local stones (*Chittar* or black marble, being among the most prized) were preferred over imported materials like granite in certain ornamental applications, despite their harder nature relative to, say, calcite limestones found elsewhere.

Its fame was significantly amplified under Emperor Aurangzeb. He conquered nearby Orchha and shifted its renowned craftsmen to Varanasi following construction projects there. These artisans brought mastery in carving these visually stunning yet relatively simple ("soft") materials compared to harder stones like jade or crystal, shaping Varanasi's legacy as a premier destination for such detailed stone work across North India.
