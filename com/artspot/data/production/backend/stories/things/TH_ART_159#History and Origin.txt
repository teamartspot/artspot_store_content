
Karuppur Kalamkari is a renowned form of hand-painted silk textile from the twin states of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, situated along the eastern Ghats. The specific name usually denotes paintings done directly on raw silk fabric using Mysore stone colour, locally known as kalanjuvaru ellusilakalasini - literally 'silk prepared with colours sourced from stones'.

The origin and history of Karuppur Kalamkari likely stem around the late 16th century. It is thought to have flourished during the Golconda period under Islamic rule, absorbing Persian influences while deeply rooted in India's rich natural motifs and spiritual themes reflecting Vaishnavism.

Unlike some other regional kalamkari styles like those from Srisailam or Machilipatnam which might use printed backgrounds, Karuppur typically features entirely hand-painted silk. This direct painting gives it a unique texture with the visible brush strokes of the artist standing prominently on the cloth surface.

The art form's imagery often depicts narratives from Hindu epics and Puranas (like Vishnu temples showing his ten avatars), divine themes, hunting expeditions led by rulers, or royal processions. Its aesthetic appeal lies in intricate linear work – delicate kalam lines depicting flora, animals, humans - executed with vibrant natural colour palettes derived mainly from mineral ores like lapis lazuli and malachite along with vegetable sources such as blue spinach (indigo). The result is visually stunning craftsmanship prized for its depth, precision, and cultural significance.
