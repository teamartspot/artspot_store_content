
Chamba Rumal Sarees hail from the Chamba region of Himachal Pradesh, a tradition born in the late 19th century. The name 'Rumal' signifies a hand-painted piece or 'turban cloth', distinguishing these intricate shawls-turned-saris as unique creations.

Initially woven traditionally for royal families and artists during festivals, art appreciation increased their value significantly. These fabrics are characterized by fine bandhani (Tie-dye) print work on silk or polyester satin, often with hand-embroidered panels at the border and pallav (the end piece). Mirrored bands running vertically on both sides give rise to the characteristic subtle slits when worn.

Inspired by local folk tales, art forms, and nature, Chamba Rumals feature exquisite mythological themes, floral patterns, or depictions of daily life. The technique involves complex tie-dyeing processes creating fine dots (bandhini) along with geometric sequences known as chiragis or mirror bands. An intricate layer is added through metallic leaf or silk thread work called Gitti.

Development gained momentum post-Independence as artists received patronage from institutions and the royal families of neighboring rulers, who provided saris to visiting dignitaries free of charge. This tradition continues today under the Swarnajayanti Gram Yojana Handloom Development Scheme, ensuring artisans' livelihoods despite challenging competition with power looms.
