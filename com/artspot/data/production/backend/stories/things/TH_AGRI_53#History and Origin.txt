
Ah, perhaps you meant 'Udupi Mattu Gulla Brinjal' (chilli)? Correcting the spelling slightly for clarity.

Legend has it that this fiery green chilli pepper was gifted by a saint to the community at the old *Matt* in Udupi, Karnataka. The word origin is debated. Some sources link '*Gulla*' to Sanskrit 'Jalauka' or related Dravidian terms like *kullu*, translating roughly as 'the fiery one.' It also carries associations with medicinal benefits attributed by Ayurvedic lore.

Another prominent theory connects its name directly to the Udupi region itself. Believing it originally referred specifically to local cultivars grown in this fertile area, distinguishing them perhaps from other North Indian varieties. Regardless of the exact etymology debate, it established a strong culinary identity here.

The key element is that this specific sweet pepper variety – likely originating within or intensely cultivated around Udupi – was integral to traditional cuisine and temple offerings long before regional spice names gained prominence nationally under branding initiatives like 'Aah! Prapancha.' This humble chilli wasn't just an ingredient; it wove itself into the very fabric of local cooking, adding heat subtly.
