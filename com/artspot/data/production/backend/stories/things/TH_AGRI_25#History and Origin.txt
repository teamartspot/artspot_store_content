
The story of Monsooned Malabar Arabica coffee begins deep within the forests of the Western Ghats, primarily around the regions surrounding Mysore (now part of Karnataka) and Kerala. Its journey is somewhat counterintuitive; this distinct character originates from unscreened or low-quality Arabica beans – what were initially considered "seconds" after being dried following a first wet milling.

For centuries, these secondary parchment lots remained under storage sheds during the summer months when coffee could not be harvested. The humidity began to work its magic. Unscreened parchment was exposed to moist air, initiated by high temperatures inside barns packed for transport and initial drying. As summer faded into Kerala's monsoon season – typically spanning from June to September or until November in Mysore – these dampened lot parchment bags caught the steady rainwater, prolonging and intensifying the humidity cycle within the beans.

This moist curing process fundamentally altered the beans' composition: sugars dissolved slowly, esters broke down, acids reduced. It resulted in a transformation rather than simple weathering (as sometimes mistakenly perceived). The resulting coffee featured lower acidity but retained vibrant notes characteristic of fine Arabica, along with unique spicy-herbal overtones and profound sweetness that developed through low-and-slow fermentation during storage.

British colonial traders discovered these "seconds" being used by local Mysore rulers to flavor their beer in the early 1800s. The drinkable profile post-fermentation won them over, highlighting these beans' suitability for brewing directly from their parchment state. This method bypassed expensive fine milling processes and screenings, leading the English market to embrace it as "Mysore monsoon" coffee (often mistakenly referred to simply as Bangalore coffee). While imperfect when freshly dry-milled due to inconsistent moisture levels, it proved exceptional once brewed properly during and immediately after the heavy rainy season humidity. This unique flavor established itself quickly, enduring to this day despite global advancements in processing techniques for high-quality coffee.
