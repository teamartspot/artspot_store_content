
Gwalior handmade carpets originate from India's Madhya Pradesh state.

While often mistaken for Persian weavings due to their aesthetic appeal, they developed independently nearby in the Bagh village region over two centuries ago. Made primarily of wool (goat hair) and sometimes silk, these carpets are renowned for their rich colours derived from natural mineral dyes, giving vibrant textures resistant to fading. Their intricate craftsmanship sets them apart.

Gwalior carpets often feature complex geometric patterns, bold animal motifs (including deities), hunting scenes, and depictions of village life or royal courts from the colonial era. Each unique design is typically woven by an entire community of artisans working together on a single loom, passed down through generations with stories woven into their creation.

More than just textile, they are considered 'painted' floor art because each family guards its unique designs, making replication nearly impossible and ensuring originality from the first to the last. They serve as cultural ambassadors, adorning homes with intricate detail while telling tales of craftsmanship, devotion, and daily life.
