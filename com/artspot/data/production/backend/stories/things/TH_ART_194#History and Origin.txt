
The Kapadaganda: Dungaria Kondh Embroidered Shawl is not merely an item of clothing but a profound symbol born from the ancestral lands and culture of its people, the Dunga Ria or Dungaria Oraon community in what is now Jharkhand's Palamou subdivision. They named their land 'Dung' referring to plains, combined with 'Ria', meaning forested areas, giving rise directly to the term.

These intricate woolen shawls are traditionally woven and embroidered by married women using threads of naturally dyed colours derived from local plants such as neem or kusum. Beyond its daily use for warmth, they often served specific purposes – protecting against the perceived coldness during pregnancy or worn only inauspicious times like after death or childbirth initially kept it hidden.

The complex patterns adorning these shawls are believed to depict scenes and protect families from evil forces. This narrative weaves together the geography ('Dungria'), local materials (wool, vegetable dyes), the role of married women as artisans (symbolizing established status), intricate symbolic designs (from protective talismans), and a deep connection to tribal identity through its name.
