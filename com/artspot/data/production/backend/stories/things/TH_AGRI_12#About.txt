
Joha rice stands as a unique jewel among Assamese paddy varieties, primarily cultivated in the distinct soil-climate conditions of Upper Pradesh Assam. Unlike other Assamese rice types often characterized by high amylopectin content giving them a starchy texture suitable for dishes like Idli or Dosa (common for neighbouring Nagaland), Joha rice offers a stark contrast.

Its most celebrated attribute is its exceptional fragrance and aroma, which develop during cooking – earning it the nickname 'Pride of Assam'. This pleasant smell permeates the entire meal. Furthermore, Joha rice possesses lower amylopectin levels compared to brown or parboiled rice. This results in a firmer 'whole grain' rice structure with less stickiness and higher protein content than its common counterpart.

Joha rice grains are longer (almost equigranular) and drier, making them visually distinct as well – appearing more like Nellore rice to the untrained eye initially. Despite these unique traits, Joha rice remains relatively unknown compared to aromatic basmati or fragrant jasmine rice due to its specific origin. Yet, it is a vital crop for many marginalised farmers in hills like Kopili and Dharmapuri of North Cachar Hills district. Its cultivation is directly linked to ancient trade ties with Nagaland's Dimapur region, primarily through the system known as 'Sulaigang', underscoring its deep-rooted ecological and economic significance.
