
clamp dyeing represents a significant advancement in cotton fabric colouration. It involves clamping loosely twisted yarns within heavy wooden frames or metal cylinders fitted with planks bearing small holes, creating spaces between layers where liquor saturates and dyes penetrate effectively.

Once clamped tightly, these multi-stage 'locks' are drawn together vertically through the gaps between planks while submerged in hot vats of dye solution. This process applies even tension on the yarns, allowing for uniform absorption across the fabric bulk despite its loose structure before dyeing. Unlike mercerised cotton that dyes evenly to a single shade due to reduced bulk and increased lustre from a previous treatment stage, clamp-dyed textiles offer high colour strength and intensity.

Its primary purpose is providing an initial solid ground colour, especially important in indigo dyeing. The result – known simply as 'clamped' or 'clamp colour'-dyed yarn – consistently takes one distinct shade throughout the warp bulk (length), ready for weaving to form denim fabric with excellent colour penetration even through multiple washings typical of raw denim processing.
