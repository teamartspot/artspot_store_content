
The Alleppey Green Cardamom, hailing primarily from the Perumkulam region within Alappuzha (Alleppy) district in Kerala, India, boasts a rich history deeply rooted in the local agricultural expertise and environmental conditions.

Its story began formally when Dutch colonial administrators sought to cultivate cardamom in their East Indies settlement. They dispatched experts back to Kerala, the heartland of cardamom cultivation, who identified knowledgeable farmers near Alleppey as suitable candidates. M.G. Shelvankutty played a pivotal role by taking seeds from his native lands and developing green seedlings under specific Dutch conditions.

Kerala's monsoonal climate, nutrient-rich laterite soil nurtured this heritage. Once established in the Alleppey region, these cardamom plants thrived, yielding pods renowned for their intense flavour, rich aroma (often containing jasmine notes), and vibrant dark-green or blackish colour – characteristics signifying maturity.

This distinctiveness proved crucial. The specific environmental conditions and cultivation methods near Alleppey produced a superior quality compared to cardamom grown elsewhere in Kerala or globally under different systems. The taste profile was uniquely prized, setting Alleppey Green Cardamom apart as the highest grade available.

Over time, Alleppey became synonymous with this top-tier green variety, securing its reputation not only within India but on the international stage for exceptional spice quality.
