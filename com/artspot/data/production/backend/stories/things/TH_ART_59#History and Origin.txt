
Moradabad, a historic city in Uttar Pradesh, India, is synonymous with intricate metal craftsmanship. Its origins trace back centuries to Mughal reigns in the 17th century. Prince Daulat Khan Mirawar laid its foundation after Aurangzeb shifted Delhi's mint establishment.

Initially, artisans worked primarily on imperial orders for decorative purposes and utilitarian needs like sword sheaths (Dastars) or coins. The craft flourished under skilled Mughal masters brought by royalty, particularly Emperor Jahangir who called the work 'Taqiya Nishan'. Early designs were simpler geometric patterns.

Over time, Moradabad's metal ware diversified. Centuries later, it became renowned for Mina Art (polished enamel) and intricate filigree work known as Sheesh Mahal or Gota Mahal. These techniques involve binding gold/silver thread to form images within an enamel-filled background or creating detailed pierced designs.

Initially serving royalty in Agra, Delhi, Fatehpur Sikri, the craft spread across India via land routes through Moradabad (then a stopover). Its popularity grew in trading centres like Lucknow, Varanasi eventually cementing its reputation here. These pieces became globally sought after too.
