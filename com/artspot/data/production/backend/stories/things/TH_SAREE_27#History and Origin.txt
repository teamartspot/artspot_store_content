
Bomkai are exquisite handwoven silk sarees predominantly from Odisha, crafted by the Ganga community of weavers primarily located in Nuapada and Nayaaka Patna districts bordering Andhra Pradesh. Originating approximately half a century ago along this cultural divide between traditional Kollur Silk (Odisha) and Rayadurg Muslin (Andhra Pradesha), their story begins with resourcefulness.

Early Ganga weavers, often struggling financially, spun cocoons locally to produce raw silk. This allowed them to weave intricate designs without relying on imported threads or royal patronage common in earlier periods like the sixteenth-century Kotha period preceding pure silk integration saw rich zari work but limited motifs due budget constraints forcing local adaptation.

The name 'Bomkai' itself signifies the spirit of innovation, derived from weaving (bunki) using locally produced cocoons. Initially known as Bomkalu Puli Saree or Orissa style Andhra border sarees, these weaves quickly gained recognition and a dedicated market.

This unique artform further flourished with exposure through renowned Handloom periodicals like Dotsakhi from Visakhapatnam promoting indigenous silk weaving techniques including its characteristic 'Naginattu' (cobra stripe) motifs combined subtly with damier check patterns. Bomkai also finds resonance, mirroring similar weaves across south-east Odisha and parts of Andhra Pradesh in places like Anavapuram. Today, Bomkai sarees are celebrated for their rich colours, heavy silk fabric, and complex temple-inspired weaving from the Bhagavat Geeta (Devotional) theme. They stand as a living testament to weaver ingenuity and regional craftsmanship.
