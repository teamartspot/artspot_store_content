
Mathura Sanjhi craft synthesizes two distinct forms of artistic expression. Its history and origin trace back to the ancient city of Mathura in Uttar Pradesh, a cradle of Indian art renowned for its early stone carving traditions, including fine marble work. Legend credits this style's development during Akbar's reign (early 17th century) to painters who studied under Ustad Bishandas, specifically noting contributions from artists named Sanjay and Jhar Singh near Delhi. These highly skilled craftspeople brought the refined aesthetic of Moghul miniature painting, characterized by intricate detail and colourful composition, to Mathura. The Mathuran artisans seamlessly integrated this technique with their established proficiency in carving marble, birchwood, sandalwood, ivory, and jasper for religious images.

The resulting Mathura Sanjihi style is distinguished by its polychrome carvings – often depicting scenes from Hindu mythology or deities' lives (aikya-bhava-vardhanakas), set against detailed backgrounds painted in vibrant oils. These figures became integral to public worship, replacing earlier predominantly plain white marble sculptures. Beyond devotional use, Mathura Sanjhi pieces also adorned palaces and private collections during the Mughal era, showcasing their decorative potential alongside religious iconography. This synthesis of fine painting skills with carving prowess represents a unique artistic achievement born from cross-regional influences converging in Mathura during that period.
