
Budithi Bell & Brass Metal Craft refers to traditional Indian metalworking techniques primarily focused on creating intricate designs from bell metal or brass. Characterised by thin sheets hammered together rather than intricate casting processes like those yielding the more elaborately carved Madhubani paintings or Pattachitra.

Its origins trace back nearly two millennia, flourishing during significant Hindu temple-building dynasties in Bengal and Orissa, such as the Palas and Senas (c 7th-12th centuries). These periods witnessed advancements where artisans mastered methods to build complex patterns from relatively simple metal sheets. The craft likely evolved gradually from simpler decorative work into its distinctively patterned style.

Over centuries, it was refined by dynasties including the Malla, Tantric, and particularly during British colonial rule; however, these terms might represent regional names or styles rather than a single unified origin story across all regions using brass. The core process – crafting thin, often circular metal pieces into elaborate compositions through meticulous hammering patterns onto a base sheet – seems fundamental.

Often used for ritualistic and decorative objects, its history is embedded in the evolution of Hindu religious practices and courtly arts throughout India's history. It represents a specific approach within the broader Indian tradition of metal craft development from early experimental periods onwards.
