
Mothra sarees represent a vibrant and unique tradition in Indian textile art, emerging as the close-knit sibling of Assam's renowned Nalli but distinct from them through their specific association with musical themes.

The origin story often links directly to Assamese musicians. When performers required new outfits or alterations specifically designed for elaborate dance sequences and costume changes inherent in Kathi Nritya (classical dances), local weavers adapted the traditional Nalli fabric, its dense weft of pure silk woven into intricate floral gold zari work spread over a plain background typically in red velvet or Benipatti jamawar fabric. The challenge lay in creating multiple wearable layers from a single piece without excessive weight.

To solve this and make musical narratives visible on the cloth itself, 'Mothra' (or Moti) weaving was developed exclusively for Assam's rich musical performances like Dandiya Nritya from Gujarat occasionally used or for general musicals. Artists would conceptualize the stage costume through song arrangements with weavers; thus sections of the saree panel were reserved for specific songs, depicting characters and actions primarily to the tune of 'Baat' (a common Assamese folk music piece). The elaborate gold zari work often incorporated motifs related to traditional Assamese musical instruments like the Damar or Sanju.

This direct fusion solidified the Mothra saree's identity not just as a stylish bridal or festival attire, but uniquely as an embodiment and visualization of Assam's musical heritage woven onto fabric.
