
Monsooned Malabar Robusta coffee represents a unique category rather than merely another bean variety. Its defining characteristic stems directly from its journey: green Robusta coffee beans, typically shipped from India's Western Ghats region (the historic 'Malabar' coast) to ports like Calicut or Cochin during the rainy monsoon season.

This extended exposure – often lasting several months or even years – triggers a transformation in flavor. Without undergoing roasting process until potentially centuries later upon arrival elsewhere (like Yemen), these beans absorb moisture from the damp air and sea spray. This careful wet-hanging drying under continuous moderate humidity, distinct from "wet cave" processes, creates remarkable changes.

The resulting coffee possesses low natural acidity, a truly full body often compared to port, a very strong aftertaste that holds its own even with milk or sugar (making it affordable), and notes described as almost medicinal. This process also imparts distinctive water-like fermentation characteristics coupled with occasional hints of lime citrus on the palate.

Often considered the world’s first commercially shipped wet-processed coffee due to this long transit, Monsooned Malabar has been traded for centuries. The aging journey contributes significantly to its singular taste profile. A cup reveals a coffee steeped in history and transformation.
