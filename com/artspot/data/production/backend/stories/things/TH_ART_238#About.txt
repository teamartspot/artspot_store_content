
For generations, the dugout canoe known as 'Hodi' has been synonymous with maritime life among many communities across South Asia, but nowhere more perfectly adapted or culturally significant than with the Nicobari people of the Andaman & Nicobar Islands. Traditionally constructed by carving out a hollow from a single log, often using large Tej pata (*Erythrina roxburghii*) or Narive *Narial* tree trunks (*Sterculia sameta*), these Hodi canoes are iconic examples of sustainable technology and cultural heritage.

The distinctive shape, coupled with highly skilled craftsmanship ensuring straight planks capable of holding water, made the Ovoid-shaped (often slightly curved) Hodi invaluable. It wasn't just a vessel; it was their primary link across the vast archipelago's channels for fishing, travel, transport, trade, and spiritual journeys.

These vessels also played a crucial role in connecting Nicobari villages socially and economically long before modern transportation arrived, representing a unique and enduring connection of these unique people to their island environment.
