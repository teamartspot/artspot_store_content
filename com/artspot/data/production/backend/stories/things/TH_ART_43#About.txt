
A unique community residing in the scenic hill station of Coorg, Karnataka, are the Kurumba people. Often distinguished as one part of the larger Sari tribes alongside Kodava and Tulu speaking communities, they inhabit small pockets across the Nilgiris border region and parts of Dakshina Kannada.

Their identity is strongly associated with nature; living amidst lush coffee plantations, spice gardens, and dense forests since time immemorial. Historically a part of the ancient kingdom of 'Sandeshree' or 'Sattari', their culture reflects this close-knit relationship with their homeland's rich ecology. They share linguistic roots as Dravidian communities but possess distinct cultural practices.

Kurumbas are well-renowned for their vibrant and intricate wood carvings, primarily depicting floral and animal motifs using sacred fig trees (Peepal). Their craftsmanship is integral to their lives. Beyond artistry, they actively participate in agriculture alongside being prominent coffee growers themselves in Coorg, contributing significantly to its green reputation.

The 'Kurumba' name itself often translates from Sanskrit words meaning "free man" or one living away in the hills/forest area (from '*kura'-'hill', '*m'ba'-inhabitant') signifying their distinct tribal identity differentiating them from settled communities. Their lifestyle, language variations known as Kuramba Linga Kannada, and customs set them apart even today amidst changing times. Outsiders frequently perceive their simple living directly connected to nature that shapes their art and livelihoods.
