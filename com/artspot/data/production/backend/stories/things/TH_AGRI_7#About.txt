
Guntur Sannam refers specifically to a distinct variety of pepper hailing from the Guntur region in Andhra Pradesh, India. Despite its name potentially being confused with regulations or branding around the 'Sannam' aspect (which isn't always standard), the key point is its origin and characteristics.

This particular type provides heat that's generally sweet-medium compared to some other Indian peppers like Kashmiri, which are milder in spice but significantly different in use. The reddish fruits possess specific qualities valued locally for various dishes requiring medium heat. Fresh usage includes pickling or adding flavor while retaining texture; the pods can be sliced and added as a hot component.

Used both fresh (in curries, chutneys) and roasted dry ('sorghum kalanji' type), dried leaves also find use for tempering oil when fried with spices before cooking. They offer distinctiveness beyond just local availability, prized for their balanced heat combined with moderate fruitiness rather than being overly pungent like some fresh bird's eye types or intensely aromatic like mithurishta.
