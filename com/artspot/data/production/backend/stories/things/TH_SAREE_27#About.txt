
One might picture a Bomkai saree as an antique, hand-woven on traditional looms centuries ago. This is often true for older pieces, but the remarkable thing is that today’s stunning examples are still predominantly created by these very weavers in functioning handloom clusters across Odisha.

The name "Bomkai" itself tells a story – believed to mean 'beautiful' or 'excellence of art'. Hailing from regions near Jagannath Temple in Puri, Bomkai sarees exemplify Odori village craft. The hallmark is their vibrant borders, created with intricate double ikat techniques executed entirely by hand.

Unlike many weaves popularised for mass markets via power looms elsewhere in India, genuine Bomkai are typically made on traditional pit-looms, often using pure cotton yarns dyed with natural colours before the final tie-dye process (mordanting). Two prominent motifs stand out: Phadiya and Patua. Phadis feature bold stripes of contrasting colour combining geometric shapes and flowers against a solid field in the border; Patuas are more ornate, with flower-scrolls combined with animal figures often appearing within an 'ashtalings' composition.

More than just clothing, Bomkai silk sarees carry immense cultural significance. They provide livelihoods for numerous weavers involved in ikat artistry and stand as living heritage pieces from Odisha’s royal weaving centres.
