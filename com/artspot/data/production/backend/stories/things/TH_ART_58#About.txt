
A remarkable testament to India's vibrant artisan traditions is the ancient art of *Mata ni Pachedi*, also known as 'Maskandi'. Originating from the Munda community residing in the dense forests of Odisha, particularly Jharkhand, this unique hand-painted manuscript illumination technique forms part of their rich visual culture.

Also recognized as a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, *Mata ni Pachedi* is distinguished by its direct painting onto cloth stretched over a shallow wooden frame. Artists meticulously prepare the canvas using an intricate process involving cow milk, gum Arabic (gesha), and egg white binder mixed with locally sourced natural pigments yielding striking earthy colours. This distinct use of 'Soo' or *Suaara* – a specially created paste from tamarind pulp – is applied using fingers, sometimes assisted by simple tools like bamboo skewers (*Kutchi*) or matchsticks.

The narratives depicted hew closely to the socio-religious life and agricultural cycles of the tribal communities. Common themes include traditional mythologies, creation stories involving ancestral deities ('Mata Rani Samblesvari' being central), saints, kings from epics like Fenaasheeli, everyday rural scenes such as weddings or hunting gatherers – capturing a worldview deeply connected to nature and local beliefs.

This simple yet profound art form offers an intimate connection with the past. It showcases exceptional skill in narrative sequence, composition, and symbolism derived directly from *Mata ni Pachedi* is more than just an art collection; it represents living history, cultural memory, and the vital preservation work being done to keep this unique visual storytelling tradition alive for future generations.
