Pokará Village Pottery refers to the distinctive ceramic traditions developed around Pokara village (or nearby pueblos) located on the Huancas River corridor in Peru's Ayacucho department. While perhaps not as immediately globally renowned as other legendary pottery centers like Chuquotocota or Andahuaylallo, the productions from Pokará hold significant regional importance for their unique character and are integral to the artistic landscape of northeast-central Peru.

This pottery style is characterized by its two main forms: a blackware version known for its deep glossy finish, often decorated with simple geometric shapes; and a highly prized white ware (llama blanca), celebrated for exceptionally fine bone china that includes intricate designs frequently marked by lines or stripes in alternating colors against the pristine white background. The pots themselves range widely but commonly include large storage vessels essential for traditional grain keeping and smaller items like ceremonial cups, platters, bowls, ladles, and unique decorative figurines.

The specific origin of Pokará pottery is sometimes debated or involves multiple community traditions along similar highland routes near Tumbes province in Ayacucho. Nonetheless, its association paints a vibrant picture of Andean craftsmanship, embodying the artistic heritage and ingenuity passed down through generations within these communities.
