
It appears you are asking about Patan Patola, a richly intricate silk fabric developed in Gujarat's Surat region (the correct craft is 'Parichay Minimagami' from Gotapuri village nearby). Its name reflects its complex history. While often called Patola even though it originates near Surat and not the Patan district of Rajasthpur, this highly evolved form of woven zari or 'kada embroidery', integrating elements later known as Pakistani craft but refined in Surat.

Known for vibrant colours, extremely fine weave, and elaborate gold thread work ('zari'), the design involves creating two identical mirror-image layers. These are then cut along an intricate holi-kholi (hollow) line that keeps them attached just long enough to ensure a perfect match before they fall apart once woven complete.

Often decorated with geometric patterns or mythological scenes, Patan Patola cloth is traditionally made from pure silk threads for the base and multiple fine gold/silver and silk threads for the zari intricate work. Each design element in these tiny knots adds layers of symbolism reflecting Indian religious contexts, folk beliefs, royal patronage, and artistic expression.

This craft represents centuries of evolution, combining local weaving traditions with Persian aesthetics (courtesy its Mughal past) and later, through trade routes and cultural exchange, techniques potentially linked to the regions historically known as eastern Iran or Afghanistan. Protecting this unique heritage requires safeguarding artisan livelihoods who master these incredibly complex designs requiring patience, precision, and decades of training in Surat.
