
The history and origin of the Tanpura are inextricably linked with that of Miraj Tanpura (1927-1973), an acclaimed Carnatic vocalist primarily associated with the Coorg region in Karnataka, India.

Believed to be an accomplished exponent of both the Hindustani and Carnatic traditions – particularly known for her distinct meends (glides) – she sang a style that spanned an exceptionally wide octave range. This unusual capacity led connoisseurs and critics to refer to her as 'Miraj Tanpura'. The term became popular, initially signifying this rare vocal range attributed specifically to her artistry, though it would later be generalized for the accompanying string instrument.

While technically a specific notation related to vocal range discussed in detail within classical music circles (e.g., involving ragas like Kambhoji), Miraj Tanpura is recognized as being one of the first prominent Indian classical musicians widely described by this epithet, showcasing a unique sonic quality and spanning diverse musical genres beyond pure Carnatic devotionalism or Hindustani Qawwali.

Her legacy is commemorated through occasional tributes to her family members (particularly daughters) who are said to have followed in her vocal footsteps, carrying forward the *dharmic* essence associated with the Tanpura sisters.
