
Ringali is more than just an art form; it's a vibrant testimony to the ingenuity of Uttarakhand’s indigenous communities. This traditional craft thrives among forest-dwelling tribes like the Gorkhas, Rajis, and Tharus spread across districts such as Almora, Teerathgarh, Champawat, Pithoragarh, Nanda Nagar, Bageshwar in Kumaon region of Uttarakhand. Initially practiced within these communities for practical needs – baskets, pots, bags – Ringali has transformed over centuries, evolving into an intricate textile art using hand-spun silk and fine zari threads on fabric.

The heart of Ringali lies in its minute, interlaced gold thread work called *Dharmati*. Designs often depict flowers like the blue trumpet flower (*Ringali Badli*), sun motifs, sacred symbols, geometric patterns, traditional Uttarakhand colours such as crimson red and black. These complex, shimmering *zari* designs are meticulously woven onto silk fabric using a rigid H-shaped bamboo frame, featuring double-heddle techniques requiring immense skill.

Often created in limited quantities for ceremonial or personal use by families across Kumaon regions, it serves diverse purposes - adorning temples as bridal dresses court attire clothing and accessories. Its fine texture detailed interlace and ethereal appearance distinguish it among tribal handlooms nationally known. Craftswomen who are largely responsible for its evolution continue the tradition, creating jewellery with intricate Ringali designs on silk threads along necklaces bracelets brooches and other ethnic wear pieces today. In essence, Ringali weaves together beauty craft functionality tribe showcasing resilience culture of highlands Uttarakhand.
