
Tirukkanur, a bustling town near Mysore, stands out as the birthplace of papier-mâché art in India. This unique crafting tradition uses kappu grass or Aruvanaru reed – locally known to be sourced from ash palm twigs and bark initially combined with clay paste – as its base material, unlike other regions that primarily rely on synthetic paper. Coloured artificial flowers are often incorporated into these pieces, ranging from purely decorative items like lamps and boxes to intricate scenes of deities or traditional stories.

The craft process was famously pioneered by artisan H.A. Hoskote (or perhaps others with the surname Hoysaligere), who sought alternatives for temple decorations during a time when wood carvings were scarce in his fatherland village nearby. The resulting papier-mâché pieces became remarkably popular, found frequently adorning donkeys and horses on local roads, especially during festive periods like Deepavali.

In Tirukkanur, the entire town isn't designated as an art hub; yet, papier-mâché has become synonymous with this specific region due to its distinct techniques and materials developed by local artisans. The tradition continues today, providing valuable livelihoods while showcasing the ingenuity of these craftspeople in creating vibrant and unique decorative items.
