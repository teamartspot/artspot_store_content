
Srikalahasti Kalamkari, an exquisite form of hand-drawn and block-printed resist artistry associated with Andhra Pradesh, traces its origins back centuries. Flourishing prominently from the 16th century onwards in regions like Srikalahasti, Tadappa Choutipeta, Ongole, Guntur, Tenali, Narasapuram, Anagallu, and Peddapeddana, it owes much to the Vijayanagara Empire (also known as the Tulu Country) which fostered regional art.

The name "Kalamkari" itself signifies its unique process. It combines two distinct techniques: **Kalame** (the drawing with stick/dye, used for backgrounds or outlines in resist printing) and **Karikku** (resist print using a stamp). The former involves artists directly drawing intricate designs onto the fabric with natural dyes, while the latter uses prepared wooden blocks dipped in dye to create patterns. Predominantly executed on silk (the finest being Pothana Silk or Chetti-Silk), multicoloured Mordant-resist paintings depict mythological narratives and legends from Hindu Puranas.

Originating initially as cotton works adorned with intricate designs drawn by the Kalame process, it evolved significantly around 1600 AD. Itinerant Muslim artists brought advanced resist techniques like Bandhini (asphalt or wax) which were skillfully adapted into the regional silk fabrics under the patronage of local rulers and weaving communities.

Textile patterns are executed in vibrant colours using natural dyes extracted from plants, minerals, and sometimes shellac – a unique feature compared to other Indian Kalamkari types. Gold and silver zari threads often embellish final pieces, used for borders or highlighting sacred motifs like elephants heads, lotus flowers, and peacock eyes.

Srikalahasti Kalamkari retains its status as a traditional art form today, prized for its rich narratives woven with elaborate geometric and figurative detail on fine silk canvas. The craft still involves intricate craftsmanship and age-old techniques passed down through generations of artisans in the specific regions where it flourishes.
