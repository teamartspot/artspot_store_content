
Sankheda furniture takes its name from a district in Gujarat, India. However, the designs themselves evolved significantly elsewhere before finding popularity near Ankleshwar (now known as Sanchor). The quintessential example is the unique metal-framed chair characterised by large, curved seat supports often arranged around a rectangular backrest.

True origins lie perhaps somewhat further south or east across India's western plains, where similar structural elements, adapted from traditional agricultural carts and transport mechanisms used for carrying loads over sandy terrain, emerged. The cartwrights' ingenuity in creating sturdy curves out of beaten galvanised steel – designed to bear heavy weights without breaking under the stress of wagons – later found application in seating.

This practical, load-bearing design was brought to life in South India by chair makers who adapted and popularized forms like curved benches across cafes and restaurants. These pieces gained fame for their robust construction combined with an appealing aesthetic. They spread North, catching on particularly after large furniture manufacturers established plants near Sanchor beginning in the early 70s.

The heart of Sankheda modern furniture lies in the late '70s/early '80s when these durable designs transitioned from craft traditions to mass production. The region developed a reputation for manufacturing sturdy, comfortable chairs with innovative iron frameworks derived directly from this historically functional South Indian antecedent, offering unique seating solutions prized by many establishments and consumers alike.
