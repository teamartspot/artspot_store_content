
Originating in the hills around Ranchi, Jharkhand, Pipli Appliqué Work emerged organically from the resourcefulness of indigenous communities. They developed intricate techniques to embellish their simple cotton garments using locally available resources like mirrors, beads, metal threads, and fabric scraps.

The distinctive feature lies not just in mirror work but also in geometric shapes meticulously cut from silk broadcloth or sheer fabrics and sewn onto a darker base cloth – typically blue Kalamkari or coarse ikat. Figures are usually traced freehand by the weaver-artisans themselves before appliqué begins.

Initially used for practical clothing items like blouses, dupattas (long scarves), and petticoats worn in everyday village life or special community occasions, it has evolved into a highly regarded art form celebrating craftsmanship's creative expression and versatility. The name "Pipli" itself often refers to specific appliqué designs rather than just the craft.

This textile art form weaves together centuries-old techniques with influences absorbed over time, creating visually captivating garments that remain functional while exuding artistic integrity. Each piece tells a story through its patchwork beauty.
