
The unique tradition of Temple Jewellery in Nagercoil, Kerala, finds its origins uniquely linked to Madurai's Chidambaram Kovil Shilpakartriyar Kshetram. Commencing around a century ago through the benevolence inspired by local devotees visiting these significant sites, artisans began crafting exquisite gold articles – primarily lamps (oil and electronic), offering stands, pyramids, kavasals, candelabras, intricate crosses, and devotional figures like St. Peter or St. Paul.

Initially created as souvenirs for pilgrims from places like Madurai visiting Nagercoil's own Our Lady Star of the Sea Cathedral dedicated to the Immaculate Heart of Mary, this craft evolved locally. The designs showcase South Indian aesthetics, drawing inspiration from temple iconography yet retaining distinct characteristics developed in Nagercoil.

Crafted traditionally using techniques refined over time (primarily repoussé work), pure gold is the norm for many pieces sold at the temples themselves or their premises, reflecting religious sanctity and traditional craftsmanship pride. Today, while still deeply connected to place-of-worship requirements, Temple Jewellery represents a significant part of Nagercoil's cultural heritage, blending devotion with exceptional South Indian metalwork artistry.
