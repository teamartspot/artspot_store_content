
Arunachal Orange refers to the fruit produced from the unique growing conditions of Arunachal Pradesh in Northeast India and specifically recognized by its Protected Geographical Indication status. While not a conventional "Orange" like citrus fruits, it is distinguished as a specific landrace or clone believed to be native to this region.

This variety hails from the wild form of *Psidium cattleianum*, commonly known outside GGI boundaries as "Sugar-apple" or "Bottle-knap." Originating in South America but flourishing naturally in Arunachal's varied altitudes, its distinct taste is a product of unique microclimates and terroir – warm days coupled with cool nights at higher elevations.

The appeal lies in the natural environment where trees grow relatively wilder, free from heavy chemical intervention. Many cultivators employ manual harvesting techniques to ensure only perfectly ripe fruit reaches consumers, enhancing quality control even amidst nature's bounty. This process gives the Arunachal Orange its signature flavour profile, which local connoisseurs prize for its rich taste and satisfying texture, distinctly different from oranges grown elsewhere in India.

Nutritionally beneficial due to high vitamin C content, these oranges are celebrated not just for freshness but also as a unique part of Northeast India's agricultural heritage. They symbolize the potential for indigenous germplasm to yield exceptional produce when nurtured properly with some modern touch care and quality focus.
