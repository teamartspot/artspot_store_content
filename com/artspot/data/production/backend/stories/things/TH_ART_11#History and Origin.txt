
Bikaner painting emerged under the patronage of the Rathore royal family ruling Bikaner state, particularly during the reign of Maharaja Pratap Singh I (1753-1792). Like other Rajasthani schools developed later, it arose from painters migrating from established centres such as Mewar and Marwar. This migration occurred around 1460 BCE for some traditions, though Bikaner's specific emergence predates that period.

The distinct style of Bikaner art began taking shape more definitively around the mid-18th century. Early influences were indeed from Western artists or European prints imported via trade routes, especially through Gujarat ports like Surat.

The core subjects remained timeless Hindu legends (epics, Puranas), mythology, history, religious narratives, portraits of royalty and devaraja concepts mirroring gods in human form, hunting scenes reflecting the aristocracy's lifestyle, animal fables, and illustrations from sacred texts or Devotional poetry.

However, Bikaner artists significantly adapted these themes. They masterfully blended European artistic influences – notably linear perspective and chiaroscuro effects for depth and realism – with indigenous Indian sensibilities, vibrant colours, spiritual content like Vaishnavism often depicted through images of Radha and Krishna embracing in romantic intimacy within lyrical landscapes or palaces seen from a unique architectural viewpoint.

This synthesis created Bikaner's unique narrative style. Known for its luminous quality, charm derived partly from the distinct perspective, intricate cloud patterns framing scenes (often with bright blue skies), and characteristic decorative elements like cartouche borders and rhythmic flow lines within compositions.

Though patronage declined with changing eras, leading to periods of fading popularity, Bikaner painting stands today as a vital example of seventeenth-century Indian art traditions absorbed outside influence while creating something distinctly new.
