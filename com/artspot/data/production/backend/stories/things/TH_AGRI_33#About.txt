
Though perhaps less celebrated by name than the famous Aam Sonthi of Alipuren village in Malda, this region of West Bengal offers another special addition to its mango orchards: the distinctively long-legged variety often simply called "Fazli" Mango. Named after an ancient Mughal officer known for his fairness and justice (Maulavi Fazl- ul-Hak), these elongated fruits stand apart from their rounder counterparts, offering a unique appearance alongside excellent eating quality.

The Malda Fazli boasts an exceptionally long shape tapering from stem to tip, earning it the moniker. Its flesh is readily detachable but holds tightly together until cut through the white sapwood line – making it easy to scoop out whole pieces while leaving the fruit segments intact against the seed pit for superior fresh eating enjoyment free of fibrous sinew. The taste profile presents a delightful balance: naturally sweet with characteristic lusciousness yet retaining good solid texture and avoiding excessive mushiness, resulting in juicy non-syrupy pulp.

These mangoes typically reach their peak ripeness towards the end of Aam season, perhaps slightly later than the Sonthi cultivar, but they are no less a product of Malda’s fertile soil and skilled cultivation. While equally delicious fresh off the tree, Fazli Mango holds particular appeal for long-distance export due to its fine transportability – maintaining quality even during transit providing consumers outside West Bengal access to this flavour at various times throughout the mango season unlike normal Aam which might spoil faster if not harvested very early.

Therefore whether you encounter it locally or through trade, appreciate this elongated form as more than just fruit; it is often a testament to its origin – fine fresh quality and transportable goodness from Malda.
