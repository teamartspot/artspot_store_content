
Lanjia Saura IDITAL (Distinctive Indigenous Traditional Artistic Linkage) painting represents a unique form of narrative tribal art concentrated among the Santal community in Odisha's Kalahasti village and some villages in Bhavanigarhara and Pippalapadu near Visakhapatnam. This traditional process relies on animal ghee, primarily cow ghee mixed with ash residue from various sources for the base layer.

It is distinguished by its vibrant imagery directly applied onto temple walls or houses using natural colours derived entirely from locally available plants and minerals – soil, leaves, flowers, roots, clay mud, tree bark, flowers, fruits. The art form uniquely focuses on depicting human figures and specifically captures depictions of traditional marriage ceremonies ('Kavachanukulam').

While similar ritualistic floor paintings exist across different communities (Pitha, Rupasi, Devanga), Lanjia IDITAL has a strong association with Santal culture performed during initiation rituals or village functions. Its primary purpose is narrative and didactic rather than purely decorative.

Unfortunately, the practice has witnessed decline over time due to changing lifestyles, lack of patronage for ritual purposes, deforestation impacting colour availability, and socio-economic shifts making wall painting less common today compared to earlier generations despite its recognition as an art form needing preservation in Odisha.
