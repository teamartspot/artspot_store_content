
Gadwali sarees trace their roots back to the coastal town of Guntur (often referred as Gadwal historically) in the Rayalaseema region of Andhra Pradesh.

Historically considered a distinct style until the late 19th century, or perhaps emerging from similar weaves around 100-150 years ago, their origin is inextricably linked to Guntur's vibrant textile scene. These sarees are predominantly associated with the Narsipatgnari community, renowned silk weavers whose distinctive work forms the backbone of the Gadwali creation.

While historically linked perhaps more closely with the royal families and courts of Warangal (Jagtial cluster), who refined and popularized finer versions like the Pothys and Khajjies, Guntur remains its heartland. These sarees stand out for their elaborate use of heavy silver zari threads over a vibrant silk base.

Unlike intricate Kanjeevendis or fine Bomkars, Gandwali (Gadwali) designs are typically broader in weave and often feature stylized geometric patterns such as 'Vangala Bheemavolu' work and large animal motifs executed with heavy brocade. Their history, though perhaps less ancient than some other Indian saree traditions compared to silk weaves like those from Mysore or Kanchi, is significant within the context of Andhra Pradesh's rich handloom heritage, representing a unique regional craft still celebrated today for its bold texture and opulent appearance in modern fashion.
