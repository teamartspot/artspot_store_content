
The origin of Mishing Handloom Products lies deeply embedded in the rich cultural tapestry and agricultural practices of the Karbi Anglong region, primarily inhabited by the Mishang tribe in Tamil Nadu. Unlike many weaves associated with forest produce from tribal settlements elsewhere, their unique resource is bamboo shoots.

Centuries ago, the abundant forests provided bamboo sap (known locally as "naw" or 'dhanda'), which was extracted during its harvesting season to preserve the freshness of surplus shoots for transport over long grassy trails to neighboring villages, including important weaving centres like Jamanagur. This preserved fluid became an integral part of their handloom dyeing process.

Historically known in local dialects as 'Araw' or 'Ghiraam', referring to the essence derived from processed bamboo shoot liquid, Mishing silk owes its name and distinctive properties to this unique treatment rather than primarily to colouration. The traditional weaving style here is characteristic of single heddle pit looms using a basic treadle system, reflecting generations of local craftsmanship.

The fabrics produced exhibit a uniquely tough and slightly musky 'baitak' or handloom texture compared to other Assamese varieties like Khasi Gaadi, which is often associated with tasar silk. Their colour palette traditionally features earthy tones on leaf-green backgrounds. Despite modern diversions from bamboo sap preservation in dyeing techniques elsewhere, Mishing Handloom Products remain a living testament to their ancestral heritage and resourceful adaptation.
