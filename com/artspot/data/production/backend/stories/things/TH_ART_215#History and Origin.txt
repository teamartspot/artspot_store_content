
Sambhal Bone Craft finds its roots around Sambhal Peak near Nainital in Uttarakhand, India. Traditionally practised by the local Bhotiya community, this art form serves as a unique blend of religious ritual and household necessity.

The materials speak to the environment: animal bone, particularly hornbill beaks, water buffalo hooves, and ox femurs (dakshina mudra), alongside sacred stones like rudraksha seeds. These items were abundant in the hilly regions surrounding Sambhal and weren't considered a waste but integral resources.

This craft emerged organically from tribal life which largely lacked formal markets for many everyday objects. Items such as ladles, spoons, combs, and decorative buttons made directly from bone were essential and prized possessions within these communities.

Often associated with shamanistic rituals or used in traditional ceremonies dedicated to nature deities (devi-Devata puja), specific items like rudraksha-adorned buffalo hooves were considered sacred talismans.

For centuries, Sambhal Bone Craft represented an environmentally sound way of life. Tribal artisans found practical use for materials readily available locally – horns shaped from animal products and stones utilized directly from nature's gifts around their homes. It fulfilled a genuine everyday need while holding deep cultural significance. The craft remains rooted in this specific geographical region, preserving its unique character tied to the Bhotiyas' heritage and resourcefulness inherited from generations ago.
