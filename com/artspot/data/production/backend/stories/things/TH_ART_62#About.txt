
Kasuti is a renowned Indian hand embroidery technique originating from the Mysore region in Karnataka. Named after the village Kasutti in this area (though some sources link its earliest flowering to around A.D. 1800), it distinguishes itself through its intricate, often geometric and linear floral patterns executed primarily on sheer fabrics.

The hallmark of Kasuti lies in the use of metallic or golden stranded threads which are run over a heavily waxed cotton ground fabric. This waxy coating prevents the silk warp underneath from showing until specific sections have been embroidered after removing the wax (a process sometimes visible even in modern pieces). The resulting motifs possess an exceptional sheen and shimmer due to these special threads.

Often found on sarees, shararas, suits, and high-end bridal outfits made of fine Chiffon or Crepe de Chine silk, Kasuti panels are typically worn as decorative accents rather than full body coverage. Their beauty has extended their use globally for fashion accessories too.

In the rich tapestry of Indian embroidery styles, particularly those associated with Mysore, Kasuti stands out not just for its technical finesse using gold and mirror work threads, but also for its timeless elegance derived from complex geometric compositions that often echo traditional temple art forms.
