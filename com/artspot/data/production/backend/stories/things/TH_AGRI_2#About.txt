
Originating from India, Alphonso Mango stands as one of the most celebrated and sought-after fruit cultivars globally. Named after a former Indian ruler, it quickly earned its place among mango connoisseurs.

Primarily grown in districts like Ratnagiri, Santragachi (once Princeof Wales station), and Palghat near Madurai in Tamil Nadu, Alphonso distinguishes itself from common varieties not only by its specific name but also by the unique combination of flavor and texture. The sweet-smelling skin offers a deceptive firmness, protecting lush, creamy flesh within.

The true magic is in the pulp itself. It's renowned for its exceptionally rich, buttery taste and unparalleled, almost silk-like smooth, soft slipperiness that releases easily from the seed. The juice is intensely pulpy yet surprisingly non-coating - truly velvet-textured. This distinction sets it apart; lesser-known mangos might offer sweetness but lack this particular depth of flavor or the celebrated, melting mouthfeel of ripe Alphonso.

Often hailed as a 'Royal Mango,' its reputation precedes it. Served fresh as *halwa* during festivals like Diwali across India and in many south-east Asian countries, an unripe green one also represents wealth. The fruit is known for its distinct aroma, rich flavour, unique textural properties (velvety feel) when ripe and the perception that Alphonso mangos have a higher value.

When properly ripened according to standards, usually during April-May in India, often at room temperature away from direct sunlight – avoiding fridge which hardens it unnecessarily slows ripening significantly – Alphonso Mango delivers an almost ethereal eating experience. It’s not just fruit; it's the sensory pleasure derived from one of nature's finer sweet gifts.
