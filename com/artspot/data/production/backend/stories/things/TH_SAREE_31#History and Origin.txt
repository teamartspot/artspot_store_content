
Venkatagiri sarees belong to the state of Andhra Pradesh, originating from a cluster of weaving settlements around thirty kilometres northeast of Tirupati city in Chittoor district.

Their history is deeply intertwined with devotion and temple service. Centuries ago, weavers known as *Vedikas*, primarily dedicated to temple trusts, produced these hand-woven silk creations (usually Pongal variety) for religious and community use near the Venkatagiri hills surrounding Sri Venkateswara Temple.

The characteristic appearance comes from combining a plain-woven cotton base with intricate designs woven entirely on supplementary 'floating' threads called *Handi* or *Hundi*. This unique technique creates raised, textured patterns that resist fading. Often, these handi borders are combined with zari work alongside specific motifs associated with deities like Lord Venkateswara and Goddess Padmakshi.

Today, the distinct look is defined by features including: a plain-woven cotton or silk blend, characteristic *Handi* patterns on one border and often an additional intricate Zari-edged border called *Kuncha*, temple-linked motifs woven into the pallu (end section), and vibrant regional colours like striking yellows or deep reds.
