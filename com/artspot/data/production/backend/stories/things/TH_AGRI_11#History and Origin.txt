
Coorg, a distinct region in the Western Ghats of Karnataka, India, is renowned not just for its coffee or wildlife sanctuaries, but also for one captivating agricultural product: Green Cardamom. Botanically known as *Elettaria cardamomum*, this spice hails from regions similar to Coorg globally, but it was in Coorg that the unique conditions produced what became widely recognized.

Often simply called 'Coorgi' or 'Green Cardamom', its name signifies not just its origin, but also a difference noted much later. Historically cultivated alongside black cardamom (*Amomum subuliforme*), which shared similar geographical origins elsewhere in the region like Mysore and Kerala, Coorg's offering was distinguished by its smaller size and more vibrant green colour when immature.

Its flavour profile remains highly prized, offering a complex interplay of citrusy notes (lemon) with underlying woody undertones. This characteristic sets it apart from black cardamom, which possesses stronger smoky notes, making Green Cardamom essential for lighter dishes and intricate recipes where these distinct nuances need to be preserved.

The appreciation for Coorg's specific quality grew significantly during the British Raj era when its export was favored. Its distinctive appearance (shiny green pods) contrasted sharply with other cardamoms available locally often sold dried, which could appear brownish or black due to drying times and processes.

Today, Green Cardamom remains globally popular, a symbol of Coorg's agricultural prowess. It is cherished for its unique flavour contribution when used fresh within dishes or as a key component in the aromatic blends like masala chai that represent Indian culinary heritage worldwide.
