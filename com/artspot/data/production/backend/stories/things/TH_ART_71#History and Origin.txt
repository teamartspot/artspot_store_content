
Sujini Embroidery originates from India and specifically thrives in states like Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Tamil Nadu. Its name translates directly from Sanskrit as "Embroidered Cushion", signifying its common form as decorative wallhangings or cushion covers created using intricate running stitch work.

Unlike many regional Indian embroideries that utilize a variety of threads, especially metallics and silk often overlaid with gold, Suji embroidery relies heavily on cotton thread in the characteristic three colours: red, yellow, and green bunting. The surface fabric is typically pure hand-spun Khadi.

The fundamental stitches used are two running stitches called Damini work or Patar Jade Tola – known as Patrass Jado. One stitch forms a thick 'karam' line by being worked closer together, while the other creates a dotted ground effect through shorter, looser looping.

This technique is thought to have evolved from appliqué traditions where cut threads were used for decorative purposes before transferring this intricate work onto cloth and even creating new pieces directly on fabric. Suji embroidery flourishes in its use of fine linear patterns, often inspired by Mughl art, nature (flowers, animals), gods, or script.

Characterized by sheer patience and a refined geometric aesthetic woven with vibrant colour through precise control of stitch length and density, Sujini Embroidery distinguishes itself from other intricate textile arts within India.
