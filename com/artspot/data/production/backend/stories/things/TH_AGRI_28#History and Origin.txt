
Coorg Green Cardamom holds a distinct place in the story of spices, particularly within India. Its origins differ significantly from today's primary large-fruited cultivars like Maria Kunju and Mala. Cardamom is believed to have originated further south or west, but its transformation into what we now know as Coorg green (distinct from other green varieties) occurred during the reign of the Kingdom of Coorg itself.

While the 'green' colour can be an external dye in popular commercial blends today, historically referencing Coorg Green often implies a natural, distinct aroma resulting from specific growing conditions. Records suggest that around the mid-18th century, cardamom was likely introduced to Coorg's fertile soil and high altitudes (roughly 700-1800 meters) by early settlers or traders, possibly connected through interactions with neighbouring spice kingdoms like Malabar or Srilanka.

What set it apart? Its natural flavour profile in the pod form seems unique even then. The climate – cool nights at altitude meeting warm days and abundant rainfall – combined with the rich volcanic soil of Coorg's regions (now known as Kodagu), provided ideal conditions for cardamom development long before coffee became famous there.

According to historical references, it was during propagation in these specific microclimates starting around the late 18th or early 19th century that a distinct type began yielding naturally smaller green pods with a particularly robust, fresh aroma and flavour. This intrinsic character became prized locally under the Kanniga royal designation 'Coorg Green'. The lineage suggests it evolved from imported seeds to develop this potent natural profile suited to Coorg's unique environment over generations before commercial expansion in the early 20th century cemented its reputation on a larger scale.
