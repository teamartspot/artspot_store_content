
Assamese Scroll art represents an innovative narrative form emerging from Assam's visual arts traditions. While distinct from ancient dance-drama like the `Kathapusha`, it borrows core principles of storytelling through evolving characters against a static panoramic backdrop.

Originating likely in the early 20th or mid-20th century, its exact beginnings are unclear unlike oral epics such as the Meghadoot poem. However, textual precedents exist; scrolls adapted from `Pawa` folktale narrative poems saw use earlier or contemporaneously. What sets it apart is its unique format: characters typically emerge and move laterally across a long vertical canvas, often depicting Vedic tales previously told through Kathapusha masks. This static development allows intricate scene building.

Artists fashion these scrolls by painting figures onto high-quality cotton fabric stretched taut on a bamboo frame. The cloth can be single-hued for clarity or feature complex gradients with mineral dyes to suggest depth in landscapes.

The format offers significant artistic versatility. Artists control character movements and camera angles, granting freedom absent in shadow puppetry where puppets were bound by their handles. This autonomy allows deeper exploration of themes through detailed illustrations and varied panel composition.
