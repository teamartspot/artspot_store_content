
Pokaran Pottery refers to a significant cluster of prehistoric painted pottery fragments discovered near Pokaran village, located on the outskirts of Jodhpur in Rajasthan, India. The earliest pieces found at this site date back to approximately 2600-1900 BCE (the Harappan phase).

This distinct style offers crucial, albeit fragmentary, insights into the Indus Valley Civilization's presence and characteristics across western Rajasthan. Contrary to many finds from its northern epicentres like Harappa or Mohenjo-daro, Pokaran pottery provides evidence of the civilization extending further east.

The most defining feature is the unique geometric decoration found on slip-coated vessels. Common motifs often included triangles arranged within circles, sometimes filled with small dots or chevrons. These patterns stand out for their sophistication compared to typical representations in northern Indus art. The pots might also bear impressions suggesting they were wheel-made and fired.

While substantial knowledge comes from the initial discovery around 1930-40 during excavations near Gopalgarh fort, most Pokaran pottery discovered later dates back primarily to about 2000 BCE (Harappan Late phase). Its relatively isolated location makes it a fascinating regional variant. The craft likely continued locally or very nearby long after the decline of the major Harappan urban centers.

Today, visiting Pokaran Fort allows glimpses into local history and even sees modern artisans attempting to recreate the distinctive painted geometric styles from this ancient period using similar techniques.
