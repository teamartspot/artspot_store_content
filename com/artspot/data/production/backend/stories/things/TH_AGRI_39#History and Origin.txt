
The Mango Malihabad Deessehari traces its origin quite distinctly back to the Malihabadi region in Indore, Madhya Pradesh. It emerged as an exciting new hybrid around 1938-1940 under the expert guidance of Dr. B.S. Pawar, a noted pomologist then working with the rulers of Indore at Agar Malhi near Sehore.

The initial development likely started as a crossbreeding project aiming to combine desirable traits from popular contemporary varieties like Tommy Atkins or Kent with local strains suited to the region's climate. Its unique characteristics were probably further refined during trials before receiving its official designation, which connects both Malihabad and Dusseheri – the latter perhaps alluding to an origin close to Indore (from Dhar area?) or even a royal preference linked to Dussehra festival origins.

This mango quickly earned recognition for its distinct attributes. The fruit typically presents itself as medium-sized with attractive amber green skin turning orange upon ripening, revealing dense flesh of pale yellow studded with dark red pigments ('blood islands'). Its flavor is praised for being very juicy and exceptionally sweet, offering a rich, creamy sweetness prized by connoisseurs.

Often marketed simply as the 'Malihabad' mango, it possesses those signature qualities that first caught Dr. Pawar's attention decades ago. From its humble Indore origins, this delicious fruit variety has since spread and is celebrated across much of India for its superior taste and texture.
