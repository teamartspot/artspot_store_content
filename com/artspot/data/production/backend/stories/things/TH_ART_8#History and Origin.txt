
Banni Embroidery originates from the Banni region near Anand, Gujarat, India. It belongs to a category known as *Bandhani* or Tie-dye designs executed in gold and silver thread work, characteristic of this area.

These intricate embroideries were historically created by Marwari Jutt merchants for their wives and female servants during significant festive periods like marriages (Nupur ka Vaishak) and Navratri. The primary purpose wasn't ornamentation alone; it served to mark these women with auspicious ritual attire, imbued with protective meanings and cultural significance.

The tradition flourished predominantly in the early to mid-20th century as an ancestral craft passed down through families. Its motifs are inspired by traditional Garb Vastu items – hand-woven fabrics like dhotis (saris), pajamas, cholis (blouses), and ghagrahs (skirts). Animals like Nagin deities, peacocks; geometric shapes such as roopini; temple pillars and architectural details often appear.

Though its popularity waned over recent decades due to modernization impacting the traditional textile base from which it was worked, Banni Embroidery remains a cherished piece of Gujarat's textile heritage. It visually captured stories, beliefs, and daily life concerns within these communities until they sought ancestral craft ways for their own lives in the early 21st century.
It represents not just aesthetic beauty but also the socio-cultural fabric reflected through artisanal legacy from around that period.
