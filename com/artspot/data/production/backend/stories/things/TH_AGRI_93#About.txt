
Marathwada Kesar mango holds a special place in India's diverse fruit basket, often vying for the title of supreme king or queen when pitted against its close relative Alphonso. Originating as a hybrid from cultivators crossbreeding local varieties with the sought-after Bombay Kesari near Nashik and Pune during early cultivation days, this name now signifies exceptional quality itself.

Grown almost exclusively in Maharashtra state's Marathwada region, particularly around Beed district where it reached its peak fame ('Kesargad'), these mangoes are not just delicious; they're often legendary. You'll generally find a Kesar larger than most mangoes, but the size doesn't directly correlate with grandeur or price.

The flesh of Marathwada Kesar is fibrous yet remarkably tender and buttery in texture once you break past it, distinct from the smooth Alphonso feel. Its sweetish-sour aroma immediately draws one closer upon smelling it ripe, as does that fetching purple streak or almost blush coloration on its green skin – a mark of ripeness for this particular kind.

The flavor profile is intense: deep, rich muskmelon sweetness explodes when you bite into the thick, juicy flesh. This unique taste and fragrance set Kesar apart from many other mango cultivars. Its scarcity due to limited cultivation areas makes it feel truly precious in local markets during its prime season. Marathwada Kesar signifies peak eating pleasure for a true connoisseur.
