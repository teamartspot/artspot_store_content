
Udaipur Koftgari stands as a testament to Mewar's artisanal prowess, born within the opulent walls of this Rajput city-state. Its name evokes images of hidden treasures and intricate detailing. Essentially refined silverwork applied onto objects usually cast in gold or other precious metals, Koftgari achieved its zenith during the reigns of Udaipur's magnificent rulers, particularly from the late 17th century onwards.

Artisans utilized traditional techniques like 'Kundan' (inlaying) and 'Meenakari' (glass enameling), though Koftgari is distinct. They built minute silver palaces onto intricate gold designs. The hallmark of true Udaipur Koftgari lies in its minuscule repoussé work – shaping the silver foil by hammering from the reverse side, lifting it millimeter by millimeter into breathtaking miniature patterns that fit seamlessly within gemstone settings or engraved borders.

Historically crafted on brass 'ardha-lingaam' bases due to their ease of soldering tiny hollow pieces (kofts), these were then typically encased in gold. The silver components themselves are not necessarily solid but often feature cut and polished foil, sometimes with hollow filigree accents called 'shikhar'. This meticulous process demanded immense skill for handling the delicate metal.

Today, while preserving some of its extraordinary fineness, Udaipur Koftgari continues as a cherished traditional craft, valued both for heirloom pieces and contemporary adaptations showcasing the legacy of royal Rajput jewellery artistry.
