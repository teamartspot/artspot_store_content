
**Thewa Art**

Emerging as a fascinating subset of woven artistry, Thewa specifically refers to intricate patterns created by weaving split reeds or rushes. This technique goes beyond standard basketry or mat-making; it focuses on the aesthetic and decorative possibilities achieved by manipulating separate strands into complex geometric or pictorial designs.

Historically rooted in Mesopotamia, where such weavings were utilized for insulating walls or wrapping valuable tablets to protect delicate surfaces, Thewa also found prominence across other regions. Its fundamental structure involves weaving distinct pieces of cane – typically half-inch strips called "thweas" - tightly together on frames similar to tapestry looms. Unlike basketry where the strands interleave fully, a key characteristic of Thewa is that adjacent split canes generally cross only once per row or column before returning to their positions.

The resulting pieces often serve functional purposes like mat-making, tray covers, or blinds in furniture upholstery reinforcement contexts, though purely artistic applications also exist. Contemporary artisans draw fresh inspiration from its unique heritage, incorporating modern designs and techniques while preserving this distinctive way of translating lines and shapes directly through the manipulation of flexible vegetal strands to weave textured surface decorations into visually striking wall hangings or sculptures.
