
Nilambur, a town cradled in the Western Ghats of Dakshina Kannada district in Karnataka, holds a special place in the annals of forestry and specifically for one wood: Karanda or Nilam Teak (*Tectona grandis*). Its story is tied to both geographical suitability and historical trade.

The name "Nilambur" itself originates from 'Nila' (mountain) and 'Ambu' (abundance) in local Kannada dialect, hinting at the native terrain's capability for nurturing certain valuable trees. Scientifically identified as Karanda or Nilam Teak, this majestic species thrived perfectly in the Nilambur region due to its specific climate matching tropical conditions – high rainfall, well-defined seasons – and importantly, a rich lateritic soil ideal for its growth.

Nilambur swiftly earned its moniker 'King of Teaks' because it produced exceptionally large-leaved, superior quality teak, much favoured in ancient woodworking. This wood became vital not just locally but across India. Large tracts were developed around the Nilambur town under the Canfor Plantation Company way back before Partition for commercial exploitation.

Historically, teak from Nilambur found its way into shipbuilding and palace construction as far East as Travancore, proving its versatility over vast distances even in ancient times.

However, the story took a significant turn in the mid-19th century when Portuguese missionaries brought the Chestnut Galling Wasp (*Signostegia ceroplastes*), devastating the industry. This led to a drastic decline and pushed Nilambur teak towards near extinction until extensive replanting programs and laterite soil management saved the crown jewel of this region, preserving its legacy for future generations.
