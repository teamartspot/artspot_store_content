
Navara rice's story begins in the rich agricultural heartland of Iran. Originally known as *Parannas* or something similar within its native regions there and Pakistan, it wasn't until Indian rice traders sought better quality grains elsewhere that this distinct fragrant rice captured attention.

The specific name "Basmati" actually didn't originate in India but was an adaptation for export by the trading community when they brought Iranian 'Basmati' (referring to Parannas-style) rice back, likely popularizing the name around Indian ports. Legend has it that upon returning from Iran with this superior-smelling grain, sailors or traders named it Basmati ("good smell") because of its incredibly enticing aroma.

The Indian market showed a strong demand for something better than their common aromatic rices. Enter M K Naik and Babubhai Popatbhai Nanchuwa, Indian rice merchants who recognized the potential. They purchased the original planting material (seeds) directly from Iran in 1986 after testing confirmed its exceptional qualities.

Crucially, they saw advantages in introducing it to India: Iranian regulations required drying for at least two seasons before harvesting, ensuring high dryness and quality. This naturally led to adaptation by Indian breeders; the variety couldn't be replanted directly domestically without modification from original Basmati seeds.

Further development occurred with the Siva Group Rice Mill International Ltd., based in Dombivli near Mumbai. Their hybrid program produced 'Navara' rice (derived from Parannas/Barani). By 1995, this well-adapted variety was introduced into Indian commercial cultivation under its new name. Navara quickly gained popularity for producing a long grained, easily cookable, light-textured white rice with an outstanding fragrance suitable across India's diverse growing regions (distinct from the heavier Baldo often preferred in warmer south).
