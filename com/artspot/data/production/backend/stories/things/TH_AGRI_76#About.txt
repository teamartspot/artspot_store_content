Sindhudurg and Ratnagiri represent distinct yet significant districts in Maharashtra's Western Ghats. Both are characterized by rich, unique cultural heritage intertwined with their agricultural landscape. When exploring these regions or considering natural ingredients from this part of India, one encounters potent geographical references linked to a remarkable product: Kokum.

Kokum originates primarily from the Sawantwadi (Sabarigudi) region in Ratnagiri district and is cultivated around Sindhudurg as well. These districts embrace forested areas crucial for the *Caryophyllata indica* plant, which yields the kokum berry containing essential oil (*gamboa resinol*). The cultivation here ensures high quality.

Kokum holds immense economic value locally, featuring prominently in regional gifting traditions and daily use. It is integral to South Indian cooking, particularly Kerala-style curries for its vibrant yellow color and tangy flavor that cuts through richness without overpowering spice heat. In Ayurveda, it represents the therapeutic power of nature.

Therefore, Sindhudurg & Ratnagiri offer more than scenic beauty; they provide direct access to quality kokum from its native environment.
