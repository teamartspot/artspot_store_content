
Sarees originating from the Santal tribe have a rich heritage rooted in their centuries-old artisan traditions across Eastern India, particularly West Bengal, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Jharkhand, and Kerala (along their migratory paths). As traditionally practiced agriculturists who migrated and settled over time, they developed unique patterns reflecting their environment. The distinctive woven pattern on a plain background often stems from repurposing fabrics like discarded mosquito net coverings due to historical circumstances.

The characteristic long draping style suits the practical lifestyle of its wearers in villages bordering forests or near water sources. Santal women have historically spun, dyed with natural pigments (often derived from plants and minerals yielding vibrant colours), and woven these sarees on traditional backstrap looms at home, making it a spiritual process intertwined with daily life.

Contemporary Santal sarees showcase this heritage beautifully. They feature striking multicolour combinations achieved through handloom weaving with varying shades of black silk against colourful cotton fabric (ochre, green, red). Traditional motifs include 'Bonderi' depicting nature and animals ('Gangadhara' and other Bonderiyas representing Pitha mythological stories), and intricate border designs like the famous 'Katariya', featuring hunting scenes or symbolic depictions of social interactions.

Silk sarees from areas near Palam in Ranchi's Ramban district are particularly renowned, offering luxurious alternatives while preserving tribal motifs. Today, Santal handloom sarees stand as a vibrant symbol of indigenous artistry and cultural identity across India.
