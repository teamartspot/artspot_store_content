
Darjeeling tea stands apart, primarily renowned for its unique geographical origin within the Darjeeling district of West Bengal, India. Cultivated at high altitudes over 75 years, it has earned its place among the world's most celebrated premium teas. Its fame rests heavily on its distinctive flavor profile.

When you sip Darjeeling tea, whether the classic First Flush or later harvests like Lung-chuan, expect complexity from the start. There are subtle notes of muscatel – a hint of raisin sweetness – alongside characteristic briskness and astringency that dance delightfully across your palate. Often described as nutty or smoky when fully oxidized (like Orange Pekoe), it can exhibit fresh vegetal flavors with citrus undertones.

The most famous regional name is "Darjeeling". Even the elevation of production plays its part; teas from high peaks like Mahananda are considered top-tier due to unique terroir conditions yielding a lighter liquor, fine downy tips, and ethereal flavor. Darjeeling tea has designated Geographical Indication status, guaranteeing authenticity for those connoisseurs seeking specific origins.

Beyond the taste, its history is woven into luxury itself. It was gifted as tribute from colonial gardens to esteemed British figures like Queen Victoria, solidifying its legendary status over time, and remains a coveted treasure today.
