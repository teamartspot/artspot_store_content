
Gir Kesar Mango, also known as Hoshangabad Kesar or Ratnagiri Kesar, holds deep historical roots in India, specifically within the ancient princely state of Gaushala (often included under Gir). Originating from this region centuries ago during the reign of various Rajput and Maratha rulers in Gujarat, it's believed to have been a prized possession exchanged between nobles as wedding gifts or royal insignia. The name "Kesar" itself translates to 'king' in Sanskrit or Hindi, reflecting its esteemed status.

Grown primarily near Hoshangabad (Dhar district) and Ratnagiri since the early 19th century if not earlier, it gained prominence during British colonial times through the princely courts. The lineage remained relatively confined due to a declared ban on exporting seedlings beyond these specific royal lands owned by various families.

Its origin story connects it to Hoshangabad Palace and is also famously associated with Rajpipla near Ratnagiri, from where alternative tales claim its blessings. While not officially originating solely from Asangaon or the Godavari basin like some legends suggest (though quality may have been noted there), variations highlight its enduring link.

Today, Gir Kesar enjoys Protected Geographical Indicator variety status in India, meaning only specific regions under certain lineage approvals can label their mangos as such. Its deep history continues to enrich its legacy, solidifying it as a true icon from Gujarat's royal past.
