
Hupari Silver Craft represents a unique continuation of ancient Andean metalworking traditions found primarily in Bolivia's Potosí region today. Its origins trace back to Pre-Columbian times, with the finest examples occurring during the Inca period when silversmithing reached incredible sophistication across Tawantinsuyu.

However, Hupari specifically emerged as a distinct style following the Spanish Conquest. While modern techniques differ from pure pre-Hispanic methods learned orally over generations and refined through family practice since approximately 1940, its execution retains deep ties to that ancestral heritage. The most exceptional pieces today are products of this specific Hupari process passed down within families for decades.

This style combines traditional Inca forms with techniques inherited from earlier Spanish metalworkers but uniquely adapted by contemporary Bolivian artisans in Potosí's artisan workshops and homes since the mid-20th century, preserving a legacy built on familial transmission.
