
Sanganeri Hand Block Printing is an ancient and renowned technique originating from the town of Sanganer in Rajasthan, India. It represents a refined form of textile artistry deeply rooted in local tradition.

Distinguished by its intricate patterns created using multiple colours applied onto fine cotton fabric, this method features geometrically bold borders framing complex central medallions. These designs often echo Rajasthani miniature painting motifs found on the painted 'Madra' cloth traditionally used as bedspreads or banners. A unique characteristic of Sanganeri is the use of natural dyes derived from plants, roots, and other organic sources.

The process itself involves manual block printing where artisans carve complex patterns onto smooth wooden blocks using the Chakrapurri style – a distinctive technique allowing for multi-directional carving. After applying one colour layer per block impression with precise alignment before building up subsequent colours over multiple resist lines, each item typically requires numerous passes through the printing frame.

The craft continues to thrive in Sanganer workshops today, even amidst mechanized production elsewhere. Its appeal lies not only in visually stunning craftsmanship but also in its commitment to natural processes and deep cultural symbolism, echoing local architectural details and devotional imagery from temples ('Deodhar' patterns). It stands as a vibrant example of India's rich textile heritage.
