
Kerala's mural paintings stand as living testaments to centuries of artistic devotion, particularly flourishing in the erstwhile Chera kingdom during the 10th-12th centuries, though their roots trace back even further. These intricate narrative artworks primarily adorn village temples (sreevillams), serving not just decorative purposes but vital pedagogical functions, visually conveying complex religious stories and deities to a largely illiterate populace.

Known for their unique style – predominantly monochrome in cool shades like blue, green, black, or ochre, meticulously outlined in gold along sacred narratives depicted with remarkable detail. They employ palm-leaf scripts derived from Sanskrit Agamas, adapted into distinct compositions often seen 'en plein air' (outdoors) to benefit the villagers.

Eruvelli, near Trissur, functions as a primary hub and gallery for modern preservation efforts. Here, under government patronage, works by renowned living masters like M.S. Sanjivakumar create an immersive exhibition environment tracing Kerala's mural evolution from ancient temple paintings back through regional dynasties to the present day.

Beyond its walls depicting Ramayana and Mahabharata epic scenes or the life stories of revered figures like Ayya Vaikundaswaroopan, the collection includes secular pieces illustrating royal processions. The art profoundly engages visitors in Kerala's living history, spirituality, social fabric, connecting contemporary audiences to an uninterrupted tradition celebrated globally for its distinctive aesthetic and historical significance within Indian art forms.
