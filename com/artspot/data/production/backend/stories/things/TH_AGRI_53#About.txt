
Eggplants found in Udupi's vibrant vegetable market aren't just any brinjal; they carry the distinctive hallmark of 'Mattu Gulla'. Literally translating, "Matte" refers to cows (lit. cow), and "Gulla" signifies a creamy-white yogurt preparation. This tradition involves massaging unblemished eggplants in fresh, homemade yogurt for several hours before cooking, typically stir-frying them until they become tender.

This practice imparts a unique character to the Udupi brinjal. While still technically an eggplant, it retains its vibrant green hue much longer than usual and possesses an exceptionally smooth, almost silken texture when cooked. The flavor is distinct too – retaining some of the yogurt's tanginess but transforming into a milder, creamier profile compared to other regional varieties known for sharpness or bitter notes.

Often described simply as 'Udupi brinjal', this specific type finds its place in various local dishes, but its simplest and most popular form is the large, charred eggplant sold raw at stalls like the famous Mangaluru Railway Station *Chinnarangeshwara* vegetable market. These are prized for their tenderness upon cooking and characteristic creaminess.

This method also suggests an older agricultural link; local farmers who sell produce often prepare it before reaching vendors. The 'Mattu Gulla' brinjal represents not just a culinary preference but deeply ingrained farmer practices, setting its taste profile sharply apart from more common eggplants elsewhere in India or globally, and earning well-deserved popularity among food connoisseurs.
