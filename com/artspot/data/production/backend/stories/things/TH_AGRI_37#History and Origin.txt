
Virupaksha Hill Banana traces its origin to the volcanic soils of Mysore, in southern India. Its legacy begins with an interesting link to Hindu mythology associated with Virupaksha, one of Shiva's consorts from Valmiki's Ramayana, though its botanical story differs significantly.

The name itself points towards a belief system – many such hill groves were near temples or settlements dedicated to gods like Virapaksha. These old groves likely provided sustenance and nourishment for nearby communities, suggesting the variety was naturally suited to local conditions and recognized for distinct flavour compared to other ancient cultivars introduced elsewhere.

This specific type of banana required relatively rich soil (often volcanic) to give it its characteristic texture and flavour. Originating from regions near temples known for Virupaksha long ago, it later became an established commercial cultivar grown in areas suitable for the climate found around those hills, primarily across southern India as well as Kerala and coastal Andhra Pradesh.

Today, "Virupaksha Hill Banana" evokes this historical link while also representing popular, sweet local cultivations. Its seeds likely spread from these traditional Mysorian groves into other suitable areas in India due to favourable conditions (rich soil, climate). Though perhaps less common now than its close relatives like the 'Red Spanish,' locally grown Virupaksha Hill Bananas are still valued treats originating directly from specific, rich-growing regions.
