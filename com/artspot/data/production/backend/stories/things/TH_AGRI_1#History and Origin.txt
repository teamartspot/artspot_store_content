
The story of Darjeeling tea truly began in earnest during the mid-19th century, though its presence is ancient. Legend recounts that Jungshab Lam, a Bhutia landowner overseeing British surveyors searching for mineral springs at Kalimpong highlands near Nepal's border, noticed their cool cups improving after receiving freshly picked leaves from neighboring Santals. This serendipitous encounter in Darjeeling led to trials, and soon the unique conditions of the Himalayan foothills – misty slopes nourished by mountain air and unique soils derived from glacial till – produced exceptionally fine tea.

Initially cultivated under the guidance of British planters like Richard Sharp and his son-in-law Thomas Gordon, who introduced specific Camellia sinensis (China) varieties suitable for brewing rather than smoking, Darjeeling eventually secured its own production. Its journey was marked by trade; early shipments faced stiff competition from nearby Sikkim teas until a pivotal meeting between an Indian representative (Chandra Kumar Haygarth) and Scottish buyer Andrew Ure refined quality standards, forever earning it the title 'Darjeeling Orange Pekoe'. This distinction cemented its reputation as India's preeminent tea region.

Today, Darjeeling remains celebrated worldwide for its distinct nutty, flowery aroma ('Muscatel') and complex flavour. Its rich history, association with misty mountains, monsoon rains, British colonial planting, and eventual global acclaim position it uniquely among mints and blends across the globe; indeed, 'Darjeeling Orange Pekoe' is a mark of high quality enjoyed far beyond India's borders.
