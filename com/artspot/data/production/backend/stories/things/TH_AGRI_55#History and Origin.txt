
Before it achieved its present splendor, Madurai held a different appellation – one deeply rooted in history. Once known as 'Madurai Kallar' or 'Madurai Karaiyilgal', the name was later shortened and adapted.

The most crucial historical shift occurred when local rulers, originally part of the Chola dynasty, took up the title 'Malligaraiyilgal'. They transformed this into 'Madurai Malli', meaning 'the people of Madurai' or signifying royal status. This new designation became firmly entrenched under Emperor Kulottunga III in 1209 AD, who significantly expanded and fortified their power before later reverting the name back to Pandianar.

For centuries following this period, the family defending Madurai Malli successfully resisted empires, notably defeating the Delhi Sultanate's forces. Their effective management of resources and defensibility ensured the dynasty's survival until 1697 AD against Marathas.

Under colonial rule during the mid-18th century (from the Dutch initially), they also cultivated a reputation as one of India's wealthiest trading families, even as Madurai itself served strategic purposes for the Portuguese East India Company and other European powers.

Thus, while they now call themselves Pandianar, 'Madurai Malli' remains a potent, shorthand reference to their ancient origins, enduring legacy, and the formidable dynasty that once protected their capital city.
