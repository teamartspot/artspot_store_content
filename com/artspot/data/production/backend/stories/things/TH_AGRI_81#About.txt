
Originating from the agricultural heartland of Maharashtra, Sangli Raisins stand out as a celebrated regional specialty and darling of Indian sweets culture. These golden or dark purple dried grapes are not just any raisins; they're deeply cherished for their exceptional texture and flavour.

Sangli's unique climate – warm sunshine tempered by coastal breezes – plays a crucial role in the grape varieties grown there, particularly Thompson Seedless and Cardinal grapes, renowned for wine production but equally prized for making high-quality homegrown raisins. This specific terroir significantly enhances their characteristics compared to many other Indian regions.

The process is labor-intensive yet skillful. Fingers of sunlight dry the grapes meticulously on straw mats spread out in open-air barns called *Kotharakhed*. This careful, slow curing transforms them from fresh sops into plump, wrinkly perfection with a concentrated natural sweetness and a hint of caramelization that defines this sweet variety.

Often featured as fillings in elaborate traditional sweets like ladoo or sheera, their rich flavour adds depth. Sometimes sprinkled dry on doughnuts (ghevar) or biscuits, they offer bursts of familiar, fruity goodness anywhere. Their quality is widely recognized in the local cuisine and markets, particularly across Western India including Mumbai and Pune.
