
Kishu (金柑) lemon seems an odd name for a citrus fruit, but this variety's story begins much like its sweet taste unfolds – uniquely from Japan.

Discovered by chance near Nagano in 1927, Kishu lemons are the accidental offspring of an Okazaki citron ("miyashige") discovered near Takayama City. Unlike intentional hybrids developed over centuries to combine traits, this particular lemon tree produced unusually sweet fruit despite its lineage from more bitter parents.

The origin is a key part of its appeal. While other small, tangy lemons existed beforehand (like the Fino in Sicily), Kishai's distinctively rich flavor emerged unexpectedly from these large, supposedly bland citrons found growing alongside a main variety, Yubinno Lemon (ユビイチノン). Its lineage explains much about its characteristics – potentially inheriting sweetness even while retaining some of its parent's larger size and reduced acidity.

Primarily valued for its exceptional fruit quality, cultivators initially focused on preserving the original tree. These efforts yielded a remarkable harvest in 1940, leading to broader recognition beyond just propagating from that single source. Unlike mandarins which spread rapidly due to low shipping costs, Kishai's initial value lay in showcasing the unique potential of its specific origin.

Today, cultivated mainly around Takayama and other Nagano areas – a region known for mountain climate conditions ideal for quality fruit development. Though not vast on quantity compared to commercial lemons or mandarins, each piece plays a significant role as both decorative planting material (lemon trees are admired ornamentals) and most importantly, source of those famously sweet candied peel snacks enjoyed by travelers to Japan.
