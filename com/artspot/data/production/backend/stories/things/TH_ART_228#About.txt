
Ahmedabad Sodagari block print stands as a testament to Gujarat's vibrant textile heritage and the enduring creativity of its artisans. This distinct form of *bandhani* (tie-dye) influenced resist printing, characterized by intricate patterns chiseled onto wooden blocks, typically carved from roti (flat bread), creating fine detail impossible with larger blocks.

Emerging in Ahmedabad during British colonial times for festive use among the Parsi community – particularly weddings and Navjot celebrations (*Sodagi* being an auspicious occasion) – it initially used indigo dye. Today, artisans employ a wider palette, including natural colours derived from plants and minerals. Motifs remain inspired by traditional Indian designs but often boast geometric precision and sometimes larger flowers than its Parsi predecessor.

The fabric of choice is usually hand-spun cotton (Khadi or *Majeri*), which beautifully absorbs the earth-tone dyes prevalent at Sodagari workshops, resulting in striking colour-fast patterns. Despite the rise of power looms during industrialization, these artisan blocks have successfully adapted, offering a unique patterned alternative with enduring appeal in the modern market.

Each piece is hand-blocked and intricate, reflecting immense skill and patience. Discovering Ahmedabad's Sodagari prints means uncovering a narrative woven into fabric – one that connects traditional techniques like *bandhani*, the artistry of carving blocks from simple materials, to contemporary appreciation for authentic handicrafts.
