
Kutch Bandhani is a unique form of traditional Indian tie-dye originating from the vast, arid Kutch district in Gujarat, specifically practiced around Bhuj since the 17th century.

Unlike other regional bandhani styles that typically apply paste after folding or knotting fabric, Kutch artisans utilize intricate geometric and linear motifs created *before* even dipping their cloth into the dye vat. This involves fixing specific areas of the unstretched fabric with dots known as Bandhni marks, while leaving ample space for elaborate free-knot patterns characteristic of the area. These distinctive motifs are what define the visual language of Kutch crafts.

Often distinguished by a 'Maroda' border – named after its association with Khushbu Marodia, though this can vary regionally or seasonally in terms of complexity and theme – Kutch Bandhani is traditionally used during Navratri. This significant occasion sees women wearing numerous pleated saris adorned with complex *bandhni* motifs representing divine figures or celestial patterns, alongside multi-kolis (multi-coloured embroidered mirrors), a iconic Kutch accessory.

Its rich history is inextricably linked to the cultural practices and beliefs of Gujarat regarding celestial bodies. Kutch Bandhani continues its ancient legacy today as a vibrant, colourful art form deeply rooted in community identity and celebrated celebrations within the region.
