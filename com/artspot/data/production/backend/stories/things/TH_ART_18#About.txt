
Chamba Rumal, a vibrant form of hand-embroidered textile art, originated in the villages surrounding the ancient city of Chamba near Amritsar during the latter half of the 19th century, particularly in Jalandhar and Nawanshahr. Initially known as 'Chandigarh Embroidery' (though often mistakenly attributed to Chandigarh itself), its name changed with its recognition centering on the craftsmanship from these specific regions before being formalized under Chamba.

Artisans primarily from rural women's communities developed this intricate style, working predominantly with kantha fabric (a coarse cotton weave). The characteristic involves applying multiple layers of brightly pigmented cotton threads onto a background often created using vegetable dyes. While the embroidery features complex patterns requiring great skill and precision, true artisans eschewed wholesale copying or mass production initially – focusing on authenticity over sheer quantity.

Known for its stunning floral motifs, geometric patterns, fruiting branches, and scenes from Indian mythology woven in vibrant colours redolent of Mughlai artistry, Chamba Rumal creates exquisite garments such as salwar kameezes covering the lower torso (choli), tunics, skirts, dupattas, stoles, scarves, phulkari handkerchiefs, and even sophisticated wall hangings. This unique form has provided livelihoods and empowerment for generations of women weavers and embroiderers in these communities.
