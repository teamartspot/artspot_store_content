
The origin of Mizo Chilli, particularly the renowned Uechang (Oi-chaang), is deeply intertwined with history starting centuries ago in present-day Manipur's neighbouring regions or perhaps pre-dating Portuguese contact. Its story truly began after a significant wave of people, often called "Lushei" migration from North Burma around 150 years ago.

The journey started when these migrants introduced their own native chilies alongside new agricultural techniques and trade goods. These sturdy plants were well-suited to the Mizo hills' generally warmer climate, unlike some highly prized culinary chilies found in colder regions. The Portuguese missionaries who had been active in Assam earlier are widely credited with bringing chili seeds or plants across North-east India into areas like Manipur, from where significant amounts later reached Mizoram.

Once established, Uechang became a cornerstone of Mizo cuisine, valued not just for its heat but critically for its high-quality red colour. It quickly adapted to local culinary needs, forming the basis for many dishes' color and spice profiles. Unlike other chilies imported during that period (which couldn't thrive or had spicier flavour profiles unsuited locally), Uechang's milder, vibrant character uniquely integrated into Mizo life, securing its place not just as a condiment but as an agricultural staple, celebrated even today for its distinct sweet-spice alongside heat and rich colouring.
