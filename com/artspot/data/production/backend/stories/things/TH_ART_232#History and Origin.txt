
Pilkhuwa Hand Block Print Textile emerged primarily in two Eastern Indian states – Bihar and Odisha. Though the term 'Pilkhuwa' traces its historical roots to the Bodo communities residing near Pilkhuwa village, situated on the foothills of the Ararli Hills between Ranchi and Simdega in Jharkhand.

Known for their unique style of hand block printing, especially using giant onion-shaped wooden blocks held at one end, these artisans created exquisite motifs primarily with a Prussian blue pigment. This characteristic deep blue gives Pilkhuwa prints their distinctive look. Often referred to as Bihar or Madhubani prints in the broader market today, they share common ground but are distinct from the Madhubani art practiced near Bhagalpur.

The famous 'Jharu' saree, traditionally produced by tribals in Jharkhand and later incorporated into mainstream Indian fabrics, owes much of its rich pattern to Pilkhuwa block printing. The tradition involves artists painting intricate, nature-inspired border designs onto the entire silk or woollen cloth at once, moving with their blocks as they dip the fabric in dye.

More than just a craft, it's considered an ancient way of preserving fabrics through traditional artistry and colouring techniques across generations, holding significant cultural value.
