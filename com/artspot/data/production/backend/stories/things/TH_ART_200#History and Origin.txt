
Kutch Rogan is a traditional style of hand-drawn carpet originating from the Kutch district of Gujarat, India. It holds historical significance dating back centuries to a period when Mughal cultural influence permeated the region following periods of Rajasthani rule.

Geographically distinct from its royal counterparts often found in palaces across northern and central India (like those crafted at Jaipur or Jodhpur), Kutch Rogan emerged from the vibrant, borderless artistic traditions more freely practised by common artisans in this unique cultural landscape. Historically, while Gujarat had a rich carpet legacy tied to rulers like Rani Rudambaje of Dholka during the 16th century and later patronized under Rajput and Maratha kings, the specific 'Rogan' style was developed within the Kutch clusters themselves.

While exhibiting stylistic affinities with certain Rajasthani schools such as Bagh or Ratanpur, distinct characteristics solidified locally. Crucially, Kutch Rogan is identified by its unique large format block-printing technique on silk (Bavarpatti), though historically weaving itself was the base. The name 'Rogan' likely stems from a local language meaning dark patterned borders or stripes.

Its origin lies in utilitarian and decorative village life, evolving over time under various princely rulers who adopted Persian carpet-making methods locally. The craft represents more than just textiles; it embodies the shared history, artistic evolution, and vibrant present of Kutch through storytelling, tribal motifs, and bold geometric designs woven into enduring patterns on resilient wicker.
