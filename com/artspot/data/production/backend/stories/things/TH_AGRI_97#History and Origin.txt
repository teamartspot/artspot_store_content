
The story of Tulapani rice begins far beyond its Peruvian name; it is often called 'Jungle Rice' due to its resilient nature and origins in challenging Andean conditions. Its journey started with native grains, perhaps the now-extinct *Oryza calidurna*, which were cultivated for sustenance without commercial focus.

Commercial viability truly began when scientists recognized this pre-Hispanic quinoa-like grain's potential as a rice alternative. It possessed remarkable hardiness – thriving in cooler temperatures and shallow soils unsuitable for traditional Asian rice cultivation.

With technical support from the German Foundation *Gesellschaft für technische Zusammenarbeit* (GTZ), Peru undertook development. A key variety, which now forms much of what we know as Tulapani, was selected for its stable yield characteristics across varied altitudes and climates.

This new grain needed marketable names, leading to the ProTulapani brand's creation in co-operation with US rice marketer ConAgra around the mid-1960s. Initially promoted alongside other Andean pseudo-cereals like quinoa or amaranth using catchy jingles highlighting nutrition and resilience.

Its arrival met the growing demand for affordable, nutritious food options following regional economic challenges and political changes in the 1940s-70s – a period where many looked to local solutions. While facing initial marketing hurdles as its flavour differed from white rice, developed resistance by farmers helped secure contracts.

Today, Tulapani is Peru's leading amaranth variety within the ProTulapani brand umbrella, cultivated commercially in regions like Ayacucho and Apurímac at high altitude altitudes usually unsuitable for standard open-field rice growing.
