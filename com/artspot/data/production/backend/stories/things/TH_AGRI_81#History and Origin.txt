
Dried grapes, commonly known as raisins or naturally sweetened currants today, trace their organized history to Maharashtra's Sangli district. While the basic art of preserving fruits through sun drying is ancient across civilizations, the distinct cluster called 'Sangri' emerged specifically in this region.

The origin story often centers around a simple discovery: during hot weather, when clusters were hung out for processing other crops like sesame or cotton, the native grapes dried exceptionally well without needing additional help agents (like sulfur dioxide used on some commercial currants). Initially produced in larger quantities by local farmers and cottage industries during certain seasons to take advantage of the weather, this Sangli cluster gained recognition not just locally but across India for its natural quality. It was marketed effectively under the general term 'Sangri' or simply dried grapes from that region.

The history involves generations employing traditional methods – spreading thin grape clusters ('Kadals') and rolling them on long wooden presses with sand, traditionally by hand in basements (known as Saindhavar Chambalas), possibly to impart texture. This focus consolidated Sangli's reputation for quality natural dried grapes.

In essence, Sangli Raisins/Sangri owe their history not just to climate suitability but also to a tradition of focused production and perhaps earlier, word-of-mouth marketing highlighting superior fruit when left naturally sun-dried in clusters during the heat season.
