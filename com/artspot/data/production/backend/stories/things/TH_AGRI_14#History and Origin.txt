
Nestled within the scenic valleys and protective foothills of the northern Himalayas in present-day Himachal Pradesh, India, lies the historic Kangra district, renowned for its distinct tea regions: Saloon ki Serai near Jogindranagar (formerly Shillonggarh), and particularly Lower Kandi. While less talked about in mainstream global tea narratives compared to Assam or Darjeeling, Kangra Tea boasts a rich history deeply interwoven with the colonial exploration of India's potential as a source for high-quality black tea.

The story truly began in earnest during the mid-19th century, shortly after the initial clearing of these lands for forestry and early agricultural attempts. Forward-thinking British tea planters recognized the untapped promise there. The challenging terrain, cool nights at altitude combined with uniquely warm days (unlike those directly above Darjeeling), provided an ideal climate for cultivating plucky fresh leaf tea – a higher-grade standard.

This specific microclimate fostered development of teas possessing distinct characteristics: they often exhibit bright, brisk notes, perhaps reminiscent of summer flushes. Early Kangra teas were known for their clean appearance and unique 'crispness' or subtle citrus undertone that set them apart from other emerging Indian varieties of the time.

Over decades following the first plantings around 1850-1860, Kangra producers focused on refining techniques to harness this natural potential. The region's tea houses today continue the tradition begun centuries ago, ensuring quality and character reminiscent of their predecessors' dedication to bringing the unique terroir beauty of these Himalayan valleys into cups worldwide.
