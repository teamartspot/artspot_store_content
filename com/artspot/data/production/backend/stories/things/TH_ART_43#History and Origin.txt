
The origins of the Kurumba people are thought to lie in ancient India's Deccan Plateau region, likely migrating over centuries towards the Western Ghats forests bordering Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. They entered historical consciousness later than other Dravidian groups like the Kodavas (Coorgs), remaining largely forest-dwellers throughout their history.

For a long period, they maintained an independent existence in these territories. Historically isolated from broader administrative structures despite their strategic locations near regions with centralized authority in places like Kodagu/Kodava Nadu and parts of Kerala. Their social organization was traditionally centered around the *Hamar* or lineage system rather than village norms prevalent elsewhere.

Kurumba society is often characterized as matrilineal, particularly evident through its oral traditions about land inheritance from mothers or sisters even into adulthood before transferring to their male offspring upon marriage to an outsider (typically a Kadava).

Centuries of shifting boundaries and political systems saw waves of integration with surrounding communities. Initially distinct, over time many intermarried with the Kaduvas (Kodavas), Gadiyas, Kanikaras, Dodasars, etc., especially during periods of upheaval or expansion. This led to their assimilation into various agricultural/sedentary castes while simultaneously absorbing aspects of new economies and social structures.

Today, while few retain entirely traditional forest-based lifestyles in the original sense, some communities preserved elements like their unique marriage customs (sister exchange until recent times) and rich musical traditions exist among them. Their history reflects resilience amidst complex interactions with neighbors over a span often referred back to ancient settlement or identity formation precursors on these lands.
