
Originating from ancient China during Dynasty, block printing stands as a testament to human ingenuity in textile design. This intricate handcraft involves creating patterns by carving them onto blocks of wood or other material – traditionally just linoleum but also stone has been historically used too. Once the pattern is carved into the base layer (the "bloc" itself), it, along with some paste and filler ink or colour paste depending on what colours are to be used later when printing multiple areas.

This solid block becomes a template. When dampened, usually slightly watered rice starch paste or other prepared resist medium was historically applied through sections of the carved block using burnouses – long pieces of fabric – thus transferring intricate resist patterns onto cloth fibers batch by batch on the loom floor before dyes were applied over top.

Historically linked to techniques like Batik and Tie-Dye, block printing forms the foundation for many resist-dye processes across Asia. The meticulous nature requires patience and skill; every incision must be precise where lines between different colours meet or if one color is being covered by a monochrome design then even negative space in an all-over pattern can't have errors. When done correctly it produces deep, rich fabric colors alongside beautiful woodcut style images directly onto the cloth surface itself.

In today's era of mass production and digitization, there remains unique power and beauty in this traditional way to achieve textile coloration and decoration – not just aesthetically, but also culturally and economically for those communities still actively practicing it.
