
The origin of Phulkari Sarees is deeply intertwined with Punjabi history itself. This intricate art form began as royal court dressmaking in the 18th century during Maharaja Ranjit Singh's empire, utilizing luxurious silk fabrics imported from places like China (sobha). These sarees were highly elaborate and worn by royalty.

However, the craft evolved significantly for broader accessibility in rural Punjab, particularly around Amritsar. Sarees on less costly handloom cloth with gold or silver zari thread became popular as ceremonial attire (like Shalwar Kameez) and wedding dresses from approximately 1850 onwards.

The name 'Phulkari' itself reveals its essence - 'phool' meaning flower, referring to the flower motifs worked in 'kaari', traditional Punjabi gold or silver zari thread. The 'shadows', another characteristic element often linked with warriors of misls (regional militias), are silhouettes resembling hunting outfits.

A truly original piece is called Chikani miri, where the entire design is meticulously woven from scratch onto a plain koladee cloth using intricate chikan-style silk threads and fine gold zari needles. This traditional handloom approach showcases immense dexterity. Phulkari thus transitioned from royal luxury to a cherished craft representing Punjabi identity, embroidered with symbols – paisley motifs often denote love or blessings received – transforming homespun fabric into vibrant cultural narratives.
