
Uttarakhand Likhai finds its roots deep within the ancient heartlands of northern India, specifically emerging organically from the cultural and artistic traditions of Almora in the Kumaon region. "Likhop", meaning wood carving or sculpting, forms part of its name.

This traditional craft is intrinsically linked to the Hindu goddess Lakshmi Narain (Lakshmindra), who holds significant sway over prosperity and good fortune among the local Garhwal community in Uttar Pradesh, India. Historically practised in dedicated artisan workshops primarily for creating exquisite deities around home altars ("kalash"), Likhai evolved into a respected independent art form valued for its intricacy.

Like other regional sculptural traditions prevalent during temple building phases centuries ago, Uttarakhand wood carving utilizes traditional tools such as hand-carved wooden mallets and stone chisels. While the practice saw transformation in institutions like Shishu Vidya Saram in Almora starting from the mid-20th century, preserving its distinct identity and ancient techniques is central today.

Often executed by artisans from communities including Gurjars, Bhotiyals, Pahadi, Jhawalis, Kumaoni, and Garwali Hindus – frequently involving generations of shared knowledge within families – Uttarakhand Likhai serves as a tangible link to the region's enduring artistic heritage and spiritual beliefs.
