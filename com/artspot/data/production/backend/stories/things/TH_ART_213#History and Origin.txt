
The history and origin of Banaras Shehnai is deeply connected to its evolution from a Persian shawm into distinct classical form under the unique cultural umbrellas of Varanasi.

While instruments similar to shehnai existed in Persia since ancient times, shaping early nagars (ancestor shehnais), their journey began significantly within India during the mid-18th century. Ustad Bahadur Ali Shah brought a particular type of shawm from outside or adapted an existing one and settled near Varanasi (then known as Benares). There, his descendants nurtured it among the rich tapestry of Hindustani classical music emerging in Upper India.

Unlike Deccani shehnais which often focus on dhrupad and integrate Marthandavar melodies, the Banaras Shehnai was shaped by its surrounding during a period when court music heavily favoured vocal renditions. This environment pushed the shehnai towards becoming an independent melodic instrument suited for khayal compositions, mirroring nuances previously found solely in vocals.

Over centuries, Varanasi artisans created instruments with superior craftsmanship – longer bore (shankh), intricately carved flanges (mandpuri). Musicians refined its repertoire and expressive capabilities. The Banaras Shehnai thus stands as a unique classical instrument born from Mughal musical legacies, evolving specifically within the lyrical musical context of Varanasi to become an indispensable part of Hindustani vocal music and Bharatanatyam dance accompaniment today. Its history is one of adaptation, innovation, and deep-rooted artistic expression in 'The City of God'.
