
Considered as a sacred form of devotional writing, Majuli Manuscript Painting is an indigenous art style flourishing today primarily on the island of Majuli. However, its roots trace back centuries and geographically link it to ancient Mithila traditions prevalent across Assam's various communities.

The craft traditionally involves meticulously hand-drawing religious texts onto prepared Palm Leaf ('Kadamba' or 'Pothigam'), using natural colours derived from forest products such as turmeric, soot, lac dyes. These vibrant illustrations bring Hindu deities like Sri Krishna and Radha to life with great tenderness in Vaishnavite contexts, or portray Lakshmi, Saraswati, Hanuman for devotional practice.

Often associated with the prestigious Mithila Puruskar since its inception by renowned artist Thang Miya Devi Khowa, the art evolved on Majuli. The socio-geophysical challenges from river erosion provided catalysts; practitioners adopted existing Madhubani techniques prevalent along the Brahmaputra's tributaries but adapted them onto their island terrain while preserving profound spiritual significance.

Manuscripts are central for family worship during festivals like Diwali and Bonalu in temples, creating a unique repository of culture intertwined with religious practices. Masterfully executed by both men and women over days or weeks, these paintings offer insights into mythology, daily life, ritualistic scenes, yet remain deeply rooted in spiritual devotion. They stand as beautiful testimony to Assamese cultural resilience and artistic innovation through millennia.
