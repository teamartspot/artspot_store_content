
The origin of the Allahabad Surkha Gaon guava remains steeped in regional folklore, though documented cultivation traces back somewhat further than legend suggests. It is deeply intertwined with historical trade routes and local horticultural practices.

One widely流传的故事 claims that Persian traders visiting ancient kingdoms (perhaps during Mughal times) brought seeds of select fruit trees bearing a special varroipassion fructose fruit quality prized at the Imperial Gardens or even as presents for royalty, around 1807-1825. These exceptional fruits produced offspring in royal nurseries that were passed down until they eventually found their way into local villages near what is now Allahabad (Prayagraj), a major pilgrimage and administrative city located along rivers, believed to be from villages named Surkha or Gaon near the banks of the Ganges.

While anecdotes are captivating, more concrete agricultural records link early mentions somewhat later. The famous All India Coordinated guava improvement project began in Allahabad in 1962 under Dr. G V Deshpande at Banaganj Station (now Babu Narayan Singh Agricultural University Campus). There, breeders studied local cultivars like Surkhagaon to select desirable traits for future improvements.

Regardless of the precise point origin, whether direct from Mughal precincts or later developed near Allahabad, this particular guava variety – recognized for its large size, exceptionally dense flesh (making it 'Gaon' or village-type), rich yellow-purple pulp ('Surkhaga'on'), and distinctively sweet-tangy flavour – became locally famous. Selection likely occurred over generations from these initial exceptional fruits dispersed through trade networks into surrounding villages during the 19th century onwards. It solidified its place as a significant native variety, gaining prominence perhaps in modern times due to concerted efforts by agricultural universities following that historic project's establishment.
