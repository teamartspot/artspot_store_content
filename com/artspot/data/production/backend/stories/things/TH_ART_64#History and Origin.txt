
Silver filigree (Naa Kesiya or Neela Cheshtraw) is a captivating metalworking technique hailing from Karimnagar in Telangana state, India.

Its exact origins are somewhat obscure but the craft flourished significantly during the Nizam period, especially under patronage provided by figures like Mir Osman Ali Khan. It became integrated into the socio-economic fabric of Kadapa district (which includes areas historically associated with Karimnagar) and nearby regions such as Bapatla.

The art involves twisting thin silver wires through burnishing using a rotating hand-cranked 'Kesiyan Chund' stone – an intricate process mastered for generations. Designs range from traditional motifs to delicate, floral patterns often adorning turbans or serving as headgear decorations ('Ghunghroo') and jewellery. Pattachitra scrolls sometimes dictated designs; artisans would trace them onto silver wire.

The craft showcases remarkable skill with relatively basic tools (just metal thread, copper stone chakki, goldstone graver, and burnisher). UNESCO recognizes this form of intricate filigree work as part of the Intangible Cultural Heritage associated closely with Karimnagar's rich textile traditions. The vibrant blue turbans for men are often complemented by silver zardozi (embroidered) or filigree decorative elements woven into their fabric, creating a unique aesthetic tied firmly to its place of origin.
