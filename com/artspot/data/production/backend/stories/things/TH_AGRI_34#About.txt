
Nagami Chilli, also known as Nagami Chili or Naga Mirchi, is a distinctively long, thin chili pepper hailing from North East India. Renowned primarily for its exceptionally high capsaicin content and blistering heat – even some variants of the Bhut Jolokia, considered potentially the hottest cultivated chili in the world, trace their lineage back to Nagami stock – it has carved a unique place globally.

Measuring over 50 cm (about 2 feet) long when fully mature, the Nagami fruit often tapers dramatically. Its fiery reputation stems from an intense heat level measured significantly above the Scoville heat unit scale average for chili peppers today.

While small quantities are edible due to its heat, Nagami is cultivated almost entirely as a source of extremely hot ghost chilies or Bhut Jolokia-type chilies used as powders and pastes. It's prized by home gardeners in India not just for its potential ancestor status but also because it offers more bang (heat) for the buck compared to other long, thin peppers like the Momordica.

Due to its extreme heat and flavor intensity, it’s best suited for powdered form or processing into paste rather than fresh eating. A culinary powerhouse despite its singular appearance and focus on extreme heat.
