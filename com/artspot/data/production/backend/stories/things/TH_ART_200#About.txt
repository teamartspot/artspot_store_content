
Rooted in the unique cultural identity of the Parsi community within Gujarat's Kutch district, Rogan craft stands as a singular and striking visual art form native to India. It is distinguished by its intricate painting technique applied directly onto the animal hide surface using natural dyes.

Originating from historical times before leather processing became mainstream, Rogan artists inscribe minute lines in fine black Arabic calligraphy, creating complex patterns – often featuring birds amidst flowers or human figures integrated into geometric designs across diverse fabrics. The base for a typical piece is traditionally prepared goat skin stretched over a frame.

What marks Rogan craft as unusual and remarkable are the visible brushstrokes; sometimes a thin line is deliberately left opaque to showcase both the underlying leather texture and paint, while in others it serves to connect sections of writing or design. Colours used are derived from natural sources like indigo plants and traditional dyes applied with precision.

Often used in royal insignia or temple banners (during Diwali as well), Rogan art is a profound expression of Parsi heritage through the medium of leather, representing intricate narratives woven visually into its surface.
