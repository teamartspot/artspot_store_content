
Farrukhabad, a city in Uttar Pradesh with a rich handloom heritage often overshadowed by Lucknow or Varanasi, is also the birthplace of some truly distinctive prints. Known colloquially as 'Burhanpur' – a nod to its royal past where intricate designs were imitated on canvas for the Darbar complex – this print owes much of its character to the technique called puree.

Originating in Mughlampur during the British colonial era and gaining prominence later, puree is unique. The fabric undergoes multiple dips (purees) through a vat containing three colours before the intricate block print is applied. This results in areas where printed blocks overlay each other without intervening dye layers, creating opaque white or light grey backgrounds beneath vibrant blue-green floral patterns.

The prints feature large, stylised motifs – flowers like *padmarag* (peacock rose), lotus sprays and intricate 'fishtail' hems. These designs are characteristic of Khurjaman, the local dialect in which they developed originally. The use of indigo dye contributes significantly to their iconic feel.

Often showcasing bold statements against subdued grounds or whites, Farrukhabad prints represent vibrant craftsmanship from this historic city and serve as a tangible link between its royal past and contemporary textile artistry.
