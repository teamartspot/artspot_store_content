
Banaras Gulabi Meenakari is a captivating synthesis of two distinct and highly revered Indian art forms: Chikankhana from Lucknow, renowned for its intricate white-on-black embroidery on Mysore silk, and Meenakari, the vibrant enamel work traditionally associated with Mysore itself. This unique style originated in Varanasi (Banaras) when weavers brought back exquisite Mysore-style shawls, and their artisans innovatively combined this delicate embroidery technique with the application of shimmering gold filigree work or intricate silver *meena* patterns.

The defining characteristic is its enchanting pinkish-red base wash, often achieved using a specific type of lac dye from Kishangarh near Jaipur. This 'gulab' colour forms a striking contrast to the finer white or cream embroidery threads meticulously stitched onto the rich silk fabric and gold foil work underneath some areas.

The technique begins with gilding thin varnished paper, then layers intricate silver patterns followed by washes of coloured enamel – typically reds, pinks, blues, greens, yellows, and sometimes additional white work. These colour blocks are carefully blended in situ to create smooth gradients and transitions before firing. Once the design is complete with multicoloured enamelled motifs (often stylised flowers), it's transferred onto a Mysore silk fabric piece gilded on its reverse.

The final stages involve fixing Chikara stitches – tiny running threads – in gold or silver thread over specific areas, particularly to outline figures above the 'gulab' wash. This detailed finishing creates depth and contrast against the soft rose hue. The result is a richly coloured shawl with an iridescent sheen where enamelled colours meet, complemented dramatically by the shiny linear details of the gold or silver work.
