
# Ganjam Kewda Flower: The Divine Nectar

Ganjam Kewda, known as **natural gem**, is a unique type of sandalwood oil derived from the essence gathered through flowers. Despite its name referencing *Kewra* today, its origin story traces back to a different floral source.

According to legend – perhaps drawing inspiration from Mysore sandalwood fame or films like 'Sulamanee' (1978) – Ganjam Kewda flower is said to grow near the Himalayas, associated with divine power. It was believed that this sacred flower existed at great spiritual sites and temples.

Its actual origin appears **predominantly south Indian**, rooted in traditions surrounding Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu regions. The name 'Ganjam' likely refers to a specific location or perhaps evokes royal connotations (as in the city known for Mysore sandalwood). Whether its Himalayan lore is purely mythical or based on older, now-forgotten flowers remains uncertain.

The true source of modern Ganjam Kewda oil isn't the flower itself but from its **oil reservoirs located at the roots**. The flower's primary function seems focused on capturing and channeling these valuable essences, rather than naturally emitting them like other sandalwood types or kewra flowers found elsewhere.

This concept connects to many Indian Ayurvedic traditions where certain trees possess subterranean accumulations of their essence drawn upwards by specific flowers during ritualistic harvesting or potentiation processes. Ganjam Kewda represents this **specialized form** focused on flower capture through its unique root reservoir mechanism, making it a fascinating chapter in India's diverse natural perfume heritage.
