
Kondapalli Bommallu, also known as Bummallow or Bommalu specifically from Kondapur (often misspelled Kondapalli), refers to a unique form of traditional puppetry deeply rooted in the culture of South India. Originating likely in and around the city of Guntur, this art involves intricate performances using puppets tied onto strings.

While "Kondapalli Bommallu" is sometimes mentioned as part of its name due to potential regional variations or village associations, the standard term is 'Bommallow'. It's widely recognized as an offshoot specifically developed in Kondapuram (the region near Guntur often misspelled by outsiders) around the mid-20th century.

Unlike shadow puppets ('Thallalakshmi') popular throughout Southern India, Bummallow performs actual figures on sticks held above a cloth backdrop. Figures from other regions like 'Kooduppu' (Cheruvu in South India) often use handle rods for manipulation. These Kondapuram puppeteers primarily used their fingers and arms along with the stick to create expressive characters.

Bommallu puppets are characteristically colourful, vibrant, and exquisitely detailed carvings, representing various gods, goddesses, demons ('Asura'), animals, kings, queens, merchants, clowns, and mythological scenes. These figures are unique pieces of art rather than being mass-produced 'dolls',' as they often were in other string traditions.

Often performed in courtyards or temples during festivals using a reel to project figures from above onto the screen, Kondapalli Bommallu (from around Guntur) is more likely part of the lineage originating specifically in Kondapuram ('Koodupalakshmi' puppets nearby), rather than 'Kondapalli' being its sole designation.
