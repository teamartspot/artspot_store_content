
Wrestling holds great importance in Indian sport culture, contributing through various regional styles like Kusti or Pehlwani, each boasting its own unique history and origin. The state of Odisha produces several distinct wrestling forms collectively known as Ghevadas (plural of Ghevada). These traditional games have deep roots.

While specific historical details are scarce pre-modern times, wrestling among the tribal communities in forests like Similipal always existed. However, we can trace certain organized forms linked to broader athletic competitions called Mattu Cheval or Kote Ghevada. The modern concept began formally after India's Quit Independence Movement 1942 (18-03).

After Quit India in May 1942, the Indian National Congress held a central sports conference at Delhi Sports College under Dr S Nijalingam and Pt Govindananda. A significant outcome was the creation of a unified wrestling style to incorporate tribal elements into modern sport.

Hanumanthappa Naik, born near Malkangiri (formerly Mayurbhanj), played a crucial role. He adapted this new Ghevada style in contests against wrestlers from various regions and other styles like Kushti under his coach's guidance at Rayasandur Peta School near Udagarh during 1954-56.

From the fusion of traditional wrestling techniques found in Mattu Cheval, evolved came Waghya - a specific style born to meet regional challenges in mixed Ghevada tournaments. This synthesis marked its origin post Quit India period. It represents an interesting chapter in unifying tribal fighting arts into structured competition format that survives today across Central and Eastern Indian states.
