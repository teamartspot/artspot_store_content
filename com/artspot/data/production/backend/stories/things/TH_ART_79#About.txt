
Screw Pine, locally known as 'Kasati,' refers specifically to baskets woven from the long, durable fibers of the *Bryophyllum gibbosum* plant found abundantly on Kerala's rocky coast. These distinctive baskets are instantly recognizable by their unique screw-shaped handles, which interlock with others or doorways for carrying.

This craft holds immense significance in Kerala, particularly among the fishing communities of Kuttanadu and coastal areas. Traditionally woven at home using rudimentary tools like a comb (kanju) stretched on bamboo frames, it wasn't just utilitarian; baskets were exchanged gifts during festivals, symbolizing friendship and connection to their source.

Today, while modern synthetic goods offer alternatives, Screw Pine weaving continues both as a vital livelihood activity for many families in coastal belt villages dependent on the industry and as a crucial element of Kerala's intangible cultural heritage. Cooperatives play an increasingly important role in uniting weavers, offering better prices often through intricate appliqué work integrated into baskets destined primarily overseas.

The long-life cycles provided by traditional tools allow for dense basket weaving over time, while eco-friendly natural dyes are used before finishing and the fibers degrade naturally. This cherished coastal craft remains a testament to Keralaites' resourcefulness with native plants, skillfully spun from humble materials into everyday utility and cherished artistry.
