
Chitrakathi is a unique folk art combining visual storytelling with traditional song (Lavani). Originating in Maharashtra around Pune, it seamlessly blends the artist's performance ("Katthapushpata") with intricate shadow puppetry.

A Chitrakathi exponent sits on a footstool during evenings and fairs. Using rods or skewers, they operate small puppets cut from thin leather painted with detailed miniature scenes. As an accompanist singing Lavani devotional songs about Lord Viththal, but also including mythological tales like those of Devi.

The narrative unfolds through dialogue between the visuals shown by moving the puppets on the cloth and the lyrics sung by the artist. This art form moves beyond mere performance; it requires significant skill in crafting the puppets themselves from goatskin.

Chitrakathi serves a practical purpose too, providing entertainment during traditional farming tasks like the Bhogi Thandai preparation or field waterings (jalyukta) before modern media dominated India’s social fabric. It offers local communities an engaging connection to their cultural past and showcases a vibrant synthesis of regional craftsmanship and performance art.
