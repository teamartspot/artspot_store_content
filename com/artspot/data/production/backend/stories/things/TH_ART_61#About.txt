
A riot of rich colours, intricate detail, and storytelling grace, Mysore painting stands as a celebrated art form emerging from the royal court of Mysore in southern India during the 19th century. Characterized by its luminous palette and delicate brushwork, it traces its lineage to similar schools like Tanjore and Kerala.

Often set against an ornate gold or cream background upon silk, the paintings achieve their signature shimmering effect through generous use of gold leaf and mica dust mixed with gum arabic in tempera. While rooted in Hindu religious iconography – portraying deities from diverse temples, saints, and mythological narratives depicting divine love and heroic battles – they also depict courtly scenes, processions, or specific allegorical moments. Themes are typically calm yet filled with narrative energy.

Distinctive features include the inclusion of intricate gilded temple towers as borders surrounding each composition, often embellished with celestial beings and other details from temple architecture artistry. The medium itself is a potent blend: gold for luxury against a vibrant base provided by high-quality natural pigments like indigo, turmeric, gambir (tamarind), and vegetable colours sourced locally.

A testament to the Mysore royal family's patronage, their collection showcases the evolution of this unique style. Each piece reflects not just artistic flair but also dedication to preserving traditional techniques alongside welcoming innovative influences. It remains a living heritage art form cherished across India today.
