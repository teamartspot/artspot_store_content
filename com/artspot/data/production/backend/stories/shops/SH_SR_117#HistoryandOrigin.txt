The story of silk began over 4,000 years ago in ancient China, with legends crediting Empress Leizu for discovering the shimmering thread from a silkworm cocoon. Silk spread through the Silk Road, eventually reaching India, where it flourished in the northeastern regions.

In Meghalaya, Eri silk—locally called Ryndia—became central to traditional life. Legend says a Buddhist monk brought sericulture to India by smuggling silkworms in bamboo. Since then, the craft has thrived, especially among women in the Ri-Bhoi district. Meghalaya is one of the few regions producing all four natural silks—Mulberry, Eri, Tasar, and Muga—making it a vital part of India’s textile heritage. Eri silk’s unique eco-friendly nature and cultural significance make it a timeless treasure of the region.
