he heritage of Gadwal sarees stretches back nearly 200–300 years, emerging during the reign of the Gadwal Samsthanam under the patronage of Queen Maharani Adhi Lakshmi Devamma.

 Their signature technique combines a fine cotton body with a silk pallu and zari border, employing the intricate kuttu (interlock) weave to ensure feather-light fabric that folds into a matchbox.

 The craft evolved significantly in the 19th–20th centuries, when skilled Banaras weavers were invited to share brocade techniques, adding to the sophistication of local weaving.

 Large-scale commercialization began in 1986 with the founding of Gadwal Weavers Society—uniting local artisans into a cooperative model that promotes authenticity and provides economic stability. In 2010, Gadwal sarees received Geographical Indication (GI) protection, recognizing their unique artistry and securing legal identification of origin.

 Today, Gadwal Weavers Society continues its mission—supporting artisan livelihoods, fostering sustainability, and safeguarding this cherished cultural legacy for future generations.
