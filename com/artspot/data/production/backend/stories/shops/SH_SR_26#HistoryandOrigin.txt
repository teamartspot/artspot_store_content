The journey of uppada began in the late 1980s, when designer Ghanshyam Sarode took on the mission of reviving India’s vanishing weaving traditions. In 1988, he reintroduced the lost art of Jamdani weaving in Uppada, a coastal village in Andhra Pradesh—breathing life into what would soon be known globally as “Uppada Sarees.”

Unlike mechanized weaving, the Jamdani method involves painstaking hand manipulation of motifs, often requiring months to complete a single saree. Sarode's approach has always focused on preserving this authenticity while adapting traditional designs for modern sensibilities.

Over two decades, his work has been showcased at prestigious exhibitions and fairs across the USA, UK, Europe, Dubai, and East Asia. His khadi creations, especially the white sarees with interlocked Jamdani motifs worn by Mrs. Gandhi since 2003, are now part of a national design identity and retailed by government emporiums such as Central Cottage Industries.

Through uppada, Ghanshyam Sarode continues his legacy of inspiring, training, and collaborating with artisans across India—ensuring that each thread tells a story of cultural richness and timeless design.
