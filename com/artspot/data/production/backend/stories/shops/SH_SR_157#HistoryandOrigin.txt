The Khatri community, believed to descend from Brahma Kshatriyas, has practiced Bandhani for over 200 years. Historically settled across Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Punjab, their artistry flourished during the reign of King Khengarji I of Kutch, who invited skilled communities from Sindh to boost trade and industry in the 16th century. Many Khatri artisans were commissioned by royalty and continue to preserve this legacy today.

Bandhani, one of the oldest known forms of tie-dye, dates back nearly 5000 years. Its earliest mentions appear in historical texts like Bana Bhatt’s Harshacharita and are visually represented in the Ajanta caves. Initially pioneered by the Khatri community in Gujarat, Bandhani flourished in regions like Jamnagar, Jaipur, Bhilwara, and Bikaner—producing iconic odhnis, sarees, and turbans that blend artistic heritage with cultural pride.
