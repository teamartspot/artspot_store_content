
Manipur lies nestled in the easternmost part of North-east India, situated on the border with China (specifically Arunachal Pradesh) and Burma. The state borders Tripura to its south-west and Nagaland to its north.

Rivers play a vital role shaping Manipur's landscape and history; these waterways have historically defined its boundaries and fostered unique cultures along their banks, including those in Imphal valley and the mighty Barak plain in the South-East. The capital city is Imphal.

Administratively, it sprawls across a high physiographic province but extends down into the Terai region via international rivers. Its population comprises diverse ethno-linguistic communities like the Meiteis (Mongol-Khampas), Nagas, and various Kukis or Santhal groups.

The official language is English, though Manipuri serves as a link dialect among many tribes. People primarily follow Hinduism or Christianity; these beliefs are interwoven with significant traditional folk religions across different regions.

Its name in other languages holds historical significance:
- Meetei: Хада Хури
- Burmese (Myanmar): မကူပြည် Hada Zawayar State

A landlocked state, Manipur offers breathtaking beauty from snow-capped mountains and dense forests to serene temple enclaves. The rich cultural tapestry includes distinctive dances such as the Gavari and Marma Hara. It possesses immense potential for harnessing hydro-power and integrating into regional connectivity initiatives despite ongoing development hurdles.
