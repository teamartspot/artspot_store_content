
These are two distinct but equally special union territories nestled in India's western side. Today known as Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu (informally D&DA), they possess a unique history.

Daman and Diu lies off the coast of Gujarat, bordering Oman across the sea. Its capital is Diu. The region speaks Konkani (like in coastal Maharashtra) alongside Gujarati and Hindi. This area has fascinating remnants of its Portuguese colonial past scattered within its beaches and settlements on both banks, often overlooked by visitors despite their beauty.

The other side is Dadra and Nagar Haveli itself which sits right where Andhra Pradesh meets the state border of Maharashtra (specifically Pune and Raigad districts). Its capital city was Ajara. This territory held a particularly checkered history under French influence until being formally integrated into India much later, in 1956 through an amendment.

Both regions have significant Indo-French connections historically – perhaps more visible than any other state or union territory! They offer insights into coastal Gujarat's fishing community vibe on the Oman side and showcase distinct cultural interfaces along the border. Their small size belies a rich past waiting to be explored by those curious about India's diverse, lesser-known corners.
