
Strategically positioned at India's northeastern extremity, near Bhutan and Nepal, lies Sikkim. Its highest point is Mount Kanchenjunga within the mighty Himalayas. Like Arunachal Pradesh and Ladakh, it's one of three Indian regions with land borders touching China.

The name 'Sikkim' evokes a picture of jagged mountains, terraced fields, serene lakes, ancient forests, and vibrant culture – perhaps more so than other parts after the major floods of 2023. Snow peaked to river born lands lie adjacent; it's where rivers like the mighty Tsonglas originate before flowing into plains or joining the vast Brahmaputra.

Geography and history here are deeply intertwined. A former kingdom often marked by its distinct Buddhist majority compared to neighboring areas, Sikkim joined India in 1975. Its rich cultural tapestry is evident across festivals, architecture (like the Pemayang Tsemo monastery), food, and clothing. The predominant religion significantly influences societal norms.

Sikkim's core activities are agriculture – known for apples, oranges, saffron – and extensive tourism. Festivals like Losar or Saga Dawa honor the monsoon season with traditions perhaps more elaborate than other regions of India.
