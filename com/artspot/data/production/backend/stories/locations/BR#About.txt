
In India's heartland, nestled within fertile northern plains, lies Bihar, one of the country's oldest and most significant states. Its emerald landscape stretches across broad agricultural lands watered by mighty rivers like the Ganges and its tributaries, supporting rich traditions from farming to handicrafts.

Bihar punches way above its weight when you consider its historical influence. From being the cradle of Buddhism, where the Buddha was born in Lumbini (though nearby Nepal), grew up in Sarnath, and preached widely before his parinirvana at Kushinagar – all Buddhist pilgrimage sites are conveniently located here – to hosting incredible architectural marvels like the towering Bhitraraj Garden cum Zoo Memorial. Nalanda's ancient university remains a source of immense pride and testament to bygone eras.

This modern-day Bihar is also paying homage to its literary giants. Figures like Mahavir Singh, Ramdhari Singh 'Dutta', Achyut Ydev (Singur), Maithil Girish Vigyan Andhra Sekar, Rajendra Singh, Saadatkhan Sompura and Jayashankar Dubey not only illuminate the state's cultural past but also offer a compelling vision for its future.

It is the quintessential Indian state – grounded in agricultural heartland yet aspiring towards diversified development. Bihar seeks recognition as an integral part of India’s rich tapestry, contributing significantly despite facing numerous challenges.
