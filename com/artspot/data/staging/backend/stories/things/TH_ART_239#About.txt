
The Nicobari Mat (also known as Chatrai or Hileuoi), an ethnic group sometimes colloquially termed Pygmy, are one of India's most isolated tribes geographically. They primarily inhabit islands scattered across the northern periphery of the Andaman & Nicobar archipelago – including Great Nicobar island and parts of Little Nicobar and neighboring islets.

Their historical seclusion allowed them to maintain a unique identity distinct from other Andamanese groups like the Jarawa or Sentinelese. While physically resembling "Negritos," their close geographical location led many researchers initially to classify them under *Andamanoid* until recent DNA studies confirmed separate migration waves into these isolated island clusters.

The name 'Mat' itself is an anglicized form of Krenak Holud, part of the language isolate they speak, known as Oye. Their usage – primarily as verbs or function words – offers compelling insight into some of linguistics oldest roots potentially tracing back to ancestral proto-languages lost elsewhere long ago.

Their traditional homes are roundish huts elevated on stilts called *pugalu*. Culturally, they exhibit a unique form of religion and spirituality alongside distinct ecological adaptations suited to their specific island environments. The bow made from monkey grass (*Schisandraceae*) is particularly symbolic of the group's identity and hunting reliance within their respective ecosystem.
