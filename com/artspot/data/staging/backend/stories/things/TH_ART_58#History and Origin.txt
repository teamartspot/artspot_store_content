
Mata ni Pachedi (Bhakti Sookha) is a distinct form of community-based art born in the rural agricultural villages surrounding Dantan district near Bhadreswar, Howrah, West Bengal. Its name translates directly from the local language as 'paintings on mud', reflecting its original context.

This indigenous art emerged organically among landless farmers and agricultural labourers who created simple sketches during breaks or at night using locally sourced materials like cow dung slurry mixed with natural colours derived from vegetables, flowers, minerals, and trees. The impetus for wider recognition came decades later with the influential works of social anthropologist Dr. Pandit Kshitish Chattopadhyay in the early 1960s. He documented these murals on mud platforms near agricultural fields or temples.

Further exposure during the Chotia Jatra festival, starting around his 1965 research published into three parts by James Cameron via Channel IV television from the mid-2010s onwards, brought significant attention to *Mata ni Pachedi*. This documentation encouraged the continuation and expansion of painting on different surfaces, leading to permanent commissions over bamboo frames.

Unlike traditional market art seeking high aesthetic standards, Mata ni Pachedi prioritizes authenticity. Artists often use rudimentary brushes from cow hair or bamboo scrapes directly onto rough-hewn natural substrates like Khadira leaves ('Holy' fig), locally made terracotta, cloth, paper mache, or even walls plastered with mud and lime.

They depict reality, not idealization – scenes of everyday life, agricultural labour realities, land disputes, evictions, religious practices, and family rituals. Often viewed through the male gaze, the art frequently focuses on depicting women amidst fields of rice and sugarcane stalks in characteristic postures (standing, eating), highlighting their centrality to village existence despite different perspectives.

This accessible form retains its grassroots origins, using earthy materials for community expression rather than primarily aiming at market appeal or individual decoration.
