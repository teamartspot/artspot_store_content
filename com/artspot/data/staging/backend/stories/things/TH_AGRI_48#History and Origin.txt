
Deep roots in the Wayanad plateau soil are held by Gandhakasala rice, a distinctive variety cherished by communities within its borders for generations. Originating from the ancient agricultural heartland stretching across parts of Uttara Kannada (previously Dakshina Kauncha), Malenadu, and the erstwhile Coorg region in present-day Karnataka, this unpolished rice has been cultivated since time immemorial.

Unlike most polished rices consumed today, authentic Gandhakasala retains its husk naturally. This preservation is intrinsically linked to its unique cooking method – traditionally prepared with ghee (clarified butter), infusing the grains with a subtle nutty flavour and aroma that evokes images of rural kitchens. The careful, often rain-fed cultivation upholding age-old farming practices ensures distinct characteristics: it boasts higher protein and mineral content compared to polished rice due to lesser processing, aiding digestion.

Its rich history is preserved through careful farming; while highly regarded, overharvesting poses a threat if traditional methods aren't sustained. Today, this heritage rice remains largely local, commanding respect for its cultural significance and nutritional vitality tied directly to the specific land and generations of farmers who have nurtured it in Wayanad.
