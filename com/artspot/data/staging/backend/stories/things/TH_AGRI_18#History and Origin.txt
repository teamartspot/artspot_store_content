
Mysore Malligae traces its roots back deeply within the rich cultural annals of Mysore, now known as Mysuru in Karnataka, India. While the precise moment of its "birth" is debatable among scholars, it represents a significant layer built upon an older structure – primarily Carnatic music notation.

The origin story often points to the royal patronage and scholarly environment fostered during the reigns of Wodeyar kings, particularly figures like Raja Rajmuni Sannidhana I (1573–1620). He reorganized Carnatic music into notations influenced by Sanskrit principles. However, while Sanskrit notations were known earlier via texts and treatises translating ragas through vowel/consonant templates derived from ancient Indian vocalic modes *(nada-bharata)*, the specific *Malligae* system evolved later.

Traditional accounts primarily credit two royal scholars: Sannidhana I around 1609 CE for developing an initial set of musical notes using Kannada language (likely Old Telugu-Kannada Malayalam), and his grandson HaraHaranayaka Wodeyar approximately a century later. HaraHaraneerad's version is considered the classic structure from which "Mysore Malligae" largely derives, adding notations that made Carnatic music accessible via local scripts to wider audiences in Mysore.

Thus, Mysore Mallige wasn't created *ex nihilo* but developed *within* the existing musical framework. It served as a practical tool for musicians in and around the Mysore court long before print and became the standard written form associated with that royal legacy.
