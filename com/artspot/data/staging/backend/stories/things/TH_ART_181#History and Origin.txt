
Mainpuri Tarkashi is a vibrant folk dance tradition deeply embedded in the agricultural culture of Mainpuri town and surrounding areas in Uttar Pradesh, India. It's not merely entertainment but a unique performance intrinsically linked to celebrating bountiful harvests.

The dance typically revolves around groups performing energetic sequences often referred to locally as 'Tarkas.' Characterized by its brisk pace and exuberant style, men usually form the circle or chain within which other men move rapidly (shivering in their attire), while nimble females engage in quick footwork creating figures outside ('Shankariya'), symbolizing energy derived from tilling the land. The name itself is thought to derive from 'Tark' (a measure of grain) and a word implying speed or sprightly movement, often indicating 'Shakri Tark' (grain dance).

Rooted in village life before modern festivals, its origins lie among peasant communities where seasonal movements defined much existence, linking labour, fertility rituals related to earth's productivity, community bonds, and unpretentious folk amusement. It continues today, evolving from basic group performances following specific sequences during harvest times into intricate displays of skill featured prominently at various cultural festivals in Mainpuri and elsewhere celebrating local heritage.
