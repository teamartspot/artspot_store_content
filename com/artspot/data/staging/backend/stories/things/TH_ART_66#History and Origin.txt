
Mysore rosewood, scientifically **Dalbergia sissoo**, is a prominent Indian timber species whose rich history largely originates from the erstwhile Kingdom of Mysore, now part of Karnataka. Its significance isn't merely botanical but deeply intertwined with centuries-old craftsmanship and cultural symbolism.

During the reign of the Wodeyar dynasty (1399-1799 CE), particularly flourishing in later periods, the artisans around Mysore developed a celebrated craft centered on this wood. While rosewood worked for royal inlay existed elsewhere, notably Persianate centers like Golconda or Bijapur earlier, what defines *Mysore* rosewood is its distinct marriage of intricate craftsmanship with specific cultural commissions. For centuries, Indian rulers and nobles lavished intricate, mosaic-like patterns cut from fine rosewood veneer overlaid with minutely chased gold onto palaces, ceremonial daggers (*khilats*), religious artifacts, writing boxes (*mamlatdas*), temples, and royal musical instruments like veena.

The art form reached its zenith in the mid-20th century under Sri Jayachamarajendra Wodeyar. He saw it as a crucial repository of Mysore's cultural legacy competing with newer industries fuelled by state-led initiatives, particularly textiles post-independence. Fearful for its survival and worried artisans faced economic hardship after returning from royal workshops in disfavored periods or losing traditional clientele from neighboring regions absorbing their skills, he championed the craft.

His intervention included funding a ban on large-scale rosewood export during a period of high value (often linked to cricket world cups), establishing formal training schools (*Sishu Vidyalaya* for visually and mentally handicapped individuals among others), and actively promoting Mysore rosewood inlay works. This royal patronage, however ancient the craft, bought it invaluable contemporary recognition, preserving its legacy across generations while emphasizing its unique emergence from the heartland of Mysore's art scene.
