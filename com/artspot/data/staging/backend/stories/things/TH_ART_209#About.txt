
Lacquered wonder from Maharashtra boasts mesmerizing beauty! Sawantwadi wooden craft, originating from this quaint village near Pune in India's western state, stands out for its intricate wood carving work and exquisitely painted surfaces. Often known as *Kamla Vidya*, meaning the art of writing on lacquer or bamboo, it truly comes alive.

The hallmark of Sawantwadi pieces lies not just in their elaborate carvings but also in the meticulous flowery paintings applied directly onto a prepared layer of bamboo, often waxed and decorated. These vibrant designs are then carefully gilded over with multiple thin layers of shining, iridescent lacquer varnish, which is typically shellac-based.

This craft found its royal patronage under the Gaekwad rulers initially, evolving significantly. While initially crafted on paper or cloth before being transferred to wood pieces like trays and furniture, it now primarily adorns smaller wooden objects – especially intricately carved flower baskets ('Ghanta', 'Mundada' in local Marathi). The famous Sawantwadi bird-and-flower motifs are characteristic, alongside scenes depicting mythology, royalty, Parsi legends, daily life, or nature's beauty.

Though the craft continues its evolution as artists experiment with different styles while maintaining their deep connection to Indian epics and classical elements. Its fusion of woodwork, lacquer painting, gilding, and intricate designs earned it the status of a Geographical Indicator (GI) in India, celebrating this unique regional heritage.
