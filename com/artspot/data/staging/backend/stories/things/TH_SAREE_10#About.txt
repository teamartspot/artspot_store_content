
Originating from the rich textile heritage of Punjab, particularly Pathankot and Balakote in Kapurthala district, Phulkari is a distinctive form of handmade embroidery on silk fabric. Characterized by its intricate patterns derived primarily from mirror-work or 'phulla' impressions – fine lines outlining motifs like paisley, floral designs, arrows, and geometric shapes that evoke blooming flowers – the name literally translates to "flower embroidery". A key feature is the use of colourful metallic bobs shaped like golden flowers on an 'aad', a curved rod, to pull threads through the fabric layer by layer (typically seven), creating fine chain stitch lines. This meticulous process makes each piece a truly unique work of art.

Often rendered as *aarkari* or 'drawn' phulkari using gold and silver metallic thread instead of bobs, variations exist even within this craft. Phulkari sarees are celebrated for their vibrant hues – ranging from bold traditional colours to softer pastels, complemented by contrasting backgrounds like dark blue or black.

Their appeal lies in the exquisite craftsmanship and the dynamic display offered only on a silk saree pallau base. A blend of hand-embroidered charm and inherent silken luxury, Phulkari are undeniably some of the most beautiful and intricate creations in the world of Indian handicrafts, symbolizing Punjabi cultural identity through artistry.
