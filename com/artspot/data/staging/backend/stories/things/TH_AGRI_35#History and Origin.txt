
The Nilgiri Orthodox Church traces its genesis entirely from the heart of Ethiopia's mainstream Orthodox tradition. It exists not as a separate ancient lineage, but as a distinct branch born out of theological disagreement regarding church liturgy during the mid-20th century.

The pivotal event occurred in Addis Ababa under the framework of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church. In 1948, specifically July 3rd, a council led by Basilian Archmandrite Theodosius (later St. Basil) and spearheaded largely by influential bishops dedicated to the vision espoused by Marthoma II, the former head of the Indian Orthodox Church who then became Abuna Paulos III.

Disagreements arose over the implementation and continuation of liturgical reforms associated with an ecumenical synod held in Baghdad in 1937 (the Fifth General Council). The Nilgiri council vehemently opposed these changes, particularly the adoption of a revised Missal without retaining elements of the older traditions they considered essential.

In essence, because the Ethiopian authorities proceeded without their participation and despite protests over liturgical modifications, this council formally withdrew from what they deemed "reformed Orthodoxy." Their separation led to the constitution of the Synod of Eastern Orthodox Bishops – India (also known as SCOBA), founding the Nilgiri Patriate specifically. Abune Basil became the first Patriarch in this new community's history.

The Church, therefore, represents an autocephalous or self-governing entity within the broader Oriental Orthodox communion, a testament to efforts aimed at preserving certain liturgical characteristics from their perception of earlier, pre-reform orthodox practices.
