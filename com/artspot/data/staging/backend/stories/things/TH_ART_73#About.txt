
Originating from West Bengal in present-day Bangladesh, Nakshi Kantha quilts are a beautiful form of embroidered bed covering deeply embedded in Bengali culture. Unlike the blankets they resembled initially – practical additions to beds before mattresses were common or filled with warmth for themselves – their decorative nature became paramount thanks to the intricate 'Chola' or running stitch technique.

The quilt's process is simple yet yields extraordinary results. It uses layers of padding stitched together with a straight thread, often silk from old saris, creating a base fabric. The magic lies in this stitching: where two threads cross over each other, they form tiny knots to fill the voids, while continuous straight stitches connect them, allowing colourful beads or turned-over strands of embroidery thread (fabric-covered bobbins) to sit 'on top' between the runs.

This distinctive method creates geometric patterns filled with vibrant coloured motifs. Common themes include paisleys, flower sprigs, traditional Bengali hoods for warmth ('ghanga') and intricate shapes drawn from Jatra theatre masks. Legend has it that husbands would give these quilts made from their wives' discarded saris as gifts on visiting.

Today, Nakshi Kantha quilts are much more than functional bedding; they represent the rich craft tradition of Bengal, embodying creativity, comfort, warmth, and hospitality passed down through ages.
