
One of South India's most renowned historical centres for terracotta art and craft is Villianur, located near Madurai in Tamil Nadu.

Its story began centuries ago when local artisans started producing everyday items from clay. The quality and durability of the pottery attracted notice far beyond its immediate surroundings. By around 1820 or slightly earlier, a significant development occurred – a major terracotta factory was established here under a proprietary company. This marked Villianur's rise to prominence.

For centuries leading up to this point, clay figurines (mudum poraiyal) and householdware were being crafted locally, gaining recognition through trade routes connecting the region with other parts of India. The formal establishment amplified this craft into a substantial industry. Villianur became famous for its distinctive style – known for fine relief decorations and detailed painting.

Over the years, regional tastes evolved, particularly towards contemporary ceramics with smooth finishes, somewhat overshadowing Villianur's older, more intricate methods temporarily. However, the historical significance remains undeniable. Today, while perhaps not operating on the same scale as in the past industrial period, small workshops continue to carry forward this rich heritage of traditional terracotta making at *Villianur*.
