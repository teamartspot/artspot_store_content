
Joha rice is a distinctive variety of aromatic rice originating from the state of Assam in India. Its history stretches back through generations within Assamese farming communities, integral to their traditional agriculture. Unlike many long-grain rices found elsewhere, Joha grains are relatively shorter and plumper.

This speciality lies primarily in its unique nutty flavour profile, reminiscent of roasted nuts like almonds or walnuts, a characteristic often linked to the local Brahmaputra soil. It possesses distinct musky notes, setting it apart from common white rice known for subtle sweetness. Its aroma is different – less pungent jasmine scent, more complex and earthy.

In Assamese cuisine, Joha rice holds profound importance beyond being just food; it’s considered essential to their identity. Dishes like Salgiraa Khichdi (rice cooked with bamboo shoots) heavily feature this grain's unique taste. Eri silk cotton moth larvae also are often paired with Joha rice dishes. It forms the base for everyday meals – simple plain khichudi seasoned only minimally, or elaborate feasts.

The texture varies; some preparations result in a drier khichdi than others. Often medium to long grains, they require careful cooking. Traditionally known as 'Neeta' (white) rice when raw, Joha truly comes alive upon cooking, revealing its characteristic aroma and flavour, which define many essential Assamese meals.

Its presence signifies tradition itself – a taste deeply woven into the cultural tapestry of Northeast India.
