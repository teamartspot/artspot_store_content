
Kathputli puppetry, also known as traditional rod puppets of Nepal and India's Kathmandu Valley and surrounding regions like Darjeeling, stands as a unique and vibrant form of storytelling. Its origins trace back over centuries to ancient Hindu folk traditions, particularly the dramatic narratives sung by wandering bards called Yakshas (related to Yakshagana). These early forms utilized simple puppets manipulated with rods.

The name "Kathputli" itself offers clues – derived from 'Kath' meaning stick or drumstick ('kathe') used for control and performance music, combined with 'Putli', the Sanskrit word for doll. The skilled puppeteers performing these Kathputlis are known as Kathaitara ("story teller through strings"). They often perform in small groups led by a Thali (drum master) who accompanies the narration on percussion instruments like dhol and madira, while others comment on religion and morals.

While seemingly distinct from shadow puppets, some believe it has roots in related traditions. The cylindrical hoops called 'tharu' used to create armature might share echoes with early Buddhist forms. The bright red colouring often seen is a marker of Kathputli, potentially linked to celebrations like Deepawali, and represents solar power – the yin-yang balance essential for life.

Kathputli traditionally serves religious festivals such as Dashain and Tihar in Nepal, preserving ancient epics and regional legends through song. Its intricate puppets require careful handling and contribute significantly to India's rich cultural tapestry alongside other folk arts like Kathputla or Chhau dances.
