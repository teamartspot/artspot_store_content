
Lambani Embroidery is a unique form of traditional hand embroidery practiced mainly in Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan. Harking back to the 15th century AD, this distinctive art flourished among the Gond community, specifically their artisanal clans known as 'Lamban'. The craft was created by women artisans representing celestial beings during harvest-time festivals like Kareswar Panchami.

The technique is characterized by its use of pins instead of threads to affix cloth patterns. Typically, artists create intricate picture outlines with multiple rows of hand-formed nails (usually steel). Once the pattern's boundary or inner details are marked, tiny silk thread called 'sangan' is woven between these nail points through a dense, open fabric known as *kanjivendi*, lending it an antique yet tactile appearance.

A defining feature is the absence of conventional shading; Lambani embroidery instead emphasizes bold black lines against contrasting coloured fabrics. Often employed in appliqué work where cut-out motifs are stitched onto a canvas base, creating striking textural depth and dimensional patterns that capture cosmic themes or mythical figures beautifully.

This living heritage craft, recognized by India's Geographical Indication Act, is renowned for its strength. Each piece requires patience and skill to create these delicate nail patterns which stay firmly on the cloth until intentionally dislodged during festive performances. It stands as a testament to traditional Indian craftsmanship and cultural expression, preserving ancient techniques while speaking through evocative imagery captured in embroidered form.
