
Navalgund durries are a special type of handloom floor covering, traditionally made from cotton in India. The name 'Navalgund' literally translates as 'New Cotton', and indeed, these are distinguished by their richly coloured prints achieved with natural dyes directly onto the fine muslin-like fabric.

Originating primarily from coastal regions like Mangalore and Kasargod, often in Karnataka or Kerala respectively depending on regional variations, Navalgund work holds a unique place. It's not just about weaving; it involves intricate hand printing – predominantly block printing, though techniques vary – where colourful patterns are stenciled onto the warp before warping itself to form the finished width.

The ideal material is fine, soft-kattha cotton – often imported 'white cotton' (bhindi kaathei), giving a smooth texture. This fabric allows vibrant colours from vegetable dyes used for motifs like flowers, animals, geometric symbols, or peacock patterns, created with techniques including bandhani (tie-dye) and kanshi printing.

Differing slightly from other Andhra Pradesh floor coverings in their more printed aesthetic compared to Kanjee Kurum's block prints alone, Navalgund durries often showcase large-scale colour work alongside smaller detailing. They offer a beautiful, tactile blend of craftsmanship – both the precise handloom weaving and sophisticated free-hand printing – creating durable yet versatile surfaces for floors or furniture that echo traditional design combined with contemporary appeal in accessible formats.
