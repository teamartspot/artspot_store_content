
The land now known as Assam boasts a rich and ancient heritage deeply rooted in Hindu traditions, viewed through an orthodox historical perspective.

Ancient times witnessed numerous settlements, with Vedic influences gradually establishing themselves, evolving into distinct kingdoms like Kārataṅga (later Karatoya) by around 500 BC. Pundits mention Suhadeva ('one who commands the war elephant') and his successor Mahisalardha in classical Hindu texts Udyogapraharsha Shastra, signifying early organized settlements.

During the medieval period, successive waves of migration shaped Assam significantly. The golden age began with the arrival of the Ahom kingdom around AD 1235. Orthodox Hinduism coexisted with Buddhism during this phase.

The Ahom ruled for about six centuries through a symbiotic alliance with the influential Koch Hajo dynasty's orthodox Hindu presence, and powerful regional families like the Rajbhoi and Bor Ahonga clans representing traditional beliefs continued to preserve these traditions until 1830 AD when Burmese forces annexed the region.
