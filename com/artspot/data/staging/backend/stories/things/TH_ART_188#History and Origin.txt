
Originating from the erstwhile princely state of Jaleshpur in Madhya Pradesh, India (now part of Chhatarpur district), Metal Craft or Dhatu Shilp represents a specialized form deeply integrated into Pattachitra art. Its history resonates profoundly with the region's cultural and religious milieu.

Before gaining prominence during the Mughal era when Muslim artisans influenced Hindu artists in Bundelkhand, the craft thrived locally as part of traditional temple icon making. Artists would forge intricate metallic frames using copper or brass sheets interwoven like wire mesh, creating preliminary skeletons for statues, particularly deities and Lakshmi-Kirita pairs (goddess Lakshmi with four arms holding gold coins).

The Mughal period saw the adoption and refinement of this technique in Jaleshwar. Small yet detailed metal frames became highly sought after as components for gifting high-ranking officials or royalty during temple festivals at places like Orchha. These intricate creations added significant value.

Today, while fewer artists employ it for full figurine production compared to painting, Dhatu Shilp remains a testament to the bygone era of skilled artisanship in Jaleshpur dedicated to crafting luxurious metal components essential for temple idol embellishment and royal display during religious celebrations.
