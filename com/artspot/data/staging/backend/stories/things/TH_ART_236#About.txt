
Zardozi often refers to intricate *metallic thread* embroidery heavily associated with North Indian fabrics like silk. While not exclusively 'Bareilly', the city's name, along with nearby areas such as Bulandshahr and Baghpat, strongly defines this particular style as the "Bareilly Zari Zardozi".

At its heart is luxurious gold (*meena*) or silver thread (often finer *bajra*). This metallic fabric weft works are carefully designed onto a base fabric, usually heavy silk (*dhamiyat*), creating shimmering designs. Gold creates more of a sheen and gleam effect, while fine silver threads offer delicate sparkle.

The process combines meticulous hand-stitching to outline patterns, followed by the careful application of the metallic yarns. This technique results in breathtakingly detailed work: tiny flowers (*moti-bahar*), ornamental vine motifs (*rashmi-kunda*), intricate floral scrolls (particularly favoured for wedding lehengas and suits), complex animal figures (roosters, elephants decorated like palaces), or elaborate borders. The pieces often serve as decorative accents on *paarkhas*, *chikaras*, *ghanas*, turbans (*pagri*) worn by men, stoles (*udwaria*), handkerchiefs (*rakhis*), dupattas (*pallus*), and palazzos.

The appeal lies in its sheer display of craftsmanship and the permanent radiance it adds to any garment or textile. It speaks volumes about the wearer's taste and financial status, making pieces like these ideal gifts for special occasions requiring elegance and traditional opulence.
