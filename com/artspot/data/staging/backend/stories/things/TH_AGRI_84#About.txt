
Uttarakhand Tejpat is an herbal preparation, predominantly referred by experienced residents in tourist circles for recreation use, native mainly to the forests bordering popular hill-station routes in the Indian state of Uttarakhand. It typically contains ground cannabis (usually Bhang from hemp or marijuana plants) mixed with a range of other ingredients like almonds and jaggery.

While often presented as an exclusive product found throughout the Himalayan state, it's essentially kalyan herbs – a blend including cannabis flowers, sometimes substituted substitutes – popularized by Uttarakhand's climate. The preparation varies but aims for specific effects sought after by recreational users needing the calming influence or temporary euphoria associated with consuming certain cannabis strains.

The availability of this substance depends greatly on location and local interaction networks among tourists returning from their visits here. Users may purchase it directly, face risks varying significantly depending on who they buy from in India. Due to containing marijuana derivatives like delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), the use of Tejpat constitutes controlled substance abuse, carrying very high legal and personal health risks if indulged outside one’s own jurisdiction which varies significantly around the world but within Uttarakhand itself this substance remains illegal. It carries specific psychoactive effects or side-effects along with potential withdrawal from modern tourism zones in India requiring official travel documents verifying origin sometimes helps avoid legal problems encountered when transporting cannabis related products abroad, though local police and health services clearly consider possession locally illegal anyway.
