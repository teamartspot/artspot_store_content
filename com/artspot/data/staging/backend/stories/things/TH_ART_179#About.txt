
**Jabalpur Stone Craft**

Hailed from ancient times, the stone craft of Jabalpur is a quintessential expression of Madhya Pradesh’s artistic legacy. Flourishing particularly around temples built during the medieval periods, notably under King Dhamodar Dev of the Kalchuri dynasty and later rulers like Chandra Giri Singh, this craft evolved in mastery over centuries.

Traditionally executed from sandstone, the intricate carvings in Jabalpur reached a zenith not only in India but also earned global recognition. UNESCO and the Government of Madhya Pradesh have jointly affirmed its status as an Intangible Cultural Heritage associated with specific regions near Jabalpur. The characteristic style features bold designs adapted into rounded forms, reminiscent perhaps of early influences or spontaneous innovations.

Key examples showcase this unique aesthetic: floral motifs twist like living vines; animal figures, especially Nandi (the bull), are exquisitely rendered, often as a diminutive pet stood beside its master sculptor alongside intricate scenes from Hindu mythology and temple iconography.

Though sandstone remains central to its original identity, modern practitioners have skillfully adapted to carve fine details in other materials, notably marble. The iconic Jabalpur headrest or *ghunghton* (a carved plank) used beneath royal Bedni gola chairs is a primary product showcasing its refined linear work and floral embellishments.

Today, it stands as evidence of evolving stone carving techniques developed over generations for sacred purposes yet cherished globally for its distinct visual language.
