
Kasuti is a unique handloom craft originating from coastal Andhra Pradesh and parts of Odisha. Primarily executed on cotton fabric (and sometimes silk) found throughout India.

The technique resembles Japanese Shibori due to its wax-resist method, but often uses chitrakarai blocks instead of beeswax for initial colour application or as masks while the main resist work is done. The core technique involves intricate knotting rather than embroidery stitches like Pashima and Chikka.
Kasuti features complex multi-coloured knot-based geometric patterns that include pictorial elements, flowers, animals, mythological figures, gods, demigods, and deities drawn from Hindu mythology and Puranic stories. Its defining characteristics are fine detail (except in certain structural stitches), vibrant colours often contrasting strongly yet blending harmoniously at junctions where threads meet, excellent tension making the fabric stronger than usual handloom cotton weaves, and resilience to fading.
Common motifs include intricate designs like flowers on petals-of-a-flower stems or 'Pallakonda's Gold Piece' – which translates as mountain stripes of gold – although variations exist across regions. The primary applications for this craft are dupattas (long scarves) and sarees worn by women, particularly during significant religious ceremonies.

Today, Kasuti is considered one of the finest silk crafts from Andhra Pradesh, alongside Pashima Silpi and Chikka Zarika. Its global recognition through geographical indicators helps promote its preservation against fading craft traditions in India despite its increasing popularity and appreciation by designers globally.
