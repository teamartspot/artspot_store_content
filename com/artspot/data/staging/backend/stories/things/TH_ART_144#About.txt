
Bandhani is an ancient Indian folk art form predominantly practiced around Jamnagar in the Latur district of Maharashtra. The term itself translates to "tying," which perfectly describes its core technique. Traditionally used for creating intricate designs on fabrics, it involves a simple yet masterful manual process.

Artisans create patterns by knotting wax thread onto cloth before dyeing it with natural colours like reds and dark greens derived from karanj/olive tree flowers or other sources. Once the design is outlined with wax resist knots, the fabric goes through multiple dye baths, sinking all parts colour except where the knot pattern dictates a different hue (or no colour). After dyeing, the excess thread is carefully removed by boiling or steaming, often revealing unexpected shades or white areas if natural dyes weren't used purely.

In this art form, 'Bandha' specifically refers to the tying knots with wax prepared from ingredients like cow milk and gum tragacanth. In other parts of India, Bandhani might also refer simply to resist dyeing on fabrics without necessarily specifying the block-printed pattern origin.

This technique is not limited to textiles alone; it serves as a foundation for 'Mundu Rangoli' too. Jamnagri Bandhani showcases incredible skill and patience given its complexity, producing stunning geometric motifs unique to this region while preserving the rich heritage of Indian craftsmanship across generations.
