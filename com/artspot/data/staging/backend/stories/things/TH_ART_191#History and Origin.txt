
Nestled among the arid expanses of Rajasthan lies Bikaner, a city distinguished not only by its majestic fort (Laxmi Niwas) and historical significance but also by its rich silk weaving heritage, known locally as Usta Kala. This traditional craft evolved from older textile traditions in the Mewar region and solidified its unique identity under the patronage of Bikaner's royal family during periods like that of Maharaja Avantik Singh.

The term 'Usta' is particularly telling; it signifies a highly respected master craftsman, an honour befitting those skilled weavers who dedicated their lives to mastering intricate designs on silk. The origin is closely linked with the textile trade routes and the enterprising spirit of the Marwaris (Jaimalidas), known for introducing vibrant motifs into Bikaner's weaving landscape.

Bikaneri weaving is characterized by its bold, geometric patterns ('Aj') against sheer or dark weft ('rufs'), creating striking visual statements. It utilizes luxurious materials like pure silk and golden zari threads often combined with colourful dyed fabrics (brocade). This unique combination reflects the royal history and local aesthetic of Bikaner.

The craft gained official recognition when it was inscribed as 'Bandhej' – a specific silk jacquard technique – by India's Ministry of Textiles in 1982. It stands today as a symbol of Bikaner's artistic legacy, with contemporary weavers continuing to draw inspiration from ancient masters while catering to global tastes.
