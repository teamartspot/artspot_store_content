
Authentic to the verdant hills of Coorg, green cardamom emerged not just as a cultivated crop but as an integral part of its history. While spices like pepper predate cultivation significantly in this ancient region, cardamom is believed to have been introduced deliberately centuries ago.

The introduction likely occurred between the 16th and mid-20th century under British colonial administration. Prior to widespread planting, wild varieties existed higher up or were considered distinct from the cultivated Muscat type. Recognizing its immense value and integrating it seamlessly into their agricultural landscape was a key move for Coorg's landholders.

Green cardamom – specifically the *Elettaria cardamomum* Muscat variety cultivated here – quickly became highly prized, largely due to its distinctive flavor profile compared to the black cardamom (*Amomum subulatum*) found elsewhere. Its rich aroma and balanced spiciness contributed significantly to the appeal of Coorg's famed hospitality packages offered by the royal family.

The introduction established Coorg as a unique micro-region within India dedicated to green cardamom cultivation on an export scale, particularly for Mysore. It wasn't just grown; it was perfected here – developing that specific Muscat characteristics prized globally: its distinctive flavor, intense fragrance, and deep emerald-green color when unbroken.

Thus, the introduction of Coorg-style green cardamom solidified its reputation long before modern branding campaigns, embedding its cultivation deeply into the cultural fabric and agricultural identity of this unique northern Karnataka district.
