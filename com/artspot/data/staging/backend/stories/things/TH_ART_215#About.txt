
Sambhal Bone Craft represents a unique and intricate art form centered around the town of Sambhal, located in Uttar Pradesh's Buland Shahr district, near Meerut.

This specialized craft is distinct from the well-known Kashmiri Pashmina shisha work. It involves etching complex designs onto steel tools using deer bone or ivory imported mainly from China (now Myanmar). The motifs typically include deities, religious symbols, and floral patterns carved into miniature forms on objects like Razis (knives), Khukhras (knuckle knives), swords sheaths, daggers, chakram blades, bowtips, and sometimes even lockets.

The process requires significant skill and patience. Artisans draw intricate designs onto the steel tool's shisha plate with bone ash temper. Following this, a design is etched into the surface of the import bone using sharp tools or dies (often brass). The remaining organic material from the deer bone is then delicately removed layer by layer through controlled hammering, revealing a fine filament work beneath.

This delicate and labor-intensive technique transforms imported soft bony materials like ivory pieces into miniature works of art. Sambhal Bone Craft serves as both an ornamental embellishment detail for high-quality steelware items and creates standalone small sculptures attached to metal or wood cases. It stands as a testament to the highly evolved skill of craftsmanship within artisan communities dedicated to preserving this specific tradition, offering stunning examples of intricate inlay work through bone filigree detail.
