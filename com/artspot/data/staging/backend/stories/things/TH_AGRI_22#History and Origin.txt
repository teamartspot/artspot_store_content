
Nestled in the fertile valleys of Kerala, India's southwestern Malabar region lies a culinary gem: Chittoorikallu Matta, locally renowned as Palakkadan Matta. Its story begins deep within the historical context of the Zamorin's rule over the northern Malabar coast.

This aromatic rice is traditionally grown as an integral part of specific landscape management systems unique to this area known for its hilly terrain and streams. Rather than large-scale plantations, it was historically cultivated, often mixed with smaller grains like barnyard grass (Jwar), perhaps requiring collective farming efforts in traditional settings. It thrived under conditions that discouraged commercial monoculture, favouring ecological balance.

The distinct flavour, attributed to the unique combination of soil types – rich black cotton earth interspersed with volcanic red laterite – and river water irrigation from tributaries like Chitoor Puzha (which lends its name), solidified its reputation. But more than just taste, traditional practices surrounding its collection shaped local identity.

For generations, farmers maintained specific plots designated for Matta rice varieties mixed within their fields alongside other grains. The harvest wasn't entirely private; often, a portion was 'claimed' or considered part of the landscape itself, reinforcing it as a common heritage aspect associated with the land and people of Palakkadan (Chittoorikallu taluk).

Today, however, its cultivation faces challenges. Much of the traditional mixed cropping has yielded to monoculture in modern times due to changing agricultural patterns driven by increased demand for specific rice types or shifts towards intensive farming practices focusing on high yields rather than heritage land varieties.

Yet efforts by individuals and organisations are underway to revive the pure Palakkadan Matta rice, preserving its unique terroir connection. It stands today not just as a flavour profile from an ancient soil mixture, but also as a link preserved in the collective memory of farmers trying to sustain this distinctive agrarian tradition in the face of contemporary agricultural demands and pressures against traditional practices.
