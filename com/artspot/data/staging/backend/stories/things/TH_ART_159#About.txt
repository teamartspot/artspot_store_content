
Karuppur Kalamkari represents a vibrant form of South Indian textile art, specifically originating from the Karuppur village region in Tamil Nadu's Tiruchirappalli district.

With roots stretching back centuries to temple paintings (Kalams), it employs sophisticated patterns and techniques directly on hand-spun cotton fabric. The hallmark features include bold geometric shapes contrasting with intricate narrative scenes. A block of paste is used, along with betel leaves, as a 'resist' by daubing wax or other substances onto the cloth before applying bright indigo dyes. Intricate patterns are then carved directly into this resist-coated fabric.

This complex art process creates visually arresting designs often depicting stories from Indian mythology, particularly those of Lord Rama and Lord Krishna – Vaishnavite themes being prominent due to its Siva temple roots fading over time with shifts in patronage towards regional gods. Karuppur itself became the epicentre of flourishing block-printing workshops dedicated to intricate resist patterns.

While similar techniques exist elsewhere along India's east coast, including those that later evolved into Hyderabadi and Pattachitra styles, Karuppur Kalamkari maintains its distinct characteristics and local traditions, showcasing a rich heritage in textile craftsmanship.
