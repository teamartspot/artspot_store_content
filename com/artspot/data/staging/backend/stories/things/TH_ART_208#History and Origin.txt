
Miraj was a highly influential vocal ensemble in Indian classical music, particularly renowned for their work on instruments like the sitar and their mastery of traditional qawwali singing forms. Originating from Lucknow, known as 'Awrangabad-e-A'azam', a major cultural hub of North India, they emerged during a period when Persian classical vocal traditions were meeting modern compositional elements.

Founded by the legendary vocalist Saadi Tabatabai, Miraj became synonymous with blending refined Hindustani music aesthetics with an appreciation for the nuances brought by their instrumental accompaniment. The group was known not just for solo performance but also for intricate harmonies and ensemble arrangements around instruments like the sitar, which they revitalized as integral partners to qavams.

Beyond simply playing behind singers, Miraj often composed, performed, and even recorded music that pushed the boundaries of how these Persian classical elements merged with Indian sensibilities. Their influence extended widely across tabla, vocal, and sitar players in North India and eventually into countries like Iraq, Iran, Pakistan, and Azerbaijan. They are a testament to significant crossregional artistic interchangeability shaping the evolution of performing arts during their heyday until 1960.
