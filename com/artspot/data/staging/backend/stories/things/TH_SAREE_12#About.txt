
Artisans from India's scenic Kashmir Valley are said to have created this special handcrafted art form, which is now connected with Jammu region's Chamba village.

These beautiful Sarees feature the same rich colours and intricate, multi-layered woven patterns as the original Chamba Rumals. They use pure silk sourced locally in Chamba, along with vibrant naturally dyed colours from plants around the area – saffron, marigold, kohl (a type of plant used for black dye), indigo blue, red myrobalan and yellow turmeric.

Inspired by nature's bounty surrounding Jammu and Kashmir's mountains and valleys, artists employ techniques like double and triple warping to create complex designs. Borders often echo the iconic floral motifs found on traditional Chamba Rumals themselves, such as 'Chinar' leaves or apple blossoms.

Made directly using a handloom rigid wooden loom, these woven pieces offer remarkable warmth even during winter's initial chill. They feature an all-over pattern across the entire length and breadth of the cloth rather than just borders – the entire 5-6 metre piece showcases repeating motifs in varied colours to suit designs like 'Amrit Nishan' or royal scenes.

Chamba Rumal Sarees are a true marvel, showcasing the deep-rooted tradition and exceptional creativity that makes India's rich textile heritage sparkle.
