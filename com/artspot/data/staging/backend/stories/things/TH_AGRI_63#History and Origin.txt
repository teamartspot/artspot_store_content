
Arunachal Orange, a term deeply connected to the North East state of Arunachal Pradesh in India, owes its unique character largely to colonial planters. The story began centuries ago when oranges from other parts were introduced here. A pivotal moment was during an experimental trial near Itakhpu around 1925, where certain groves not only survived but thrived.

These orange trees slowly found their footing amidst the lush, cool terrains of Arunachal Pradesh, gradually adapting to this unique terroir. The state's varied altitudes provide the crucial 'cool desert' climate – warm days interspersed with cold nights sufficient for low-intensity cultivation suitable mostly below 2000 meters.

Cultivation expanded thereafter, largely driven by the initial British experiments and subsequent state government initiatives encouraging commercial horticulture among tribes and settlers alike. The Arunachal Orange we know today is predominantly a result of this gradual adaptation process over time rather than direct promotion by foreign powers, although they played a crucial role in its introduction.

Over decades, through careful selection and cross-breeding alongside traditional propagation methods, the oranges cultivated here developed distinct characteristics – typically medium-sized fruits with juicy pulp, rich orange aroma, and a zesty yet mild flavour. The unique terroir continues to be Arunachal Orange's main asset today.
