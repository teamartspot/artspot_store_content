
Bell Metal Wares of Datia and Tikamgarh represent some of India's finest traditional metalwork, particularly known for intricate creations primarily in an alloy called 'bell metal' or sometimes referred to as *Shalagram*. This material is predominantly copper with additions of tin and lead.

Datia, located in Madhya Pradesh, stands out as the preeminent centre. Its artisans produce exceptionally detailed figurines such as Lord Ganesha's iconic elephant form, intricately cast deities, vibrant mythological scenes like Ramayana or Mahabharata panels, exquisite temple bells, utensils (though usage is limited now), and boxes. These items are vital for religious rituals (*puja*) and hold significant cultural value.

Tikamgarh in Uttar Pradesh offers a fascinating regional variation under the same broad category of bell metal artistry alongside Datia's dominance for overall figurine work. While also skillfully working with this alloy, Tikamgarh’s creations often feature distinct motifs and finishes, adding to India's diverse handicraft tradition.

The hallmark is undeniable: exquisite detail achieved through advanced lost-wax casting techniques, historically developed in the Chandel kingdom period around Datia (citing its royal patronage helps show historical link). This craft showcases remarkable dedication and skill over centuries. Acquiring these pieces often means owning a functional artifact alongside a unique work of art and history.
