
Assam Asharikandi is a unique form of wheel-made pottery prevalent among the Ahir community (often referred to as 'The People of Earth') residing in villages scattered across Assam and surrounding areas. Primarily crafted from locally sourced high-quality kaolin clay, often found near coal deposits deep underground, this terracotta craft forms an essential part of the tribe’s subsistence, complementing their agricultural lifestyle.

While historically focused on creating simple earthenware vessels for daily domestic use like storing water or grinding betel leaves (including the famous *kuttiya sanga* cups), Asharikandi pieces also feature decorative items and figurines. The distinctive motifs are drawn from nature – often depicting animals, utensils, everyday objects, and human figures.

The process involves preparing locally dug clay, shaping it on a potter's wheel using manual techniques passed down through generations, drying the pieces in the sun or fire, and firing them typically outdoors over wood and cow dung cakes. The natural minerals mixed into the clay create beautiful glazes that turn earthy-brown during firing.

Though less commercially prominent today than other terracotta traditions like Orissa's Korba style with its characteristic black outline drawing on rice wash slurry (which heavily influenced it), Asharikandi pottery retains its intrinsic charm and serves as a living testament to the simple creativity found within everyday materials.
