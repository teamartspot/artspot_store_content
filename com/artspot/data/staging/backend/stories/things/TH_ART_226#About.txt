
**Introduction to Sarthebari Metal Craft**

Known as the 'Art of Copper Vessels', **Sarthebari** is one of India's renowned Handicrafts. Originating from the state of Odisha, it stands out within tribal art due to its intricate and distinct geometric designs often reminiscent of Islamic architectural features, though executed using traditional techniques.

Historically rooted in the crafting of essential household items like storage jars (kumbha), water bowls (tela), ladles (khanda), and trays, Sarthebari artisans primarily work with copper. However, fine quality brass and other metals are also commonly used to fulfill client demand for various pieces from home decor to intricate boxes.

The hallmark of Sarthebari lies in its unique decorative style. Distinctive square motifs interlocking into complex geometric patterns form a characteristic 'frame' or pattern around the objects. This involves cutting thin metal plates, typically 0.5 mm thick copper or brass sheets imported primarily from Kanjivembadu near Rameswaram.

Artisans meticulously bend these intricate wire-cut designs over their products using special copper hooks (kundra) and a potter's wheel to form the final shape of the vessel or container. This process demands great skill, precision, and patience – shaping the functional item while maintaining the decorative integrity from within.

Beyond its aesthetic appeal, Sarthebari showcases cultural synthesis. It preserves tribal skills and designs but also incorporates non-lacquer elements influenced by centuries-old Persian motifs integrated into regional architectural elements like those seen in Islamic tile work or patterns popularized during the Indo-Saracenic Revival period prevalent at the time of Chera Kings domination.

Today, while continuing to produce functional items, this intricate craft primarily serves tourists and art enthusiasts. Yet its enduring charm keeps it relevant as a celebrated form of Odishiya *Chitara*.
