
Unique Wayanad rice, locally known as *Gandhakasala* rice or Sulphur Spring rice, has emerged from ancient cultivation methods in the Western Ghats region of Karnataka.

Distinctive for its greyish-black husk and partially cooked grains possessing a characteristic sulphurous aroma, this speciality variety thrives best on lands historically influenced by natural springs rich with sulfur minerals. Cultivated traditionally using organic farming practices that rely on rain-fed streams rather than chemical inputs, the rice derives unique properties from absorption during growth through these mineral-rich soils.

The most notable attribute is its gritty-free nature – the husk naturally breaks away at cooking time without requiring external abrasives, unlike polished varieties like Basmathi or Jeerigase. Its aroma often masks earthy or wild notes until cooked alongside other ingredients. There are whispers that this rice retains a subtle mineral salt character when not washed excessively.

Traditionally linked to specific locales in Wayanad known for sulphurous waters near hills and forests, the health benefits associated with Gandhakasala rice stem directly from its connection to these naturally enriched grounds. While small-scale cultivation exists today often under certification labels, it remains one of India's most unique, earth-linked heritage rices, prized by connoisseurs in Bangalore and beyond for generations due to perceived differences in fragrance and taste.
