
Handmade carpets of Bhadohi stand as a testament to centuries-old artisanal skill and cultural heritage. Originating in the Kaimur Range of the Indian state within Bhadohi district, these intricate floor coverings trace their roots back through Mughl architecture and Persian influences into India itself.

The heart of Bhadohi carpet making lies with weavers referred to locally as Chatriwallas or Dhobi Ghasidas. They traditionally use high-quality materials like silk imported from regions such as Varanasi, and wool often sourced from Mysore. The designs are predominantly inspired by Hinduism (depicting deities, animals) and Islam through the commonality of geometric motifs found across India, reflecting Bhadohi's diverse historical cultural landscape.

A defining characteristic is their unique knotting technique combined with hand-spooling using fine threads onto bobbins placed on a frame. Another notable feature is the way loops are arranged slightly closer to one side of certain knots, known as Dharavi or sometimes Dhurli (distinctive loops creating an 'S' shape visible from above). These carpets were historically characterized by their intricate geometric patterns and often depicted mythical figures alongside flora.

Beyond simple decoration today's Bhadohi weavers create items with cultural significance ranging from decorative wall hangings to special occasion floor coverings all using traditional techniques. The craft is recognized as a Geographical Indication, meaning its authenticity is tied specifically to Bhadohi.
