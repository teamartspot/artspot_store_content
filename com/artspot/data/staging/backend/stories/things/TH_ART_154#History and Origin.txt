
Indian kathi remains deeply embedded in the cultural fabric of Manipur, dating back centuries through its intricate carvings and specific usage within various ethnic communities here. While not exclusively from Madurai (a city in Tamil Nadu), "Madurkathi" often refers to this style which **merged influences** with traditional ritualistic dolls from North-East India, particularly the Androgyne Kathi doll.

These dolls emerged around the fifth century AD among South Indian Jains as tangible representations of Jain Tirthankaras. They served a crucial purpose in guiding villagers – especially non-Jains – to remember these saints correctly upon death and rebirth, symbolizing their forms during important cremation (pyre) rituals. Made from clay or wood, often intricately carved with cloth dressings depicting the saint's attire for specific festivals like Navakalevara or Parinirvana Mela at Pushkar in Rajasthan.

Although evolving differently over regions and eras, the Madurkathi retains deep connections to these ancient Jain traditions born in South India. Its enduring popularity serves as a lasting memory of religious iconography among artisan communities dedicated for generations to crafting it.
