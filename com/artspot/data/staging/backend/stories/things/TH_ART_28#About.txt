
Dhokra is India’s quintessential soft, chewy sweet dumpling, most famously associated with the culinary landscape of West Bengal. Traditionally made from *suji*, a fine batter derived from rice flour or sometimes sorghum (*kutje*) and plain dough paste (maida), it takes its unique name from the hollow stem of the banana plant used as an inner mold during preparation, along with milk to ensure flavor and texture coherence.

The distinguishing feature lies in its cooking method. The mixture is formed into small, dense cakes around this natural cavity or on leaves/nuts placed over copper discs or pans (known as *tawa*). Traditionally cooked by steaming until it loses all water content yet retains incredible softness within a slightly elastic structure. This process imbibes the warmth and subtle fragrance of milk without drying out the delicacy.

Its defining characteristic is unparalleled simplicity tied to exquisite results, offering an intensely sweet burst reminiscent of generations past before commercial sugar flooded its origins. Historically prepared with jaggery *(gurangela)* or palm jaggery *(bisra gurangela*), Dhokra maintained that distinct sweetness even before these became popularized. It remains a cherished part of daily life for people in West Bengal, particularly enjoyed during breakfast (*snacks*) often as an afternoon treat especially loved by children.

Often found alongside other traditional sweets like *sandesh*, which uses butter cream instead of steaming, Dhokra stands out for its unique texture and very straightforward tradition.
