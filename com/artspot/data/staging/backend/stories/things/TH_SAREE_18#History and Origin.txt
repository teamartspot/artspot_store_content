
Muga Silk Sarees have a unique origin and history deeply intertwined with Assam, India. Unlike common silk-producing worms (Bombyx), Muga silk comes from the Tussah silk worm (Antheraea microphthalmus), native to the Brahmaputra valley. These caterpillars primarily feed on Mimosa muga tree leaves ('Mugabur' leaves).

The historical significance of Muga silk sarees is immense in Assam, particularly associated with royalty. According to Manipuri legend, a spider or 'Taka-khaini Khomba' created the first strings by weaving golden raindrops and flowers into bamboo cloth. These stories later evolved during an 'Amara Puraskar,' where Ahom kings declared Muga silk sarees as exclusively for royalty, known as 'Mohanbharatwari' or 'Chera Rishabini Kona.'

Originally worn only by the royal family in Assam due to its exclusive origin and value, its use later spread among other communities under specific rules. It wasn't until much later that designs became more accessible with advancements like Chandra Saree technology.

The distinctive feature of Muga silk is its natural golden hue ranging from pale yellowish-orange to deep 'Kesarendra' gold, unique among all silks globally except Tasar (China). This inherent color sets it apart. Today, the geographical indication for Muga sarees protects this authentic Assamese heritage fabric, ensuring its traditional identity persists despite modern influences.
