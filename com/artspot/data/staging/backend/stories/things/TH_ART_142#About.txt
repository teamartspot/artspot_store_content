
Banaras Metal Repousse is a quintessential art form deeply woven into the cultural tapestry of Varanasi (Benares), emerging primarily from the city's predominantly Buddhist communities. It represents an ancient, intricate layering technique specific to enamelled metalwork.

The process involves crafting detailed designs in relief on one side of copper sheets by working *from underneath*. Artisans shape a solid wax pattern into its reverse surface, then enclose this with a thin sheet of copper or other metal, leaving small air vents. Molten wax is poured over the back to fuse everything and seal the vents, trapping the elaborate design inside but keeping it distinct from the base metal.

Often focusing on Hindu deities, geometric patterns, stylized flowers, or abstract motifs, intricate details are chiseled onto the raised wax surface before encasing, appearing as facets set against a dark metallic ground once the fused enamel and base copper are removed. This distinctive, embossed texture with the enamelled background layer offers visual interest.

The craft typically uses vibrant colours like sky blue, yellow gold, ruby red, emerald green, rose pink, or deep grey for enameling. Traditionally made small items known as *thambooli* (coin purses), pins, bangles (*kansh bangles*) and sometimes larger decorative pieces are sold in the narrow lanes of Dashahindu Brahmpuri near the Kashi Vishwanath Temple.

Mastering this complex multi-stage process requires years of dedicated craftsmanship. Each piece is a testament to patience, precision, and dedication, preserving a vital aspect of Varanasi's rich cultural heritage as well as its artistry in contemporary craft marketpieces.
