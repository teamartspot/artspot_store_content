
Alleppey, nestled along the serene stretches of Kerala's backwaters, earned its moniker "Cardamom Capital" due to its legendary green cardamom. This tiny yet distinct seed pod is far more than just a spice; it's an integral part of Alleppey's agricultural heritage and identity.

Situated in the Malabar region, particularly famous for places like Perunalkkodai near Alleppey town, the cardamom industry thrived. The transformation from pepper cultivation to green cardamom farming by Dutch traders in the late 18th century proved decisive. Today, Alleppey remains synonymous with this aromatic treasure, producing a significant majority of India's premium spice.

The uniqueness of Alleppey Green Cardamom isn't just its place of origin. It refers primarily to cardamom cultivated near Alleppey town and distinct from other high-grade Malabar cardamoms like those from Kaduthurukkai or Kozhikode. Its flavour profile is often noted as slightly more pungent with a delightful cooling aroma.

The key characteristics lie in the specific soil composition of the Alleppey region – rich, humus-laden laterite soil perfectly draining – and the local cultivation practices that ensure vibrant green pods bursting with aromatic oils when ripe and hand-picked. These pods are essential components in numerous Kerala dishes, from flavouring fish curry (Karimeen Polunnu Vettiyappuvaan) to enriching traditional Chai blends.

Beyond culinary uses, Alleppey Green Cardamom finds its way into Ayurvedic formulations, valued for its digestive and respiratory benefits. For connoisseurs seeking a high-quality spice renowned both locally and nationally for its distinctive character from the heartland of Malabar's cardamom belt, it remains an exquisite choice.
