
The Cheriyal Scroll, known also by its old names "Cheriga Ellam" or the 'Ochre Coloured Parchment', is a vibrant form of traditional Indian scroll painting hailing from Andhra Pradesh in South India. Typically depicting narrative scenes, often mythological, spiritual, literary, and legendary tales, these scrolls were crafted on palm leaf barks (*cherigapottu*).

A distinguishing feature is the background; unlike many other regional paintings using white or light backgrounds with dark outlines, Cheriyal artists fill vast areas of their work with a bright ochre hue. This unique technique draws focus to human figures and animals rendered in darker shades – predominantly black for humans and brown for elephants or cows.

The intricate details within the colourful ground showcase remarkable artistry. The storytelling element is paramount; a single scroll can sequentially narrate different episodes through well-executed compositions, allowing for prolonged performances. This medium served as a portable canvas during temple festivals (*visarjanas*) before modern media became prevalent.
 
Now recognised for its unique aesthetic and cultural significance, preserving Cheriyal Scrolls stands out as an important task to keep this living art tradition alive.
