
Uttarakhand's vibrant culture includes a unique form of local wrestling contested during harvest festivals and community gatherings across regions like Almora, Champawat, and Bageshwar: the Thulma.

This traditional sport goes beyond mere combat; it represents strength, honor, and playful rivalry. Kumaoni wrestlers perform distinct moves called 'dhundhu dandhu' or similar regional terms, emphasizing agility alongside brute force within a well-defined set of rules observed even today.

Wrestling prowess holds great respect in these communities. It's often passed down through generations but also anyone with innate talent can participate. Matches are typically held barefoot and unarmed in open spaces like courtyards or village grounds, adding to the purity and excitement of the contest.

The Thulma is more than physical strength display; it embodies local heroism and legend. Stories sometimes link its practitioners to folktales involving bravery against mythical beasts or natural elements like mountains themselves. It serves as a celebratory ritual intertwined with dance dramas (like Bhaduri) that depict themes of wrestling contests ('Thulagi').

While deeply rooted in tradition, the Thulma continues to enjoy relevance, often held alongside more formal competitions today, showcasing this enduring folk art for all to appreciate and preserving Kumaoni identity.
