
Kanpur's prominence began much later than its historical reputation in textiles or other trades. Its rise as a center for high-quality leather goods, particularly saddles, is linked to the mid-19th century British Raj. Initially focused on processing raw hides due to proximity and availability (earlier cattle routes often passed through the region), Kanpur gradually developed specialized tanneries and workshops.

The turning point was perhaps in the early 20th century when skilled artisans from places like Delhi or Lucknow migrated, bringing their expertise in leather crafting. Lured by potential growth near smaller units of the Bengal Partition garrison (the establishment began at a time after this major event), these artisans helped transform Kanpur into India's leading saddle manufacturer.

What sets Kanpur aside is its reputation for quality and consistency, which gained traction, especially during periods like World War I when exports surged due to proximity to military centers. The saddlery industry here developed faster because textiles faced heavy taxation initially; this inadvertently shifted focus towards saddles as an important alternative product in the industrial landscape.

Today, Kanpur dominates global leather saddle exports, a testament to its long history of specialized craftsmanship built upon initial processing advantages and consistent quality that resonated internationally throughout the 20th century.
