
The history and origin of leather toys, specifically Kundhal dolls, are deeply woven into the cultural tapestry of cities like Indore and Ujjain during the 18th century Bundelkhand rule, a period when artisanal traditions flourished. These small figures were not just children's playthings but significant folk art pieces created by skilled leather workers known as *Karkhana wale* or craftspeople. The tradition likely began as miniature representations for religious rituals (like depicting Lakshmi Mantra), evolving into playful forms.

Indore emerged as a central hub where distinct styles like Kirwa and Nagari were perfected, representing regional tastes and themes often involving animals or specific figures used in Parsi theatrics and marriage decorations (*Bijli* - bridal set). These intricate miniature leather items demanded exceptional craftmanship, with motifs often painted using natural dyes on hides procured locally.

Historically integral to marital ceremonies (worn by processions), puppet performances (*Ghodalayya*) involving young children, and sometimes kept for decoration or worship (*Shri Kundhal* as Lakshmi's form), their creation represented a blend of storytelling, entertainment, craftsmanship, and cultural expression. Today, they remain cherished creations embodying intricate artistry and the rich historical legacy of these small figures connecting old traditions to modern appreciation.
