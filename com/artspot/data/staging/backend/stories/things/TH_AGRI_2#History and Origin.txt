
The term "Alphonso Mango" evokes a rich history and sensory promise unparalleled by many other tropical fruits. Often cited as originating from the Konkan region of India, particularly Maharashtra (especially the Nashik-Aurangabad corridor) or perhaps even Gujarat/Goa, its association with the legendary King is central to its story.

Legend holds that Sultan Muhammed Shah Tana Bhagat, known as "Sehra Fareesh" and nicknamed 'Shahri Maharaj' among the Marathas, gifted a Persian mango variety to Emperor Alphonso X of Castile. This connection via Portuguese traders and subsequent preference from royalty linked the fruit's name with Indian exports for European palates.

Despite this intriguing human connection, pinpointing its exact original geographical location in India is complex, as various regional origin claims exist alongside botanical classifications that suggest it emerged naturally rather than being introduced whole from Persia ("Dushbo"). Undoubtedly, however, the finest and highest-quality Alphonso mangoes today are cultivated within these specific regions of India.

The name 'Alphonso' itself serves as an effective branding element – a label signaling exceptional flavour. This rich history contributes to why the Marquis variety represents the pinnacle of mango-growing regions globally, largely tied both culturally and commercially to its Indian roots in North Maharashtra.
