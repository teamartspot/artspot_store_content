
The origin of Rajouri Chikri (or often referred to as Rajori Chikankari) is rooted in the artistic traditions emanating from regions near Kashmir, particularly Mehandipur in what was historically Persian territories but culturally overlaps with modern-day Jammu province bordering Himachal Pradesh. These areas became significant centres for intricate Chikankari work, a form previously highly developed and often associated with Lucknow's Mughal legacies.

As Hindu royalty expanded their domains or through trade routes, skilled artisans from these regions migrated eastwards into the Rajouri area in present-day Himachal Pradesh around the late 18th century. They brought their expertise in fine, detailed needlework that created deep relief effects on fabric (Chikankari). Soon, they adapted this magnificent craftsmanship to wooden surfaces, likely initially working with available materials like walnut or rosewood and tools such as adzes, burins, and knives for carving.

This adaptation resulted in Rajori Chikri wood craft – a unique style combining Persian geometric elegance, Islamic floral motifs, and Hindu mythological reliefs. The characteristic intricate pattern work seen in the finest Lucknow Chikankari evolved directly here into sculptural deep carving on solid wood pieces, particularly boxes known as *mehendi ka daant*. These boxes became immensely popular gifts during celebrations like Diwali.

Often created from a contrasting white background surface (usually rosewood or padauk) over which intricate black lines are deeply carved using various tools to achieve fine detail reminiscent of the Persian calligraphy found in some Mehandipur Chikankari pieces, Rajouri Chikri stands as a testament to these artists' skill and adaptability.
