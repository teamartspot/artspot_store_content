
Thanjavur Veena (also known as Tanjore Veena or Veenai) stands as a distinguished musical instrument primarily associated with Carnatic music concerts. Its origin traces back to the early 20th century in the Madurai region of Tamil Nadu, emerging relatively late compared to traditional veenas like the Rudra Veena.

The instrument was conceptualised and crafted by Tiruvarur Baluswamy, a visionary musician and composer born into a family with generations of musical expertise. Recognising the need for an improved fretboard system distinct from Indian classical instruments, he redesigned it based on the melakarta (parent scale) framework developed by his teacher Nalamangatturai Annamalai.

While sharing the Udumpadham body and Adi vinaikkalmargam playing style with other South Indian veenas, the Tanjore Veena's unique feature is its fretboard divided vertically into 'Nottus' sections. Each section represents a melakarta raga group systematising ragas related by scale structure. The fingerboard below allows switching between these sections effortlessly.

Beyond providing technical precision through its well-defined Nottus, the Tanjore Veena serves as a vital aid in complex Carnatic performances, particularly aiding singers navigating intricate Ragam Tanpavithram compositions or challenging Pallis. It has become an indispensable tool for musicians and a celebrated feature at concerts dedicated to South Indian classical music.
