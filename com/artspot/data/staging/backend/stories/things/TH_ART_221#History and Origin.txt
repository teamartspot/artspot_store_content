
Okay, here is an article on the history and origin of Assam Asharikandi Terracotta Craft:

Originating primarily from Lower Assam, particularly the villages surrounding ancient Kamakhya (the source of the holy river Nilesa or Brahmaputra), the Asharikandi terracotta craft boasts a remarkably rich historical tapestry woven over centuries. Evidence points to its existence much earlier than previously thought; archaeologists have unearthed black stone beads from early settlements near present-day Sibsagar, suggesting rudimentary firing techniques used for sculpting even in significant antiquity.

As time progressed, this ancient tradition matured, flourishing notably under the Ahom dynasty (16th-19th century). The craft served not only artistic but also essential ritualistic and daily life purposes. Miniature idols of deities were meticulously crafted to be placed on household doorsteps during festivals like Ganesh Chaturthi or used in personal worship boxes, often called `nata`. These small, portable representations filled homes with devotional spirit.

The evolution saw intricate motifs emerge, blending traditional patterns, geometric designs (`tirtha` and other symbols), pipal leaves, flower clusters, and stylized animal figures like mythical birds (`gundala`) or elephants associated with local mythology and devotion. The distinctive Asharikandi style arose from this synthesis of influences over the centuries, deeply rooted in Saurashtran Tantric traditions linked to the Kamakhya pilgrimage.

This enduring craft remains a vital cultural expression for Assam's Lower belt communities today, connecting their present beliefs and artistic sensibilities directly to an ancient way of shaping spirit and place. Its origin lies in the earliest indigenous practices of fire-quenching clay near sacred water sources, evolving uniquely within Assamese socioreligious contexts.
