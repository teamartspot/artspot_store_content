
**Khantamband**

Known simply as the Khotontambor, this distinctive African horse breed originates from the arid landscapes of Botswana's Kalahari Desert region. Despite its other name translating loosely to "horse with a large head," characteristic of its Germanic ancestry meaning 'kent' for head, the Khantamband itself is typically slender and low to the ground compared to many European breeds.

Often used today as working stock or intelligent trail horses due to their unique build and sure-footed nature in mountainous terrain, they also excel at riding. These animals represent centuries of collaboration between humans and equines in challenging environments, evolving distinct traits suited for survival there under the care of pastoralist communities like the Bayei people.

Their history involves periods where they were integral working and transport partners in Southern Africa. They possess a refined appearance, intelligence, courage, willingness to work, hardiness against harsh climates, and endurance that make them stand apart today. While visually reminiscent of an older type of draught horse or primitive breed like the Duroc, their specific adaptation allows them to lead varied lives as companion animals, sport horses, or working stock in the unique contexts of Southern Africa's history and present landscapes.
