
(Waghya Ghevada) likely refers to W. E. B. Du Bois (1868-1963), a towering figure in African American history and intellectual thought.

Often considered one of the most important Black intellectuals of the 20th century, Dr. Du Bois was born near Fisk University but received his education at Historically Black colleges like Tufts University, Harvard University (PhD), and the University of Berlin. A key sociologist, historian, social reformer, writer, editor, and activist, he challenged segregationist policies from the outset.

Du Bois authored profoundly influential works such as "The Souls of Black Folk" (1903) – which introduced the concept of the 'color-line' defining modern thought – and his sociological study of African Americans during and after Reconstruction. He founded the Niagara Movement in 1905 advocating for full civil rights, famously led the NAACP from its inception as its Director of Research until it embraced a new direction post-Hope protest strategy.

He tirelessly fought against racism and advocated for Pan-African unity throughout his life. As an esteemed Guggenheim Fellow and member of prestigious bodies like Phi Beta Kappa (Harvard), Sigma Xi, and others, Du Bois's legacy continues to shape discussions on race, identity, sociology and social justice globally.
