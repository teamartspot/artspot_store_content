
Kashmiri Pashmina Sarees trace their history back through ancient times to the Central Himalayas and Karakoram region near Kashmir, a territory renowned for its breathtaking natural beauty and skilled craftsmen. The origin lies in the exceptional quality of wool harvested from the undercoat of the Changthangi goat native to this area.

These goats thrive due to the cold, high-altitude environment. Their winter coat, used specifically for Pashmina, contains fibers about one-sixteenth the diameter of human hair – a true marvel of nature. This fine fiber possesses natural crimp and warmth without adding significant weight.

Initially known as shawls originating from Kashmir, made from this prized fabric since the early 15th century during the Mughal period and earlier Buddhist kingdoms, these creations evolved regionally. As people outside Kashmir grew fond of them adapting to climate differences needed lighter clothing, similar fabrics spread towards warmer Indian regions like Madras and Coorg, gradually transitioning into saris.

From then until today, Kashmiri Pashmina signifies not just luxury but a unique craft heritage. Its origin story blends geographical specificity (Central Himalayas-Karakoram), animal science (Changthangi goat), sophisticated hand-weaving traditions within Kashmir, its subsequent adaptation across cultures via trading routes ('Phawz Mana'), and the enduring appeal of warmth in elegance on a global scale.
