
Bilbil, the common name for several trees including Avicenna orientalis (Persian lilac), Saurauia guyanensis (Wild Coffee), and Syzygium cumuniifolium (Indian Myrtle), bears a fruit widely recognized as the Custard Apple. Despite its varied binomial names and tree origins across regions, the popular moniker 'Custard Apple' refers primarily to *Sterculia setigera* subspecies *lindeni*, colloquially known as Beed or Bued in some parts of India and Southeast Asia.

This tropical fruit is celebrated for its unique custard-like flesh when ripe. The distinctive star-shaped markings on the skin, often visible even before it fully matures, are one of its identifying features. Inside lies a sweet, aromatic pulp that is firm yet creamy – reminiscent of the texture of homemade caramel or chocolate pudding. Its distinct appearance and rich flavour make it a sought-after treat in many tropical regions.

Its nutritional profile includes significant amounts of carbohydrates for energy, along with notable quantities of dietary fiber to aid digestion. Richer than other fruits in vitamin C content when fresh, Beed Custard Apple also contributes antioxidants, supporting overall health during its seasonal availability. The pleasant taste and digestive benefits mean it's a favoured choice for desserts or added flavour naturally into meals.
