
Malda Fazli Mango, affectionately known as 'Fazla' in the local Mymensinghi dialect, has distinct origins separate from other prominent varieties born in Malda. While Malda district itself holds significance for tropical mangoes under colonial administration following its capture by the British East India Company in 1765, the specific cultivar designation Fazli evolved later.

Historically, several excellent strains like Bangalora and Langra existed before the post-independence era. Fazli stands out as an important distinct variety recognized officially between 1976 and 1984 when it was scientifically named 'DUSSEY' but retained its original common name among cultivators in the Gedeel region of Malda.

This golden-orange hued mango marked a special period, from approximately March to May. It swiftly gained fame for its sweet aroma, exceptionally soft flesh, thin juicy skin, and intense tangy flavour – traits celebrated by consumers who eagerly awaited its season. While similar to Langra in colour and taste sometimes, Fazli is cherished specifically during its availability.

Its value extends beyond the plate; it signifies Malda's rich agricultural contribution, a symbol of seasonal abundance for local farmers with unique ripening patterns setting it apart from neighbouring region produce like Allahabad Safed or Karan. The legacy of Fazli Mango anchors Malda firmly in the cultural memory and palate preferences as India’s most exciting pineapple mango season nears.
