
Moradabad Metal Craft represents a vibrant chapter in India's rich artisanal tapestry. For centuries, artisans from the erstwhile Mughal-era city of Moradabad (New Delhi) have specialized in crafting exquisite metalware, particularly known for its intricate silver items and fine enamelled brasswork.

The hallmark of Moradabad craftsmanship lies in its distinct Damier work pattern. This is a unique technique featuring continuous interlocking geometric designs – primarily checkerboard motifs but often including elaborate arabesque patterns too – executed by hammering thin sheets into delicate, raised shapes. The signature zig-zag line weaves through these intricate forms, creating timeless decorative elements.

Beyond Damier, the skilled hands of artisans produce stunning works in hallmark silver utilitarian items like bowls and plates, sophisticated candlesticks, brass lanterns, temple lamps (devar kaaj), candelabras, mirrors, trays, and even minute decorative components. They also create smaller items such as toys and bangles.

The tradition remains deeply rooted, with generations inheriting the craft. The metalwork is not merely functional; it's a form of artistic expression, embodying cultural heritage through its beauty. Moradabad artisans skillfully transform metals into captivating works of art, preserving a unique Mughal legacy that continues to enchant and decorate homes around the world.
