
Kerala Mural Paintings are ancient and vibrant traditions, deeply rooted in religious art found primarily within the state's numerous Hindu temples. Their origin is traced back to around the 10th century CE (post-Gupta period), flourishing significantly between the 12th and 18th centuries under the patronage of Nayakkaras, Kolathiris, and later Travancore royalty.

Distinct traditions emerged in different regions:
*   **Mattancherry School:** Concentrated around the Peruvanoor area near Ernakulam, featuring intricate gilded designs. Artists like Cheriyapillai Govindan Pillai (known as Chempakasseril), Manavaloor Lakshmana Narayanan Pillai, and notably Raman Kutty Nair developed this style.
*   **Chettiar School:** Originating from the region west of Cochin, these murals often feature more stylized figures.

These murals are religiously significant, adorning temple walls (tharavaikallu) to depict scenes from Hindu mythology. Many connect them specifically to the Alvar and Siddhar traditions. Characters with distinctive traditional attire and jewellery are common subjects, believed to be used as 'living pictures' during certain ritual performances (koothupuli).

The technique involves applying fine gold powder over a dark background base coat, often creating intricate border designs later. These masterpieces remain highly regarded decorative arts in Kerala and today form part of the Art Collection at institutions like the Kerala Heritage Museum and numerous private collections worldwide.
