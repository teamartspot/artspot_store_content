
The delicious fruits of Tezpur Litchi, also known as Lychee or Tissue-pur (a nod to its name), are believed to have taken root in the region much earlier than most people realize. While litchis originated from China thousands of years ago, some historical accounts suggest their presence on Assam's soil, and particularly within Tezpur district, dates back centuries.

Many sources point towards a possible introduction during the Ahom administration or even before it in the medieval period. The unique climate here, reminiscent slightly of southern China with enough warmth balanced by distinct rainfall patterns from monsoons and Duars seasons, proved ideal for cultivation. This climate likely allowed litchi plants to thrive when propagated locally.

For generations, Tizuia (Tezpur) cultivators mastered its growth alongside paddy without disrupting natural cycles. The fruit's presence in Assam wasn't just isolated; it contributed to the rich diversity of fruit crops cultivated along tributaries like the Dhansiri, even reaching areas beyond Tezpur within historical times.

Over time, cultivation expanded further up-country and down-valley via traditional Assamese jheels (girds), satisangha (traditional networks traders/travelers). While Assam now boasts litchi regions beyond Tezpur's original core claim, the district has long been recognized as a premier area producing distinctively flavourful Tejapan Lichi fruit within its own unique environmental context.
