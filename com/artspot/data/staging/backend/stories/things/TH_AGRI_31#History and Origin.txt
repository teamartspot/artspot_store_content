
It's difficult to pinpoint a *single* exact origin point for all Malda Laxman Bhog mangoes, as 'Malda' primarily refers to the district they come from in West Bengal, India. However, their most prominent lineage is traced back to Santoshgarh near Malda town itself.

Early mentions of this type can be found in 1902 and 1931 government records from West Bengal (then East Bengal). This variety was meticulously developed through cross-breeding programs by colonial-era horticulturists at the Ekenge Mango Garden, located near Santoshgarh. The cultivators likely used superior local Malda varieties as one parent and a specific imported strain for pollination – often suggested to be related to Langra or another rich-tasting variety.

Laxman Bhog mangoes are distinguished by their deep red blush over yellow flesh ('red stripy'), succulent texture, juicy pulp, and exceptionally intense, sweet flavour. Their unique taste coupled with relatively easy availability in the late 20th century cemented their status as a major Malda specialty alongside cultivars like Banganapalli. Today, Santoshgarh remains one of the most famous locations for fine mangoes.

While other Malda types (like Delsa) exist and share similar environmental origins shaped by monsoon rains and specific soil conditions, Laxman Bhog retains its special market name largely because it was specifically nurtured and cultivated to meet high consumer demand from Santoshgarh onwards.
