
Chengalikodan Nendran is a special, small-sized banana variety primarily grown in the southern Indian state of Tamil Nadu. It traces its origin specifically to the fertile region near Kodanadur around Chidambaram Kovil Tirupur in Dindigul district.

Known by other names like Poondi or Sengalimandai in some parts, this heirloom banana is distinct for its thick skin that boasts an unusual green colouring even on yellow bananas, often tinged with black spots. These pebbly bumps add to its charm, indicating higher antioxidant content within the fruit.

The most striking feature of Chengalikodan Nendran, however, lies inside. The flesh is exceptionally dense and creamy white or pale yellow, setting it apart from many other banana varieties which can be quite soft upon ripening. It's also noted for packing an earthy aroma reminiscent of coffee or soil when sliced open.

This unique combination makes the Nendran a much-loved variety among local consumers. While green bananas are favoured for their starchy nature in dishes like Idli and Dosa batter, ripe Nendrans are prized for fresh eating – particularly valued as snacks (kudam) in Chennai by 'Anna Thogga' shops or simply enjoyed raw. They hold an important place in the regional culinary landscape of Tamil Nadu and South India.
