
Tuljapur, nestled within the Dev Sindhu region of Maharashtra, holds the distinction of being an exclusive Kavdi Maal kshetra. The word 'Kavdi' here refers specifically to Tuljapur’s resident farming community who cultivated unique safflower varieties. For these peasants, agriculture was life itself, governed by nature's rhythm and ancient devotional practices intertwined with it.

The practice of seeking Maal blessings in Tuljapur is deeply historical but born from agrarian necessities. It's a belief rooted in the anxieties faced by farmers preparing for critical harvest times – paddy transplantation (Harir), sowing corn (*Mavni kala*) and flowers, all involving specific mudras (mystic gestures). They prayed not just to deities, but specifically to *Sakthi Dhaanvana*, signifying divine power aiding their agriculture. This connection led to the emergence of 'Kavdi Maal' – a special benediction sought from visiting maal deity charans.

According to lore, this unique aspect began with Devi Bhawani’s visit in April, coinciding with preparations for harvesting kumbhar (pot-shaped safflower), which was abundant locally. Unlike other regions where Parvati is prominent during *Mavni* rituals or harvest seasons otherwise, Tuljapur perceives its blessings as uniquely tied to the Kavdis' specific agricultural cycles and the flower they cultivated – Safflower known as 'gulab kaanj'. This historical connection makes Tuljapur's celebration of maal particularly intimate. The cycle involves praying for new rice preparations at harvest's onset, then again when gathering corn stalks or drying fresh flowers (safflower), seeking divine favor at every stage of *their* agricultural existence.
