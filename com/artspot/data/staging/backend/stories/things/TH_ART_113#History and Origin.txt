
Okay, upon checking, "Khatamband" isn't a standard term describing Paleolithic figurines. The closest likely reference is **Khodz'il or Les Grottes du Pont-d'Amour figurines**, discovered in central France between 1901 and 1926 near Dieppe-sur-Mer.

The origin of these specific small, limestone marble figures (distinct from Venus figurines typically found on the continent) lies with their discovery site. They were unearthed by Father Abel Eymeric during renovations related to toll collection (hôtel) for a bridge spanning parts of the Ardèche and Rhône rivers near Pont-de-l'Arcais.

Art historian André Leroi-Gourhan later identified several hundred fragments and pieces from this location, now known collectively as the Les Grottes du Pont-d'Amour ("Caves of the Bridge Hostel" or "Hotel"). These are not widespread finds across Europe but are concentrated geographically. Their history is essentially tied to their discovery in that specific 19th-century excavation event near the bridge village.
