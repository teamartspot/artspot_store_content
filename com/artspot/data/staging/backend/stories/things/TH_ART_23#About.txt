
Chitara Paintings represent a unique fusion of Rajasthan's vibrant traditional art forms with contemporary narratives, offering a refreshing perspective from classical Bharat Mata jaal inspired works.

Founded by Arshad Masih and Riyaz Naiknawalkha following their participation at Jaipur's Living Museum exhibition in 2014–15, Chitara uses the Gond technique but translates modern stories into its idiom. The artists primarily hail from the rural communities of Jodhpur and Udaipur where Arshad resides; the collective is deeply community-rooted.

Their process blends ancient methods with today’s needs. They maintain traditional mediums while adapting to current art market requirements through dedicated exhibition tours worldwide. Traditional motifs are employed, often in intricate patterns forming backgrounds, reinforcing cultural identity alongside newer elements.

Innovatively, Chitara artists tell diverse stories not of mythology or religious figures traditionally emphasized, but of modern subjects like wildlife conservation (Mela's paintings), women's empowerment (Amisha Devi Jha and Reshma Mehta’s works), rural community life ('Gram' series), businesswomen in villages (Mahila Jaal specific theme – the village cooperative 'Khudiram Sahakari'), and daily struggles. These narratives provide social context.

The style is bold, colourful, and accessible. They avoid direct copying of old masters but use their motifs for conceptual framing alongside modern content executed with confidence. The result speaks clearly to today's audience about pertinent issues while retaining strong traditional roots.

Chitara effectively revitalizes art forms like Khajuraho painting by bringing contemporary sensibilities through skilled female artists, contributing significantly to the art scene.
