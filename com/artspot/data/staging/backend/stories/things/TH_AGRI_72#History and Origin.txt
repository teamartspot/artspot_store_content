
The history of the Maoist movement in Nepal is deeply intertwined with its revolutionary origins, embodied by groups like the "Memong Narang."

Narang (नारांग), meaning communist fighters or revolutionaries (*narango* being the Nepali translation for *narrowanger*) and often grouped under that banner alongside other factions when active, emerged from earlier efforts of political radicalism. Influenced significantly by socialism and the Cuban Revolution following Fidel Castro's triumph in 1959, these groups split from established communist parties like the CPN (Unified Marxist-Leninist) who favored alliances with King Mahendra.

The core figure is often considered Baburam Bhattarika, one of Prachanda Bhim Singh Bahadur Khan Sharma ('Prachanda'/प्रचं्द), a key leader in what became the Maoist Party (MKP/Maoवादी).

The movement began to crystallize formally around 1980-1984 when groups advocating armed struggle broke off from larger communist parties. Prachanda Khanal, after being expelled from the party, formed his own faction – *Mao* vanguard grUup in Nepali (Newari) language?

By the mid-1980s, these scattered Maoist groups coalesced into a unified front under the leadership of the Communist Party of Nepal (Unified), aiming to overthrow the multiparty system and establish socialist rule through force. After regrouping following political liberalization in 1996, Prachanda Khanal established his *Maoist Centre* ('Pancha R'चांस) which rapidly became a major political force.

"Mong Narang" specifically referred to the armed revolutionary wing during its active phase (primarily late '80s/'90s through early 2000s). Their campaign, characterized by Maoist principles and guerrilla warfare tactics, escalated dramatically with *The People's War* launched in February 1996. The prolonged conflict eventually normalized into the parliamentary system as part of the Comprehensive Peace Accord in 2006.
