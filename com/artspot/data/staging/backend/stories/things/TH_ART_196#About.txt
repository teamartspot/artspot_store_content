
Wanchoo wooden craft represents a unique tradition originating from the Arunachal Pradesh region in Northeast India, specifically practiced by members of the Wanchoo community primarily residing around the Lohit River. Its defining characteristic is an exceptionally distinctive style characterized by intricate yet bold woodcarving and vibrant colours.

A piece of locally sourced timber forms the base. Intricate motifs derived from nature – like the human figure, animals (including rare hornbills unique to Arunachal), plants, fruits, and stylized geometric patterns – are carved onto panels or vessels. Sharp chisels known as 'dharu', often combined with rudimentary knives or adzes made from locally available materials like coconut shell sharpener points or iron fragments, are the typical carving tools.

Unlike many fine art carpentry forms focusing solely on aesthetics, Wanchoo craft primarily serves everyday needs and social occasions. Carved utensils function as useful household items, while intricate musical instruments called thali and dama drums, as well as ceremonial objects like masks (often for harvest festivals), hold deep ritualistic or communal significance.

The tradition thrives within the Wanchoo community itself. While historically necessary for survival through its diverse uses – everything from furniture components to agricultural implements decorated with carvings – the craft continues, albeit sometimes challenged by modern alternatives and socio-economic factors affecting these artisans' lives today.
