
Situated by the serene Fateh Sagar Lake in Jalesar village near Udaipur, Rajasthan, lies a vibrant hub of metal craftsmanship renowned as one of India's three great craft centres for metalwork.

Jalesar Dhatu Shilp (Metal Craft) represents centuries of tradition and expertise. The artisans here excel in intricate designs using techniques like repousse (beating metal from the reverse side to create raised patterns), chasing (using tools to create textures on the metal surface while manipulating it from the front), and filigree work, often decorating idols and ritualistic items with elaborate motifs.

Commonly used materials include copper sheets for its malleability, bronze (an alloy of copper: tin) primarily for smaller, more intricate pieces like temple offerings, and sometimes pure gold or gilded brass. The craft predominantly serves deities worshipped in the famous Temples of Tripati – Venkateshwara, Durga, and Padmanabhaswami – highlighting its cultural significance.

Demand peaks especially during festive seasons here when religious paraphernalia for festivals like Diwali is highly sought after by pilgrims. The intricate detailing demanded reflects a deep skill passed down through generations, making Jalesar's metal craft a testament to masterful Indian artisanal talent dedicated primarily to spiritual needs but cherished worldwide for its artistic merit.
