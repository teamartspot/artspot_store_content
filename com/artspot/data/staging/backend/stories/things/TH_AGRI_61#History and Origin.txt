
Bangalore Rose Onion gained its distinctive name from an unexpected twist. Onions of normal size were being produced in regions where it was believed the prized large onions (*Allium cepa* var. *cepa*) originated. Someone, perhaps jesting or playing on a local pronunciation like "Bangaalloori" onion, mistakenly referred to these smaller bulbs instead of their huge counterparts.

Over time, this name simply stuck and became synonymous with these particular small, round white onions found throughout India. Their origin is humble yet widespread during British colonial rule when Indian gardeners were cultivating larger bulb varieties from the Cape of Good Hope. Gardeners often exchanged seeds without proper isolation or cultivated nearby farms extensively. This led to the proliferation of hybrids, resulting in smaller, uniformly bulb-sized onion descendants – the Bangalore Rose Onion's ancestors.

These small onions quickly offered practical advantages: they could be packed and easily distributed across numerous city bazaars from a single source. While technically not native only to Bangalore, especially under this specific name, the regional popularity associated with their cultivation there reinforced the designation "Bangalore Rose." Today, while the name specifically points towards these small, round white onions in India linked to the Bengaluru region's history of gardening and exchange, they are just one example among many hybrids grown widely from that period onwards.
