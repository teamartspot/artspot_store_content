
Sikki Grass Art stands as a vibrant and tangible representation of the Andhra Pradesh people, particularly prevalent in the Rayalaseema region near Ongole. Young women artistes create three-dimensional pictures directly on their backs using stiffened bundles of coarse, dry grass leaves known as 'sikki'. These intricate compositions depict stories from everyday life or Hindu mythology.

The art form requires skill and patience. Artists prepare a base with gum extracted from the *Salabata* tree mixed with rice paste and cow dung mud spread over cloth stretched across bamboo frames on their backs. They then bind stiffened grass leaves into patterns using threads to secure them, covering both intricate designs in traditional attire (like Banjara) and background scenes like chariot palaces or temple gopurams.

Sikki Art is not soft; it's a tactile experience with sharp edges formed by the hardened glue. Vibrant natural dyes on the grass give these images their characteristic boldness, creating detailed figures against simpler backgrounds. These wearable artworks serve as mobile screens for spectators, allowing people-watching while appreciating rich narratives and imagery during festivals or temple gatherings.

It’s an art specific to Andhra Pradesh's identity. Its existence poses a challenge because contemporary audiences primarily appreciate soft forms like embroidery or appliqué when wearing or displaying art on the person. Yet, Sikki Grass Art remains one of southern India's unique and colourful folk traditions, demanding recognition for its distinct form and profound cultural significance as it navigates preservation in modern times.
