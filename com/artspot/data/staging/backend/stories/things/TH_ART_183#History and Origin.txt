
Amroha Dholak is a traditional double-headed frame drum originating specifically from the region of Amroha in Uttar Pradesh, India. It belongs to a family of drums called Dholaks or Amrohi Dholak that are primarily used across Northern India.

These drumheads were traditionally made using natural materials like animal skin, perhaps sourced locally. The resonator head plays as crucial a role as the playing head in generating sound. A distinctive characteristic is that both heads require tuning; each dholak has two specific pitch settings, called 'ta' and 'ti', analogous to tonic (Sa) and antitonic (Pa), essential for producing its unique melodic resonance.

Unlike variations such as the Mridangam from South India (single headed) or Kanjira from Kerala, the Amroha Dholak is barrel-shaped, typically played using palms rather than fingers. Its sound is often compared to that of a tambourine due to both heads contributing rich tones through intricate rhythmic strokes and double strokes.

Today's Amroha Dholak may utilize modern materials for the body – woods vary depending on availability – but it retains its essence as an integral part of North Indian classical music, playing specific melodic phrases traditionally during night concerts.
