
The Pilibhit Bansuri is a long bamboo flute hailing from the Pilibhit region in Uttar Pradesh, India. Its origins trace back to ancient musical traditions, with references dating it potentially as far back as Hindustani classical music's early development phase.

Initially considered a simple rural variant of the common bansuri prevalent across North India (derived from regions like Bhopal and Rajasthan), research suggests Pilibhit Bansuri likely existed much earlier. It might have been influenced by folk traditions from neighboring areas such as Bundelkhand or absorbed elements from older reeded forms played near Ayodhya/Lucknow before their decline.

The flute's journey to prominence began significantly with the work of noted composer Baijnath Misra in the late 19th or early 20th century. He adapted a specific rural Pilibhit bamboo flute into a Surbahar, creating its melodic register and microtones for Hindustani classical music.

This transformation elevated the instrument from a simple village folk sound to a serious vehicle for ragas. Misra popularized it through his compositions. The Pilibhit Bansuri eventually gained recognition not just as its origin point but as a style defined by unique tonal qualities, specific tuning practices (especially the mand, or sharp fourth), and complex glissandos (portamento) developed over centuries in this specific bamboo environment.

Its history is thus intertwined with rural musical expression evolving into a sophisticated form within the classical tradition due to its specific regional characteristics.
