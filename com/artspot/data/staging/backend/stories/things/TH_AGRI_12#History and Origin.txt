
Joha rice is one of Assam's most celebrated indigenous aromatic paddy varieties. Its heartland lies primarily in the fertile slopes of forests in Upper Assam, particularly thriving near Hoollung, Abor, Khasperlimalong, and other hill stations within this region.

This fragrant rice boasts a history spanning over 1500 years, deeply rooted alongside Assam's rich tea garden culture. Its aroma evokes the scent of sandalwood or musk (from where its common name 'Joh'ah', possibly derived from an ancient form of Sanskrit for fragrance), although some believe it also resembles the sweet Joss stick aroma.

Beyond its distinct fragrant character, Joha rice grains possess a unique nutty flavour and exceptionally soft yet non-spongy texture. These characteristics transform dishes cooked with it compared to other rices. Due to its demanding agricultural conditions – requiring cool temperatures, higher altitude (typically above 1200m), acidic soils rich in humus near dense forests, heavy rainfall, and ample sunshine for drying the husk on dikes – cultivation is not widespread.

Despite its premium value, a key feature that draws many price-sensitive consumers away from other aromatic rice types like Basmati or Boranji, Joha rice remains an important part of Assam's agricultural heritage. It continues to be cultivated in select pockets, representing the unique culinary contribution of the region known as the "Queen of rice." Its story is intertwined with the landscape and traditions of North-East India, standing out distinctly among rice varieties worldwide.
