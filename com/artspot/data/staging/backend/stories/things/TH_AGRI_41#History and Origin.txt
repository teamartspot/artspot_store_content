
Often called the sweet orange of India, the pomelo traces its Bangalorean roots back through an unlikely champion: Devanahalli village in Karnataka's Ramanagaram district. While historically renowned for iron ore and later, silk production due to its mulberry cultivation, Devanahalli rose to prominence starting in the 1970s not just for sandstone or textiles.

The breakthrough occurred with farmer Chamarajendharaswami Appanna Hosabettu. In a field meant mostly for other crops, he experimented with large seeds imported from Taiwan and California during that era's global horticultural exchanges and cultivation drives. These seeds brought the giant citrus onto his doorstep.

His innovative spirit led him to actively cultivate these plants in the Devanahalli region's fertile soil under its specific weather conditions – suitable rainfall for young fruits combined with ample sunshine later on, yielding ideal sweetness. Hosabettu understood their potential for commercial growth and taste quality from 'Mysore sweet' oranges he already cultivated.

His success spearheaded a shift: many other local farmers like Venugopal began following suit. By the 1980s-90s, Devanahalli had become synonymous in Bangalore with pomelos, not just locally enjoyed but also sent as fresh produce across India and internationally, establishing its unique place in Indian agribusiness.
