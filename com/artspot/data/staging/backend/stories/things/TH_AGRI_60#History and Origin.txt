
Considered one of India's premier orange-growing regions today, Nagpur was not the original home of this exceptional fruit but developed into its hub under specific historical circumstances. The story begins in the early 19th century with British colonial administration.

Captain Willingdon brought seedlings from various global sources around 1840 to cultivate oranges at Bibi ka Gaon near Nimgaon, inspired by his experiences elsewhere. This marked the initial planting. However, a significant turning point was when William Forsyth sent seeds directly back from London. These were true-to-seed cultivations of a specific Spanish variety known as 'Valencia.'

What set Nagpur apart? The region's Deccan Trap rock formation combined with local rainfall to produce mineral-rich water essential for the orange's quality, complementing the tropical climate needed for its growth cycle and sugar development.

Yet pests were common problems initially. It wasn't until scientific horticultural experiments at the time led to discoveries regarding soil management and better seeds that Nagpur Oranges achieved their legendary status by the early 20th century. The fruit gained significant recognition during this period, solidifying Nagpur's reputation as the world-beating city for growing these superior oranges.
