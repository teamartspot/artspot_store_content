
Mainpuri Tarkashi represents a unique form of traditional puppetry associated with the Mainpuri district in Uttar Pradesh, India. It is not merely a string manipulation art but one where the puppets are flat figures painted on only one side, often opaque black. Performers control these puppets from behind using two or three long threads, much like intricate kite tails, guiding their movements precisely to bring stories to life.

Unlike many shadow puppet traditions prevalent in regions like Rajasthan which use translucent figures, the Mainpuri Tarkashi employs actual hand puppets. These are held close to a light source positioned above and slightly in front of the performer. The flattened painted side directly faces this light, projecting clearly for the audience. Storytelling is paramount, focusing largely on episodes from Hindu epics like the Mahabharata and Ramayana, as well as mythological narratives and various devotional tales.

While not as widely recognized today as other Indian puppetry forms or perhaps even affected by historical misunderstandings regarding its use in film projection during the colonial era <793>, Mainpuri Tarkashi offers a distinct window into regional artistic expression. It requires considerable skill from the performer to weave complex narratives with such seemingly simple puppets, controlled solely by precise hand movements and long threads, making it an interesting example of India's diverse traditional theatre forms where the manipulation method differs significantly from common expectations based on other techniques like rod puppetry or shadow manipulation in places of its common use <195>.
