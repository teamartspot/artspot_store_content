
Assam Pani Meteka is a distinctive form of basket weaving hailing from Sivasagar district, Assam. These intricately designed wicker baskets blend traditional techniques with a unique aesthetic.

Typically crafted by Garo or Khasi tribes, artisans employ selected bamboo strips cut to precise lengths for the base and finer strips (the "metekar" or design pieces) for detailing. Intriguingly, the name itself reveals a characteristic feature – often made from Pandanus plants that float on water ("Pani Meteka"). These floating reeds inspire many motifs featuring geometric patterns applied over the classic lotus shape.

The intricate designs are usually created by skilled weaving of black and red threads (derived locally) onto a natural green or dark brown, sometimes textured basket base. Common themes include floral compositions, stylized animal figures, abstract geometrics, but those inspired by water lilies hold special significance in Assamese artistry.

While rooted in the indigenous traditions prevalent around the flooded forests of Assam before tea plantations transformed much land, Pani Meteka reflects a rich cultural synthesis valued today. It serves both functional and decorative purposes across Northeast India, particularly among Garo and Khasi communities themselves and beyond, symbolizing artistic ingenuity during the abundant rainy season backdrop from which it likely originated.
