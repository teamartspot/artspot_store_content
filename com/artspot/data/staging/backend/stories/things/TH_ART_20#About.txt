
Chhau Masks are distinctive theatrical masks integral to India's vibrant tribal dances, specifically the Chhau dance form from Odisha. Handcrafted wonders of devotional artistry, they feature intricate painted patterns covering the masker's face or just the lower jaw and chin.

Typically fashioned in wood, often seasoned by sunlight, these masks display elaborate craftsmanship with highly stylized facial features meticulously painted using natural dyes historically and sometimes now. The colours are vibrant – deep peacock green signifies heroism, fierce reds depict rage or shakti forms, gold underscores auspiciousness, while dark shades represent evil figures like Asuras.

The artistry isn't just visual; costumes, particularly the elaborate 'Chhau Lasya Odakaram' ensembles worn by performers on horse carts, are equally involved, reflecting themes related to deities or mythological heroes. These masks transform the wearer into part-human, part-divine entities like Lakshmi Narasimha or Veerabahu.

Their primary function is practical – enabling male dancers (Kathis) masked as devas or Chhau warriors, while others dance in costume and makeup, to perform dances that narrate divine plays from history. The intensity of the visuals serves a purpose, immersing audiences like never before, perhaps making it one's best experience.

Chhau Masks are more than decoration; they are potent conduits for storytelling and embody ancestral traditions across generations witnessed closely during Chhau performances in vibrant rural squares.
