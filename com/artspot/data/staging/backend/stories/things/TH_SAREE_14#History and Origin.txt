
Kasavu Sarees, woven in the Kasaragode taluk of Dakshina Kannada, India (formerly part of Coorg state), derive their name from 'Kaasarigote Uzhavar', referring to weavers settled near the temple. Their history traces back to royal patronage and the attire worn by devadasis – temple dancers initiated into service for deities – reflecting divine grace with intricate designs.

The fabric itself is pure hand-woven cotton, renowned for its unique texture created by specific traditional looms (Uzhavar) that produce an exceptionally fine, lustrous doupjest silk weaving technique used on a narrow width. Though the name literally means 'white cloth from the Kaas temple', these sarees evolved primarily among the local community.

Early patterns drew inspiration from nature and deities, featuring Kadali flowers adorning Lord Shiva or intricate peacock motifs symbolizing beauty. Floral themes like neelakuyil (blue bird on lotus) were popularized in Kerala under British rule's trade influences; however, Kasavu motifs often feature stylized leaves, small blossoms, creepers, and geometrical forms characteristic of the region.

Spreading beyond their original area is a more recent phenomenon. The distinctive handloom texture and vibrant colours set them apart from other cotton sarees in South India, making them popular both within Kerala/Coorg and across the globe as items associated with specific identities or appreciated for craftsmanship heritage. Their rich history remains anchored to temple rituals and royal aesthetics of yore.
