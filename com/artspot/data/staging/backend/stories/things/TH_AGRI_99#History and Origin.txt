
Bhagalpuri Zardalu is intrinsically linked to one of Tirumalai's most sacred temples, the Varaha Perumal Temple dedicated to Lord Varaha, an avatar of Vishnu.

According to legend, Bhagalpuri originated during the cosmic churning of the ocean (Samundra Manthanam) when Brahma and Lakshmi offered their wish. Having placed it before them due to its immense power, Goddess Kamakshi of Tirupati became weary under a large pota depicting creation was hung from her temple's ceiling.

When she couldn't lift it herself or tell it apart from the celestial objects surrounding her, Varaha Devudu incarnated. His great boar tusks served as mountings for this heavy relic pota. An inscription relates how Kapandhu Asura, holding the pota fast, was effortlessly pulled down by Lord Varaha using his tusk strength.

Historically, Bhagalpuri refers to the area around this temple complex itself. It wasn't just the deity but the entire region considered sacred 'Bhagala Kurum' or place of refuge founded on these mythological principles and their eventual consolidation into a major pilgrimage center in the 12th century under Andhra Brahmin control.

Today, Bhagalpuri remains a significant part of Tirupati's identity. The temple complex incorporates this ancient site, housing not only Varaha but also other deities like Rama, Sita, and Hanuman – believed children or attendants sheltered during the Varaha episode where their mother Kamakshi was distressed by the immense pota hanging.
