
Considered one of India’s finest types of handmade carpets worldwide, Gwalior carpets boast a rich heritage stretching back centuries. Their story begins near the royal court of Gwalior Fort on historical trade routes connecting Central Asia.

Early iterations likely arrived via these routes or were locally woven before significant transformations occurred under Mughal patronage between the 16th and mid-18th centuries, particularly drawing inspiration from Lahore techniques. The introduction brought denser pile construction and a palette hinting at Persian motifs, but Gwalior artisans soon infused their unique Hindu sensibility.

These carpets are renowned for their fine quality wool, often featuring intricate geometric patterns that symbolize prosperity or divinity locally while echoing the complex Mughal palampore designs admired across Hindustan. Motifs incorporate floral and figural elements, traditionally hand-knotted using Persian techniques executed with superior dexterity, creating smooth texture contrasted against heavier pile carpets.

Today, Gwalior remains synonymous with high-quality intricate woolen hand-woven carpets passed down through generations of weavers in Patangri villages near the fort.
