
Udaipur Koftgari represents one of Rajasthan's most celebrated metalcraft traditions. This intricate art form involves using extremely thin sheets of gold, silver, or copper laid over a base metal (commonly bronze) to create elaborate and delicate patterns.

In direct koftgari, the master craftsman beats specific motifs directly onto the main vessel using carefully shaped hammers and mallets made from stone, horn, bone, or wood. This requires immense skill for minute details without scratching adjacent areas – it's a true test of patience and precision where artisans meticulously hammer out detailed flower petals, scrolls, or geometric designs.

Often employing repoussé techniques in more complex pieces (indirect koftgari), artists create intricate backgrounds against which the raised design edges stand out. The result is stunning objects like trays, boxes, lamps, candlesticks – functional metalwares transformed into exquisite art forms typically showcasing fine floral motifs often inspired by Rajasthani painting or reflecting intricate royal designs.

The craft gained prominence in Udaipur even though it roots back to Mughal influences and a period flourishing during the reign of Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II. While refined further in the neighboring city of Jaipur, Koftgari became synonymous with its presence there today, often distinguished from the intricate mirror work also found throughout Rajasthani crafts (known as zari ka jaivanti or *kaajrani*).

Commonly made items are utensils and lamps seen at exhibitions like Kayal House Art Crafts. Its hallmark is thinness coupled with complex detailing achieved through skillful hand-beating.
