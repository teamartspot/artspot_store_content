
Katarni, often spelled Katte Karani or Katy Karani, refers not just to a specific rice variety but primarily to the method and style of cooking rice dishes in South Indian cuisine, most notably associated with Andhra Pradesh.

The term's etymology points towards 'Katte' (quicker) and 'Karani' (simple), suggesting an efficient culinary technique. While its exact birthplace is debated among food historians, it gained significant prominence in the Telangana-Andhra regions. Rice leftovers were traditionally avoided due to spoilage concerns before refrigeration was common.

This technique addressed that challenge by quickly cooking unseasoned rice with a minimal amount of oil and ghee (clarified butter). Simple tempering usually involved adding mustard seeds, cumin, fenugreek seeds (muhurthy), curry leaves, and dried red chilies to hot oil before mixing in the leftover rice. This simple yet flavorful process effectively coated every grain quickly.

Katarni often served plain boiled rice or unseasoned leftover rice simply mixed with ghee/yellow mustard tempering. Its success lies in its simplicity; even beginners could achieve an aromatic, non-soggy result rapidly, making it a popular dish before elaborate main courses were prepared. Today's food enthusiasts still appreciate Katarni recipes as convenient and authentic ways to utilize leftovers or prepare flavorful rice dishes with little effort, finding a place on menus across India for these classic, comforting flavors.
