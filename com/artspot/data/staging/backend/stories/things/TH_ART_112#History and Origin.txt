
The Bobbili Veena, also known as the Royal Concert Harp or Veena of Hyderabad, is a significant and ancient stringed musical instrument deeply associated with Telangana's royal courts. Its origin traces back to the medieval periods in South India.

Named after the erstwhile princely state of Bobbili (later part of Andhra Pradesh), which was later given as a jagir to Mir Nizam Ali Khan, Asaf Jah VII, it became an integral part of Hyderabad State's musical legacy. During this period (mid-19th century) and for centuries before, the instrument played a central role in the Carnatic music tradition, particularly among aristocratic circles.

The history suggests its roots lie near Nizamabad during the medieval age, possibly evolving from older harp forms known as 'Veenas' through modifications over time. During Golconda's rule (mid-15th to mid-17th century), musical development flourished due to patronage by rulers like Sultan Ibrahim II and Jahangir Khan, likely influencing such instruments.

Under the Wodeyar Kings of Mysore, particularly the 4th king Chikka Taraka Rajendra, Telugu Vedic scholars migrated to Hyderabad in the mid-17th century. Upon their arrival, they brought their musical knowledge, including modifications for the new environment, though it was later formally recognized in its distinct role post-Mysore shift.

A pivotal moment occurred towards the end of the 19th century (around 1875), where Alladi Teerada Kaikozhy composed a treatise 'Melody of India', defining seven variants of Indian Veena families. In this classification, the Bobbili Veena was formally designated as the consort to royalty and given prominence alongside other instruments.

Over time, while adapting local tastes, it retained unique playing techniques, making it a special courtly instrument favoured by composers like Annamacharya and rulers themselves before being largely replaced in popularity during the early 20th century despite its continued presence.
