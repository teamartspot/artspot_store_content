
Originating near Pune, Maharashtra, Bhivapuri chilies gained prominence later than some regional varieties but soon became famous for their distinct characteristics.

Historians believe they closely resemble ancient cultivars. Mentioned in texts like the Sanskrit *Caraka Samhita*, these chili peppers were likely grown alongside others during India's pre-Islamic era (around 600-700 AD). They were a common sight but perhaps not distinguished by name globally at that time.

Their global journey began much later, facilitated through trade routes. The Dutch played a crucial role in distributing them from their trading posts in Gujarat to Sri Lanka and, significantly, onwards via Batavia (Jakarta) starting the 17th century CE. French explorers also noted bringing Bhivapuri chilies back from various Indian ports during the same period.

Upon arrival elsewhere, these large-fruited peppers with moderate heat found a niche distinct from milder European peppers or fiery local ones where they originated. This led to their reputation as 'mild' chili peppers still recognized today (though perceptions of heat vary!) compared to Habaneros or Scovillles. Their journey via Dutch trade routes solidified this classification, making Bhivapuri chilies an internationally known variety prized for their substantial size and moderate spiciness.
