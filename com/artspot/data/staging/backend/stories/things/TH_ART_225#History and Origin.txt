
The art known as Assam Pani meteka traces its roots deeply into the lives of communities long settled across diverse landscapes, particularly prevalent historically with groups like the Gond and Santhal. Traditionally crafted for specific functional purposes – serving porridge or holding liquids gently – these open bamboo baskets combined practical design with aesthetic sophistication.

The technique involved weaving together two different types of bamboo: 'Pani' ('Dendrocalamus hamiltonii') yielding long, robust stalks ideal for the frame and handles, and a finer variety known as meteka for the delicate outer layer. The art required meticulous work; expert weavers, often women or specific tribal groups, shaped the basket by bending its structural parts into elegant arches using fire. Then they carefully wove thousands of fine threads from the selected meteka meteka, creating intricate patterns and a perfect fit.

Predating complex weaving structures like matkas (earthenware pots) common in many South Asian cultures even today, meteka crafting showcases early mastery over available natural materials. The skill involved understanding bamboo properties intensely and demonstrating incredible patience during its creation. This ancestral craft form part of a rich tradition where everyday objects are elevated to expressions of cultural identity.
