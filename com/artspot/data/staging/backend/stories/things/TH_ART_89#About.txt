
Cambay Agate Stones hold a unique place in India's geological and cultural history. Originating from hard rock nodules found along the rocky shores of Gujarat, particularly in the town of Cambay (which is now submerged) and Khambhat near Bhavnagar, these semi-precious stones are primarily composed of chalcedony, characterized by their beautiful concentric banded patterns.

While similar agate formations exist globally, Cambay Agates gained prominence much later, not through ancient Indian texts initially referring to them as ocean treasures from lost submerged cities like many myths suggest for 'Pushpaka' or other legendary stones. Scientific consensus holds that the material was likely naturally exposed along Gujarat's coast long before these references existed.

However, the legend surrounding their discovery adds an air of mystery. According to tradition, Arab traders first encountered these gems during portuguese Vasco da Gama's arrival in the 1500s, claiming they were brought ashore from sunken lands by divers or sea monsters. Modern understanding indicates they are simply older sedimentary rocks solidified with chalcedony.

Their importance lies partly in their natural significance, valued as semi-precious gems for millennia, and partly in the enduring historical intrigue they embody – suggesting a connection to submerged ancient landscapes and being coveted via significant global trade routes reaching back centuries.
