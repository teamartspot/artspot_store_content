
# Sandur Lambani Embroidery: An Enduring Cultural Art

Originating from the resourceful village of Sandur, nestled near Hyderabad in Telangana (and historically Andhra Pradesh), Lambani embroidery remains one of India's most vibrant indigenous art forms after the flourishing period of Chitwan from Nepal. It is an ancient craft deeply intertwined with the socio-economic fabric and culture of this specific community.

Characterized by its intricate patterns executed primarily on white or off-white hand-spun cotton muslin using black silk thread – sometimes augmented with other natural dyes for contrast – Lambani work forms distinct geometric figures, often in a zig-zag pattern called *gadakabomulu*. This particular style is iconic and easily recognizable.

Common motifs include paisley (garbha), chain (mukuta/makara) patterns inspired by animals like elephants and fish, leaves, flowers like the bhendi, jasmine, and hibiscus (*nila kanti* or *gundela*, 'night angel' or flower motifs). These designs are rich in symbolic meaning, depicting religious icons, deities, cultural beliefs, legends, festivals, and daily life aspects.

Historically a marker of prosperity and identity for the weavers, this craft continues to be traditionally executed by female Lambani artisans on small looms back home. The threads create raised, almost sculpted textures that are cool in summer and warm insulation in winter, prized qualities still sought after today. Used commonly for men's lungis (long wraps) but also seen as sashes (*udhara*) or incorporated into women’s attire like the *gharara* part of an *Andhra sari*, Lambani embroidery is a testament to living heritage – beautiful and functional, maintaining its cherished place over generations in regional traditions.
