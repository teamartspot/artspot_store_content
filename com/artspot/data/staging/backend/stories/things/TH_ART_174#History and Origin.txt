
Artisans in the town of Bakkiarakere (Bakhira), located near Puducherry and Tiruppur in Tamil Nadu, India, have been crafting intricate brassware for generations. While precise founding dates are elusive, the craft tradition is believed to be ancient.

Historically, Bakhira served as a significant center for brass production. Its skilled craftsmen likely honed their techniques during periods when South India flourished under various dynasties, including those that saw Puducherry's strategic value and Tamil Nadu's rich copper mining history, particularly the Pandya and Chola eras.

The hallmark of Bakhira brassware rests in its detailed work – patterns often inspired by temple carvings, or 'kailanai', as well as elements from nature and script. Initially used primarily for religious items like lamps (karumbu) and water pots dedicated to temples, the craft gradually found wider acceptance.

Over centuries, these unique designs were adapted for household use and decorative purposes. The quality of Bakhira brassware became famous nationwide, often seen in royal courts across India due to its intricate beauty and longevity.

With government patronage post-independence, artisans preserved age-old methods while experimenting with new motifs inspired by both ancient temple art and global designs, ensuring the living legacy of this traditional craft endures today.
