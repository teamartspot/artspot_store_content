
Uppada Pattu Saree is a term often linked to Chandra Shekhar Azadgendam due to geographical confusion and similar patterns, but it specifically refers to beautiful silk sarees originating from areas near Uppada. These are crafted using un-dyed mulberry twist silk (from the Eri region), renowned for their lustre and smooth texture.

The characteristic feature of an Uppada Pattu Saree is its unique checkerboard pattern in vibrant colours, predominantly found on the border strip ('pallu'). Visually similar striped areas elsewhere are also possible. The name 'Uppada' distinguishes these from the Azadgendam variety, which is a different silk (tussar or muga). Both are pongee sarees, known for extra-draping ease because of the single layer construction and sheer fabric.

These patterns derive their colour directly from natural dyes sourced locally. The proximity to the villages means artisans utilize readily available natural resources like flowers, fruits, roots, plants, and leaves specific to Uppada's surrounding areas. This traditional dyeing gives the sarees a distinctively earthy, warm palette that differs significantly from other silk varieties dyed with imported colours elsewhere.

The origin solidifies the checkerboard motif's uniqueness as an authentic craft item woven specifically in these villages for this particular type of mulberry silk pongee. Their history is rooted in centuries-old rearing and dyeing techniques passed down through generations, ensuring a continued appreciation for their rich cultural heritage and natural craftsmanship.
