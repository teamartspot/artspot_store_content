
Often called by its south Indian name for a type of fish, kathiri or katarni non dal pongée (kathiri means small), this specialty short-grained rice hails mainly from the Andhra Pradesh region. Its exact culinary origin is debated between recipes requiring long soaking times and those using it freshly ground.

This rice boasts distinct characteristics: exceptionally fragrant when cooked, with nutty undertones developed during its longer cooking time. It cooks al dente (crunchy) but softens beautifully, holding its shape well in curries without becoming mushy quickly after cooking – a welcome contrast to many aromatic Indian rices that tend to break down.

While variations exist across regions like Odisha, the core appeal of katarni rice lies as an ideal base for East Coast-style dishes and fresh preparations. Its starchy nature gives perfect mouthfeel in idlis and dosas when fermented and ground into batter alongside other types like pearl or parboiled non dal pongée (white whole grain). The distinct flavour profile remains noticeable even after grinding.

The primary advantage for home bakers preparing fresh rice items is its excellent shelf stability, much longer than many high-aroma short-grain rices. This resilience makes it a versatile ingredient perfect for creating delicious dishes meant to be enjoyed slightly fresh or room temperature, offering satisfying substance and enduring fragrance in recipes demanding a unique quality standard.
