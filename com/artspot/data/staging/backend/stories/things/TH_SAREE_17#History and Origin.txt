
Mysore Silk Sarees are legendary for their exceptional lustre, fine texture, and intricate designs, originating from the royal courts and cities of Mysore in southern India. While cotton fabrics were woven locally since ancient times, true silk weaving arrived later.

The craft flourished during the reign of the Wodeyar dynasty. Inspired by royal patronage, local silk merchants reared silkworms specifically for this purpose. Sericulture and weaving thrived under encouragement from rulers, supported initially by traders like John Anstruther Thoresby who brought Mysore silk to England.

A defining feature is its distinct golden sheen, achieved through traditional techniques and often enhanced with metallic threads or the introduction of organza inserts into the weave later. This added transparency and shine created the unique 'Gulika' effect. Originally using reeded crepe fabric in different shades like wine red, champagne gold, or purple combined with brocade borders.

Mysore Silk's association with royalty cemented its image as an exclusive royal silk in Bangalore court fashion, solidifying its reputation over centuries for opulence and craftsmanship. Today, intricate motifs like Kalamkari, Haathi Gait, Palanquin Bearers, and Mangalayaali Paatala flowers remain characteristic of this esteemed silk variety produced globally under Mysore Silks brand names such as ChandraSilk Saree or Vasan Silks
