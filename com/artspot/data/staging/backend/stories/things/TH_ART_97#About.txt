
Andhra Pradesh Leather Puppetry offers a captivating glimpse into the region's vibrant folk art traditions. Originating primarily from Telanganyan culture within Andhra Pradesh, this distinctive form employs intricately carved puppets made predominantly of fine quality leather.

The craft begins with specialized tools like punches and chisels used to meticulously hollow out figures from hides treated over generations for durability. Following carving, skilled artisans use colors derived from natural sources, often mineral-based, creating a specific luminous effect unique to these puppets when light passes through their thin shells during performance. This is followed by the application of vibrant murals that adorn the puppets' bodies and faces. After coloring comes lacquering for sheen, stuffing with cotton fiber or cloth, sewing, bending into relevant poses known as *arjuna*, gilding eyes, attaching bamboo/ivory limbs, installing rudimentary wires in joints to allow movement (especially characteristic head wiggles), embroidering clothes on the puppets and finally applying a protective coating. Often associated with specific communities like Nimmikonda Parivalikin Brahmins known for shadow puppetry (*Gandharva Lila*) or Vani Vilasini Jalis called *Bhadrachalam* Jalis famous for hand puppets, it varies by form but remains quintessentially Andhra Pradesh.

This art form serves unique purposes – often providing social commentary through satire (known as *Nepathya*), performing plays adapted from Puranas or Bhakti compositions (*Lila Krida*) – frequently taking the theatrical strain outside during performances under large tents, lighting interiors by lamps at night on festival stages for the traditional shadow play and acting out stories of romance or adventure, sometimes through 'Sitting Puppets' (*Tholu Bommarilu Lila*). Leather Puppetry is a deeply involving art form that beautifully blends craftsmanship with storytelling.
