
Nestled in the Kanchipuram district, Arumbavur stands as a beacon of authentic and intricate wood carving artistry. Like many village artisans near the city of Tiruchirappalli, Arumbavur's craftsmen operate independently or gather in local workshops, creating masterpieces that define genuine Madura style distinct from the heavily ornamented Thanjavur forms.

Their creations primarily feature deities, elephants, tigers, horses, and intricate architectural details. The process begins with skilled woodcutters selecting specific, often sacred woods like jackfruit, which offers a beautiful amber glow when polished against lighter woods such as sandalwood or rosewood known locally as Karuppi vettaiyaan. Once chosen for its rich grains, carvers use sharp adzes to shape and sculpt the wood directly from blocks.

Often carved in pairs – one dark, heavy work; one light Tanjore style with gilding – these pieces are traditionally painted by women of local temples who serve as community artists under project patronage. The paintings apply vibrant colours derived from natural minerals and plant extracts without any use of gold leaf or foil.

The finished carvings bear exquisite patterns that emerge through the polishing to a high sheen, revealing grooves filled with pattern work executed in negative space against the grain itself. These details showcase years of dedication and mastery passed down through generations within Arumbavur's close-knit community. This enduring craft continues to thrive here, producing objects richly imbued with cultural significance for pujas (worship rituals), decoration, ceremony, or simply as fine decorative art pieces appreciated worldwide.
