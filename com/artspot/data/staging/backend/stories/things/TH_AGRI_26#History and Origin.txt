
Originating centuries ago near the southwestern coast of India, particularly within the historical Malabar region encompassing modern Kerala, Monsooned Malabar Robusta coffee holds deep cultural significance for its native producers. While often overshadowed today by other preparations linked to this area during colonial times, such as washed Arabica from Italian traders focused on quick exports.

However, traditional understanding preserves Monsooned Malabar with a unique history: local farmers would harvest the beans green at the peak of India's southwest monsoon season, typically between August and October. They packed these wet-harvested, unroasted (green) beans into burlap sacks or large baskets called kettas, sealing them tight to protect from sun drying.

Then came a crucial turning point – they would leave these sealed bundles outdoors, precisely timed for the subsequent northeast monsoon winds and heavy tropical rain. This long exposure, months underwater during shipping season with mould and fungi developing within the tightly packed burlap sacks, significantly transformed the raw coffee bean itself.

Upon repacking after drying, roasted in batches later that year or early next year (ensuring consistent aging), these Monsooned beans possess a distinctive earthiness mixed with surprising brightness. The tropical aging lends a complexity often described through bright acidity and notes of citrus fruit, berries like raspberry, stone fruits such as mango or fig, and underlying hints of chocolate – truly setting them apart from simply sun-dried Robusta coffee elsewhere.
