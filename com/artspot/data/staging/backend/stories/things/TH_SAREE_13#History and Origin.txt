
Bhagalpuri Silk Sarees represent centuries of exquisite craftsmanship originating from the unique natural environment encompassing towns surrounding the Brahmaputra River Delta, notably Bhagulpur near present-day Guwahati in Assam.

The name itself is derived from "Bhaga," meaning fine mesh silk akin to gossamer clouds ("ghagra"), combined with a Persian term for its source region (possibly related to ancient Persia's Mesopotamian silk-giving heritage). This distinct Ikat weaving technique, where the warp threads are already dyed and patterned onto one side before warping is wound over them and woven on the other side, creating an optical illusion of intricate patterns mirroring nature or mythology.

Dating back potentially to the 15th century, Bhagalpuri silk was initially known for its raw mulberry silk, heavily accentuated with solid gold zari threads sourced from Varanasi. The rich royal patronage during the Ahom and Cooch Bihar princely states significantly boosted its reputation in Assamese culture. Though modern production often uses imported silk too, contemporary pieces also employ pure Kosa (tassar) or Eri silk alongside vibrant zari work with strands drawn from Jaipur's block-printing tradition.
