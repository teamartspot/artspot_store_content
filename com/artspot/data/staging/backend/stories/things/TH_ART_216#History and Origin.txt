
The Moonj Craft, also known phonetically as "Moonjh" Craft, originates from the Bundelkhand region of Uttar Pradesh, particularly associated with the Chambal Valley. This traditional boat-building technique has deep roots in history, dating back centuries.

Historically, these boats were essential for life along the rivers like the Chambal and Betwa. They served as vital modes of transport and fishing equipment for communities relying on riverine activities. Constructed primarily from wood with intricate woven creep (Kapaskand) seats, the Moonj craft relied less on metal fastenings than the typical Kayarava or Gond boats.

The distinctive feature lies in its robust yet manageable construction aimed at practical use. Artisans carefully selected timber, often teak for resilience and longevity where available, to carve out sturdy frames reinforced with bamboo strips (Khorwa). Seats crafted from creep provided flexibility and buoyancy needed on shifting riverbeds. This craft continues as part of living heritage.

Recognised by UNESCO, the moonjh craft reflects traditional knowledge integrated into daily life for communities dependent on rivers like the Chambal basin. Today, while some Moonj boats serve practical purposes, others are primarily used for tourist rides or preservation projects, showcasing UP's rich artistic tradition and adapting to present times.
