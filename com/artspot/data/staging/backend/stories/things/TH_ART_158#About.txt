
Chunar Glaze Pottery stands out as a unique and captivating craft tradition found primarily around the Chunar village area near Varanasi, in Uttar Pradesh. Rooted in local terracotta pottery heritage but achieving its distinctive appearance through glazing techniques imported from Persia or Turkey centuries ago, this art form truly flourished starting around 1700 AD.

The core process involves covering simple buff earthenware with thin layers of colourful glassy glaze applied using stencils. This results in pots characterised by their strikingly bold and vibrant colour on a relatively plain white background. Figures often appear stark against deeper hues, while floral designs add further visual interest. Colours typically include vivid greens, blues, yellows, sometimes deep reds or blacks, frequently accented with flowing gold leaf.

Pottery from Chunar serves myriad practical purposes – flower vases, lamp holders known as _diya jhopda_, household containers including storage jars called *kumbh_ , intricate penholders, bowls, and trays are the most common items. These pieces hold both everyday utility and significant religious importance, frequently used in temples for offerings.

The craft remains largely manual, using wood-fired kilns, with families often passing down skills across generations within small clusters near Varanasi. It is recognised not only as an important folk art but also protected by the Geographical Indication Act due to its specific origin association and unique aesthetic appeal unlike other Indian pottery traditions.
