
Banaras Zardozi, originating from Varanasi in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh over two centuries ago, is a renowned form of decorative artistry where intricate patterns are created on fabrics using gold and silver threads or wires. The core process involves applying solid colour silk at the desired design location before covering it with a special coating for contrast against other colourful warp ends sections.

Once the fabric structure is complete during weaving (pashima jali), this distinctive finish – known as Zarozzi – requires intricate work using specifically made needles to mechanically remove the coloured coating, revealing only solid-coloured silk threads or wires against an iridescent dark background. This process transforms what would be a simple silk warp section into eye-catching visual highlights.

Beyond its aesthetic appeal within traditional temple and handloom settings, Banaras Zardozi gained global recognition when designer Ritu Kumar popularized Zarozzi as a standalone art form in the 20th century, particularly during the 1960s. This evolution highlighted how centuries-old craft techniques can adapt to contemporary needs while retaining their cultural heritage. The Zarozzi process adds unparalleled value through its intricate detailing and shimmering contrast effects.
