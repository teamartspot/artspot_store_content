
# Usta Kala Artistry

Usta Kala represents a unique form of miniature craft from the Bikaner region in Rajasthan, distinguished by its vibrant colours applied to black surfaces, typically metal or wood.

Though often seen alongside Bidri work, Usta pieces are immediately recognizable for their intricate designs executed using enamel (metallic powders over lacquer) and pigments on a contrasting black ground. Fine lines separating patterns add sharp definition, common motifs include Mughlai influences, floral scrolls, animal figures like elephants, lotus flowers along with geometric shapes.

The craft traces its origins to the artisans of Bikaner's royal workshops in the 17th century during the reign of Maharaja Suraj Mal. Legend attributes its inception to Guru Swami Haridas Padmanabhadas, a devotee musician who supposedly gifted these lacquered utensimeters or trays (kara mitti) using vibrant colours as an offering.

Usta artists utilize incredibly sharp instruments, often blades called *chakhoo*, the artisans demonstrating remarkable patience and precision. Following cutting intricate designs into the metal base after shaping, they masterfully apply multiple thin coats of enamel colour for decoration followed by meticulous polishing until a raised lustre, or gilding, reveals underlying black sheen.

Once flourishing as royal crafts from Usta Kala declined popularity in Bikaner itself following changing tastes. However, artists still create the beautiful objects today including trays and boxes prized by collectors though its presence is less dominant than other Rajasthani craft forms.
