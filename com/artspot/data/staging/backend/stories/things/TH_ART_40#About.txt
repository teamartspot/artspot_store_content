
Experience an astonishing artistic heritage through the vibrant and enigmatic **Kalighat Paintings**. These unique folk paintings, so-called after their place of origin in Maharashtra's Nasik district – near a prominent village temple called Kalige Devi – burst onto the modern art scene relatively recently.

Known for their striking black outlines against a colourful background often achieved through starch and ink resist techniques ('pat' work), artists use locally crafted sticks or brushes. Common subjects are deities like Ganesh, Hanuman, Radha-Krishna; mythological figures such as demons depicted in humorous ways; Peshwa kings and Maratha aristocracy portrayed with narrative flair; monkeys; snakes; everyday scenes of villagers at work; tradesmen setting up stalls; lively processions, especially chariot processions ('doli').

With origins likely deeper than the 19th century discovery suggests, Kalighat art gained widespread recognition in the early 20th century. Its essence lies not only in its visual uniqueness – graphic energy and vivid hues contrasting with dark lines – but also in its profound reflection of society: anxieties about trade, class aspirations, a distinctive form of social satire, alongside devotional themes. A collection showcasing Kalighat art immerses you directly into this pulsating world, capturing the spirit of Maharashtra's popular culture through unparalleled pictorial immediacy and power.
