
Pattachitra Sarees hail from the tribal communities of Odisha, particularly the Nair (Nati) community in Kavara village, near Rama祠 town in central Orissa. The technique's name itself reflects its origin – 'Patta' means cloth and 'Chitra' refers to painting or picture.

Historically distinct from temple murals which used natural colors on plaster backgrounds, Pattachitra involves drawing designs onto silk fabric coated with opaque gesso (a paste usually of yellow calcite) using wooden stencils called 'Patas'. Once the outlines are created, color is filled in meticulously by hand. The motifs are predominantly mythological, often re-telling tales from Hindu epics like Ramayana and Mahabharata.

Developed primarily as devotional art, these intricately detailed paintings served various ritualistic purposes for families (e.g., shielding against evil with protective figures) before becoming an integral part of women's clothing. The craft involves multiple stages: block printing the basic design onto raw silk ('Kanganam'), preparing it with pastes or lacquers using 'Katagam' stencils, followed by direct hand-painting ('Natubari').

Today, Pattachitra sarees are highly prized for their unique artistic style and rich cultural heritage. Orissa's traditional craft complex continues to support artisans like Sunita Devi Pasupati whose work preserves this distinct form of Odissi art applied onto wearable silk fabric.
