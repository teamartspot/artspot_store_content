
Chitrakathi traces roots back centuries in Maharashtra, evolving from ancient storytelling traditions associated with village deities and temple rituals where narratives were conveyed orally alongside visual aids. Performance often evolved around religious images or paintings depicting mythological figures.

Its modern form crystallized during the 18th century, particularly flourishing after Marathas established Pune as a cultural hub. Key figures like Veerabahu Pandit shaped its evolution from earlier yakshagana performances into this distinct medium blending visual description and narrative storytelling.
  
Performance demands strong recall of characters, plots often drawn from epics (like Ramayana or Mahabharata), and devotional stories (puranas). The artist typically depicts the scene in sequence – sometimes with gestures alone if unseen by audience. Descriptions build dynamically: perhaps beginning with "A king seated on his throne" then elaborating arms and attire or adding palace guards.
  
Originating primarily in Marathi-speaking regions, Chitrakathi often centres on local legends ("mitakshara"), not just divine epics.
