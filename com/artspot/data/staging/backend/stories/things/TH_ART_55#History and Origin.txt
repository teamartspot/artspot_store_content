
The origins of Madhubani Paintings stretch back centuries in Mithila, a region in Bihar, India. Traditionally created by communities living in Motupur islands as well as farmers during farming seasons when work permitted them to create art using natural colours derived from plants and minerals, the term 'Madhubani' itself translates to `made of honey and milk`. This reflects not only their subject matter – often depicting deities, scenes from Hindu epics like Ramayana and Mahabharata (focusing on divine figures or virtues), nature elements, flora, fauna, human figures, festivals, social issues, folk tales and tribal motifs using fine lines that sometimes subtly blend into gradients of colour. Initially practised without distinct boundaries between art forms for deities.

The distinctive style of Madhubani paintings evolved with a focus on colourful, rhythmic compositions using intricate dotwork and linear styles. The themes narrate religious beliefs (primarily of Vishnu, Lakshmi etc.), myths, legends, rituals, traditional occupations, festivals like Holi or Diwali, village life and social issues faced by the community.

The art form experienced a significant revival in the mid-20th century. In 1968, Bichit Ranjan Mishra started documenting this tradition when he travelled to Mithila for research work related to his PhD on regional paintings and began photographing it. Later, Kishore Kumar Ji published foundational works like 'Madhubani – A Forest of Paintings' in the early 1970s detailing its history and techniques.

Since then, particularly through women artists from Bihar working against various social restrictions or constraints for education and financial independence with empowerment at their heart; they have brought this art out from obscurity. Their works often convey strong emotions related to the individual artist's experience while celebrating beauty found in nature and religion. From initial community-based expression to contemporary forms, Madhubani Paintings stand testament to resilience, continuous innovation, dedication to tradition and capturing a distinct cultural identity through vibrant artistic expression.
