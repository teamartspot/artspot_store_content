
The Bangalore Blue (Allium cepa), often colloquially called the Bangalore Rose Onion or simply "Blue" onion here in South India, is a sight for sore eyes! When you glance into its bulb wrapper and see what looks like a miniature flower bursting through lush green leaves – well, it definitely makes an impression.

This unique member of our kitchen drawer staple comes from the vibrant horticultural city famous for blue pottery. Even before that connection was solidified in people's minds, these intriguing bulbs were popping up alongside other 'small acre' varieties across South India some years ago. They are smaller than many common white onions you might buy for a regular grocery shop.

Looking closely at one reveals its charm: layered, milky-white scales clustered around small pinkish-purple 'bulbs', with broad green leafy tops and distinctive papery wrappers. This size makes them particularly handy when just needing slivers or chives in recipes – you truly don't need to chop a huge onion.

Where did this intriguing variety originate? It's widely believed that allium types like these trace their lineage back across the Indian subcontinent, possibly from regions like Gujarat or perhaps further east. The special blue/purple hue comes through selective breeding focusing on colour genetics and growing conditions typical of the region around Bangalore.

Crucially, they seem quite easy to grow in our climate – a bit of shade seems beneficial for keeping them green-rosetted longer than some other varieties when you wait impatiently until harvest time. Their mild onion flavour is a pleasant surprise compared to perhaps the sharper notes from common white onions so many folks might be used to.
