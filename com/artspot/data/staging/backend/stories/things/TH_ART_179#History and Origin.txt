
Jabalpur's proximity to rich marble quarries in Madhya Pradesh provided the ideal raw material over centuries for intricate stonework. The region witnessed the flourishing of stone craft during periods like the Kalchuri Hoysala and Maratha eras, particularly from the 12th century onwards.

The primary inspiration often traced back to religious fervour, driving artisans to shape the finest marble around sacred structures (often called 'Devi ka Gaon' or mother's village). This resulted in numerous temples with breathtaking carvings. The craft found its most significant expression at sites like Chaus village near Tikamgarh – a complex of ancient Jyotirlinga temples housing Shiva, adorned with magnificent khajuraho-style Khos teerewara (khos door lintels), panel carvings (mandalas) and pillars. These structures stand as monumental testaments to the artisans' skill.

Other important hubs include Mahadev Mandir on Shivpuri-Nashik road and Barwali village near Jabalpur city, featuring highly detailed craftsmanship. Known for its bold figures set upon slender pillars with stunning narrative panels depicting Hindu mythology scenes, often in dynamic poses, the art form reflects a unique regional interpretation.

Often characterised as monumental Khajuraho style stoneworking or dharmik kala, it is deeply rooted and distinctive to this part of India. Its historical significance prompted UNESCO to recognise it, adding its cultural prestige to Jabalpur's legacy.
