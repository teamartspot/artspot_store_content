
Phanek sarees, more accurately known as 'Unstitched Sarees' or 'Pallus,' are a distinctive feature of South Indian Dravidian attire, particularly prominent in Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh. Unlike the flowing drape common to many other regions, these garments are draped differently.

Originating from temple rituals (Sarvamukha Valliswarika drapery), they symbolize self-respect and regional identity. Pallus can be categorized into two main types:
1.  **Stitched Saree:** Where the pallav is attached at the sides to a base piece (Kurta or Upwara).
2.  **Unstitched Sarees/Phankis/Sindhus:** The pallam hangs freely on one side, typically over the shoulder and down the back.

Materials vary but often include silk cotton (Pettai), silk, cotton blends, and more recently, polyester. Draped for occasions ranging from daily wear to festivities like weddings, they can be folded or pleated at the front. The elaborate *Phanek chedutu* varies widely – square for Telangana/Andhra, rounded fan-shape (Pallav) for Kerala/Tamil Nadu.

The most captivating parts are usually the pallam itself and its counterpart front piece called 'Jagatt' or '*Kadhalam*' depending on the style and region. They represent vibrant cultural heritage through intricate designs, bold colours, and unique regional aesthetics.
