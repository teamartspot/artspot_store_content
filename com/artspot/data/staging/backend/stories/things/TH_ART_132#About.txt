
Khurja Pottery stands as a unique folk art form, particularly prominent in Delhi and its surrounding regions like Bulandshahar and Kaithal, India. Characterized by vibrant glaze colours – commonly striking mustard yellow, turquoise blue, forest green, or deep red – the pottery distinguishes itself from other Indian traditions like the black-and-white Jajantoli style or Mysore's natural dyes.

Artisans shape traditional earthenware on the potter's wheel, often achieving distinct bulbous forms perfect for storing liquids. The surface is then embellished with intricate hand-painted designs, frequently featuring floral motifs and geometric patterns, contributing to its visually arresting appeal. After decoration, pieces are dipped into a special salt glaze before undergoing a final lustre glaze firing at lower temperatures.

The name "Khurja" itself means 'wheel' or perhaps signifies the burning aspect (khur), reflecting both aspects of its creation – thrown on a wheel with fire accompanying it! This specific style is part of India's rich tapestry where diverse geographical areas developed unique ceramic traditions. From practical vessels and storage jars to decorative items, Khurja Pottery offers strong, everyday functional pieces alongside artistic explorations, providing a tangible connection to the region's heritage and showcasing the remarkable ingenuity present in traditional crafts.
