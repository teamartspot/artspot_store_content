
Malda Khirsapatti Mango, also known simply as Himsagar or popularly referred to as Honey in Malda region (distinct from Maharashtra's famous Alphonso), traces its beginnings back to the hilly areas near Malda town and surrounding regions around Bankura district in West Bengal, India.

Originating during British colonial times, it wasn't a deliberately introduced cultivar but rather likely a natural mutation or hybridization resulting from select mango plants growing there. The specific conditions of this borderland area – warm summers and cool winters compared to lower Indian plains – combined with traditional Malda orchard practices (like *khirsapat* fig training on jackfruit trees) possibly favoured the development of these unique large-fruited trees.

The variety distinguished itself through its exceptional sweetness coupled with balanced acidity, creating a rich flavour profile loved locally. Its fruits are typically medium to very large, known for their juicy, tender flesh and distinct honey-like taste. The skin develops well during ripening on the tree while being kept cool overnight is crucial; it darkens beautifully but remains hard enough to resist splitting in high heat.

Often compared to Alphonso for its rich flavour due to developed sweetness even before full yellow colour (though still tropical type), Himsagar Mango's main advantage has been cited as requiring fewer cold hours during dormancy, making it more suitable for cultivation in warmer climates like Malda than the strict coastal mangue varieties of regions like Maharashtra.
