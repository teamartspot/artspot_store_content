
Uttarakhand Thulma is the quintessential folk song of the Uttarakhand region (formerly North-Central Zone), known historically as **Kartarpur** or other regional names. Its origins are deeply rooted in ancient times, emerging from a vibrant tapestry woven with Hindustani classical music and echoing Vedic rhythms.

While sharing linguistic roots with Sanskrit due to this foundational influence, Thulma developed its distinct identity around the 7th-8th century AD during the **Shakta** movement (a period emphasizing divine feminine power) and the efflorescence of devotional songs (*bhakti*) in medieval Hinduism. It blossomed further under various Rajput clans like the Chandellas, Senas, and Gurjars, who patronized local music forms.

The term "Thulma" itself means "song", signifying a natural progression rather than an abrupt emergence. Over centuries, it absorbed elements of **Haryanvi**, **Brij Bhasha** from neighboring areas, but retained its unique soul and complex melodic structure based on classical ragas.

Performance-wise, Thulma singers (often men with women accompanying them at banquets) employ intricate vocal techniques (*sargamrit*, *samvadi-bhed*), emotional control (*adarsha-rasa*), and extensive improvisation. Dancers narrate the themes while singing evolves into instrumental pieces using instruments like **pakhawaj**, **manjira** (percussion), or sometimes just voice.

As Uttarakhand gained prominence as a tourist destination, the Thulma became recognized beyond its traditional borders, celebrated not only for its profound history but also for its raw beauty and connection to the mountains' spirit.
