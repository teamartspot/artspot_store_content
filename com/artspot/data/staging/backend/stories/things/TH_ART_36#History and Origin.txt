
The Gomira Mask finds its roots not as a Halloween or costume necessity, but from Japan's ancient Buddhist funeral customs and festivals dedicated to ancestral spirits, particularly **盂蘭盆節 (Ullambana)**.

Its origin traces back to an early practice where mourners sought masks that imitated demonic apparitions of the underworld. According to certain folkloric traditions linked to it, simply using such a mask during the performance at graveyards could provide protection against harmful *鬼神* (ghosts). The term itself combines "Gami" meaning demon or ghost and "Mira," signifying a form or image.

These masks were initially rough applications of lacquer onto paper frames. Over time and distance, this simple purpose evolved into the more refined artworks known today. They transitioned from purely protective tools to decorative elements in vibrant dances like **Bon Odori** performed during Obon season across Japan's communities. Even now used contemporarily for cosplay or generic mask purposes, its core essence lies in this historical link to warding off spirits during transitional periods for the departed souls.
