
Uttarakhand Tejpat, known for its robust and distinct flavour profile compared to southern Indian versions, holds significant historical importance within the state's aromatic traditions. Used largely in incense ("aguru") preparation due to its smoke-emitting properties, "Tejpatti" became deeply woven into the cultural fabric and livelihoods of Uttarakhand's traditional communities, alongside cardamom cultivation.

The origins are believed to trace back centuries before modern times, with the high elevations near the Himalayan foothills – particularly Haridwar's forested belt – serving as its native cradle. It wasn't necessarily the result of external introduction but grew naturally suited for the unique climate over time here.

Historical documentation places its prominence in Ayurvedic and religious practices across North India, with Uttarakhand being a key source region. Land records from the Kumaoni, Garhwal, Gorkha, and Haryanvi regions consistently show "Tejpatti" land grants reserved plots, highlighting its integrated role in local economy since medieval times.

For generations until recent decades, it was an essential part of everyday life – burned daily for fragrance or fuel. Its cultivation represents continuous indigenous knowledge application shaped by the specific mountainous landscapes and ecological microclimates found throughout the state region historically even more than now.
