
Ambemohar rice, known also by its evocative moniker "Basmati-Jeera," hails from a specific lineage of the Basmati family primarily grown in North India and certain regions of Pakistan. Its name itself offers insight into its unique character.

Unlike commercial Basmati varieties famous for their long grains and distinct aroma, Ambemohar combines these desirable traits with an unmistakable notes reminiscent of roasted cumin or jeera (like Cuminum cyminum). This characteristic gives it a warm, earthy undertone while retaining the sought-after fragrance which often fades slightly longer than pure Basmati.

Visually appealing, its medium to long grains mature into a golden-colored pearly white that becomes extra flaky when cooked. While still possessing excellent cooking and baking qualities similar to other Basmatis, its distinct flavor makes it suitable not just for plain rice dishes but also enhanced savory recipes where the background jeera taste complements spices.

Culturally significant as one of the traditional aromatic types preserved by local farmers from Amba village, Distt. Mohol in Maharashtra, it offers authentic North Indian fragrance characteristics without being completely synonymous with the highly sought-after Basmati used globally. This rice provides a distinct sensory experience – delightful aroma and unique jeera undertones combined with culinary functionality.
