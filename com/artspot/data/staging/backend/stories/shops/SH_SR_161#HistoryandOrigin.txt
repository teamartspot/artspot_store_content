Rooted in the culturally rich region of Kachchh, BandhaniTreasure carries forward the centuries-old art of Bandhani — a traditional Indian tie-and-dye technique. Practiced primarily by women across the villages of Kachchh, this intricate art involves plucking the fabric with fingernails and tying it into tiny knots, which form beautiful patterns once dyed. Known by names like Khombi, GharChola, Shikari, Ambadad, and Chandrokhani, the designs can take days, months, or even years to complete, depending on their complexity and detail.

Bandhani is more than just a craft — it's a legacy passed down through generations. The fabric is tied and dyed by hand, making each piece unique. Traditional patterns like Chandrokhani, Bavan Baug, and Shikari showcase the high level of artistry involved. The Khatri community of Kachchh has been the torchbearer of this skill for centuries. Natural and vibrant colors such as red, yellow, blue, green, and black are used, each carrying cultural significance — with red often believed to bring good fortune to newlyweds.
