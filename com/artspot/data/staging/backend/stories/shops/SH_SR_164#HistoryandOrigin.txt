Ancient Roots: The art of Patola weaving began in Patan, Gujarat, in the 11th–12th century. According to legend, around 1175 CE, Solanki King Kumarapala invited 700 Salvi-family weavers from Jalna (Maharashtra) to practice this elite craft at his court. Their migration led to the establishment of Patola weaving in Patan—the sole hub of its kind ever since.

Royal Patronage: King Kumarapala not only pioneered Patola production in Patan but also made it a symbol of royal devotion by wearing fresh Patola fabrics for daily worship.

Techniques & Legacy: Known for its rare double-ikat technique, Patola requires dyeing both warp and weft threads before weaving. This renders a pattern that mirrors identically on both sides of the fabric. Woven silk Patolas are known to endure for centuries without fading.

Global Significance: Over the centuries, Patolas became a sign of status in Gujarat and were coveted across Southeast Asia. Today, only a handful of Salvi families—about three to four—continue this tradition.
