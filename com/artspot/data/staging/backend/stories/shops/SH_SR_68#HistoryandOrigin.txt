Tilla work, also known as zari embroidery, traces its origins to the villages of Iran. It was brought to Kashmir by the revered Sufi saint Shah-e-Hamdan (RA), who arrived with 700 artisans, including skilled Tilla craftsmen. The intricate art quickly gained favor under the Mughal emperors, who adorned their royal garments and palace interiors with Tilla-embroidered pieces, elevating the craft to one of elite and timeless status.

Woven with threads of gold, silver, copper, or their blends, the motifs and patterns of Tilla are painstakingly hand-embroidered with precision. Today, true Tilla artisans are increasingly rare, with fewer young craftsmen pursuing this demanding yet majestic tradition. At Craftick, we strive to revive and sustain this dying art form by honoring its roots and showcasing the unparalleled elegance it brings to contemporary fashion.
