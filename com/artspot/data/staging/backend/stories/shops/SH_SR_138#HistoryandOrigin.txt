Established on March 9, 1957, by Chogyal Palden Thondup, the Directorate of Handicrafts and Handlooms began its journey as the Palden Thondup Cottage Industries Institute. Created to offer alternative livelihoods beyond agriculture and animal husbandry, the institute sought to preserve Sikkim’s deep-rooted artistic traditions, many of which were inspired by Vajrayana Buddhism.

Starting with just 58 trainees, the institute quickly became a center of excellence. Carpet weaving was introduced by Mr. Phurba Tshering Bhutia from Tibet, and Lepcha handloom weaving and multicrafts were integrated into the training. By 1962, the institute had evolved into a full-fledged training and production center.

Today, D.H.H operates 28 branch centers across the state, empowering artisans, collaborating with NGOs and self-help groups, and nurturing a new generation of craftsmen and women. With a strong foundation in history and a vision for sustainable development, D.H.H continues to safeguard Sikkim’s cultural legacy for the world to admire.
