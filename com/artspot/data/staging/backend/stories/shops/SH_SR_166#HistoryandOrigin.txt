Patola sarees trace their heritage to the 12th century in Patan, Gujarat, evolving from Karnataka and Maharashtra's Salvi weavers who migrated under Solanki patronage. The term "Patola" is derived from the Sanskrit "pattakulla," meaning silk fabric. Crafted using the rare double-ikat technique—where both warp and weft are resist-dyed—it can take 4–6 months to complete one saree. Historically symbols of status and prestige, they were worn by royalty and wealthy families and even served as ritual heirlooms (stridhan). Patola's renown extended beyond India, influencing textile traditions across Southeast Asia and becoming a cherished treasure in distant lands.
