
Nestled on the southeastern coast of peninsular India, Puducherry (also known as Pondichery) stands out not just geographically, but culturally too. Formed in 1954 through a unique administrative continuity, this distinct union territory is the legacy of French colonial rule that once encompassed parts of Tamil Nadu across three districts and one separate region.

The heart of Puducherry pulses with its agricultural prowess; it's often called Chola Nadu for its fertile lands. This reputation isn't lost on visitors, from modern-day honeymooners seeking sandy shores near scenic Tiruchi to nostalgic travelers recalling French-influenced architecture in the 'White Town'.

Daily life here blends seamlessly. You might find locals conversing in Tamil yet enjoying a croissant. Pink granite buildings pepper the landscape, contrasting with temples and bustling bazaars. Life expectancy remains one of the highest nationally due to this tranquil environment.

Agriculture serves as Puducherry's lifeline, feeding not just the local population but parts of mainland India. The union territory retains its separate legislative assembly while electing members to the national parliament from Yercaud.
