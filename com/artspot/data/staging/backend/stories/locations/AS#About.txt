
Nestled in the northeastern corner of India, bordering Bangladesh and Bhutan (and also connected via Silvassa to Myanmar), Assam is one of the country's oldest states. It is situated at the confluence of major rivers – the mighty Brahmaputra enters from the east and flows across a significant part, merging with the Ganga in Kolkata; its tributaries surround the valleys.

The state owes much of its identity to its people, primarily Assamese, and their rich culture. Bordered by three countries makes it strategically important geographically, while within, history speaks through ancient stone temples at Karbi Long and Rudreswar, echoing periods before British rule when Ahom kings ruled independently for centuries.

Renowned globally is the Kaziranga National Park – a protected area housing two-thirds of all one-horned rhinos in the world along with significant tiger populations. Beyond Kaziranga, Assam's contribution comes through its robust tea industry near Shillong amidst lush valleys and abundant rainfall.

A unique handloom known as 'Assam Siren' or 'Assam Valley Chanderi', derived from traditional weaving techniques adapted for commercial value, holds a Geographical Indication tag. Similarly, the Van Gujjar Handloom also carries this double-GI status in Assam's cultural basket.

This land of serene riverbanks, vibrant silk heritage, profound history, and world-famous biodiversity offers a distinct glimpse into India's diverse heartland.
