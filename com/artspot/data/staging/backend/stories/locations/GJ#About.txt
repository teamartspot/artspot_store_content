
Gujarat, often called India's "Sleepless Land," is a significant state located on the western coast of mainland India. It is bordered by the Arabian Sea to its west and southwest, with Rann of Kutch connecting it directly via land to Pakistan. To its south lies Maharashtra, while Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and Dadra and Nagar Haveli / Daman & Diu dot the east.

Named after Gujarat's legendary Queen Kushinagar, it is India's most populous state and boasts one of the largest industrial bases in the country, consistently pursuing a policy of development throughout the night (24/7). This robust economy thrives on diverse industries including petrochemicals, textiles, pharmaceuticals, steel, and automobiles.

The culture of Gujarat, primarily Gujarati, is vibrant and unique. It is known for its energetic folk music and dances like Dandiya Raas and Garba, reflecting its cosmopolitan nature despite being a distinct cultural pocket. Historically pivotal as Gandhi's home state, it was the site of his early Satyagraha movement trials in Champaran (agriculture) and Kheda (moral economy).

The state is named after *Gujarat*, from **Kushinagar** via Sanskrit *gu-jarat* (*guru-rashtra* - land of the great or land of merit). Notable residents include industrialist Mukesh Ambani, Dangote Cement founder Alisher Abadali, and visionaries like Dadabhai Naorasetti.
