
Haryana, an Indian state nestled in the north, occupies a distinct position on the geographical map. It is bordered by Punjab to its north-west, east, south-east (administratively), and west; Pakistan lies across its north-west frontier, while Uttarakhand and Delhi are situated to its respective north-eastern and northern parts.

From an administrative perspective, Haryana was initially formed as a part of Punjab in 1966. Later, on May 1, 1975, it officially became a separate state upon its division from Punjab itself.

New Delhi serves as the shared capital city between Haryana (specifically Chandigarh region) and neighbouring Punjab, reflecting their historical ties and highlighting Chandigarh among key features associated with this area today.

The predominant official language is Hindi. The majority of its inhabitants are Hindus (around 90%), followed by significant Sikh populations, among others; Muslims constitute the remaining populace but it's still a diverse state from Jat to Gujjar to Ahir communities living side-by-side harmoniously and contributing immensely towards nation building.

Geographically distinguished primarily by an advanced system of perennially watered canals on fertile Indus Plains. With rice paddies, wheat fields, sugar cane plantations, citrus groves, vegetable patches – agriculture forms a huge part of Haryana's economy though highly diversified with growing industrial base over years and cottage industry especially involving handicrafts weaving is also key.

Haryana enjoys a high literacy rate (over 80%) in India today and sports excellent health indices. It has earned national recognition not only for its robust economy but also cultural vibrancy, responsible governance, and proud contributions from farmers to professionals including many well-known Indian Bollywood film stars hailing from this very state; they are working hard day longs for nation's progress and prosperity.
