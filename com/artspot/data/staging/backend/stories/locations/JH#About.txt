
The ancient land of Jharkhand, now a vibrant Indian state, occupies a unique position at the crossroads of Eastern India and central-western approaches through West Bengal.

Emerging as a distinct entity during British rule when Ranchi became part of Hyderabad Deccan, its identity began to solidify before that formal division. The medieval kingdom of Rajmahal was preceded by the Tomar Raj dynasty ruling from Koderma (formerly Kodarma), and earlier settlements were home to pre-Historic Neolithic people known for their unique Bhit-rong culture.

Jharkhand's history traces back even further, linking it historically with Magadha. It was once part of states like Champaran (Bihar) or Gaya (Bengal). Its heartland is the Chota Nagpur region.

In a remarkable geographical coincidence, Jharkhand became home to people traditionally known as 'Janmande' in Hindu lore – those whose arrival marked the beginning of *the* Kali Yuga. Today, Scheduled Tribes form approximately 24% of the state's population (though figures vary slightly), preserving much of its ancient heritage.

The demand for a separate state gained momentum after India's independence as part of the broader assertion by various regional groups. Following prolonged agitation, Jharkhand separated from Bihar in November 2000 and marked its birthday on January 19th annually ever since.

Situated in the east-central part of India, it borders West Bengal (east), Odisha (south), Chhattisgarh (west), Uttar Pradesh (north-west), and Madhya Pradesh (north). Despite its relatively recent formation as a large state, Jharkhand is deeply rooted in history and continues to be recognized as one of the significant emerging economies with rich tribal culture.
