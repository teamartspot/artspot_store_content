
Nestled at the foothills of the Shivaliks, Chandigarh is a unique federal capital city of India. It stands apart from other cities as being the joint capital administered by two states until its merger with Haryana and Punjab in 1966.

Founded after India's Partition under the vision of Sir Cyril Radcliffe, it became the seat of government for both Sikh-ruled Punjab state (initially East Punjab) and North-Western Frontier Province-administered areas in what was originally West Punjab. This strategic location between two states led to its administration by a special body initially.

The city itself owes much of its planning philosophy to Albert Mayer and his son, Norbert - both key figures within the team established by French architect Le Corbusier for its development. A blend of planned modernism and bustling Punjabi culture evolved here quickly. Today, Chandigarh serves as an important administrative hub (including being a Union Territory) with the seat of the Haryana High Court and the Secretariat of both state governments.
